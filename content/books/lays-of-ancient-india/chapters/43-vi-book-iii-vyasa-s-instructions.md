<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-185"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="185" data-folio="147" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n184_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 147">[p. 147]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK III.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>VYÂSA’S INSTRUCTIONS.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Beaming</span> with a gentle lustre<br>
Soft as rays of autumn night,<br>
Graced with auburn locks that clustered<br>
Like a cloud with lightning bright!<br>
Bearing grace in all its fulness<br>
On his more than mortal form,<br>
Filling every living creature<br>
With affection pure and warm !<br>
Speaking by his gentle features<br>
Peaceful thoughts that filled his heart,<br>
By his soft and trustful glances<br>
Seeking confidence t’ impart !<br>
Mighty source of all the Vedas,<br>
Source from whom all virtues flow,<br>
Him the King Yudhishthir questioned,<br>
Curious his great end to know. (4)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Unattained by mighty virtue,<br>
Saint ! thy visit is to me<br>
Like a holy life’s fruition,<br>
Like the rain from cloudless sky !<br>
Rites performed bear fruit to-day,<br>
Brâhmans’ blessings bring forth weal,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-186"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="186" data-folio="148" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n185_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 148">[p. 148]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Mighty Saint! since by thy visit<br>
Honoured in this world I feel!<br>
By its grace it conquers evils,<br>
By its glory spreads our fame,<br>
From thy kindness as from Brahmâ’s,<br>
Untold bliss and blessings come!<br>
Not the moon with gentle radiance<br>
Cheers my sad and weary eye,—<br>
Now my heart forgets its sadness,<br>
Beats with joy, for thou art nigh!<br>
Thy desire I may not question,<br>
Peaceful souls have no desires!<br>
But a wish to hear thy utterance<br>
With a boldness me inspires!” (9)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">In graceful words thus spoke the monarch;<br>
Vyâsa, by his kindness led,<br>
Anxious for the monarch’s glory,<br>
Thus unto Yudhishthir said: (10)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“He who strives for fame and glory<br>
Bears for all an equal love,<br>
He who strives for peace and virtue<br>
Should with love impartial move.<br>
Yet my partial heart, O monarch,<br>
Is by virtues drawn to thee;<br>
Virtues have a power attractive<br>
Even on holy saints and high.<br>
Are ye not of race imperial,<br>
Worthier far than Suyodhan?</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-187"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="187" data-folio="149" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n186_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 149">[p. 149]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Has the old king lost his reason<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-001" href="#sec-43-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Thus to wrench from you your own?<br>
And will fortune help a monarch<br>
Who on Karna places trust?<br>
Friendship with unrighteous mortals<br>
Is but fame and honour lost!<br>
When your foes left paths of virtue,<br>
You in virtue took your rest;<br>
And midst changes, still unchanging,<br>
Shewed forgiveness, ever blest!<br>
Vainly did they seek to shame thee,<br>
Man of ever changeless love!<br>
'Tis thy wealth of worth and virtue<br>
In true light their actions prove!<br>
But mark my words! by valour only<br>
You can win in battle's hour;<br>
And in might is strong the foeman,—<br>
Therefore seek increase in power. (17)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Jamadagni's son,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-002" href="#sec-43-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> who conquered<br>
Thrice seven times the kings of earth,<br>
Great though he, the chieftain trembles<br>
At great Bhîshma's<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-003" href="#sec-43-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">‡</a></sup> mightier worth!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-188"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="188" data-folio="150" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n187_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 150">[p. 150]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Death is powerless, death is conquered<br>
By that chief's resistless power ;<br>
Trembles earth when mighty Bhîshma<br>
Wields his bow in battle's hour !<br>
Doughty Drona !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-004" href="#sec-43-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> in the battle,<br>
Speeding arrows in his ire,<br>
Like a world-consuming furnace<br>
With its quivering tongues of fire !<br>
Fiery Karna <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-005" href="#sec-43-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> learnt his lessons<br>
From great Jamadagni's son.<br>
Death himself, in Karna's presence<br>
Owns a terror, strange, unknown !<br>
These are chiefs, believe me, monarch,<br>
Whom in battle thou shalt face !<br>
Hence let Arjun with due penance<br>
Seek celestial arms and grace.<br>
Let him seek that gift of prowess<br>
Gods themselves by penance crave,—<br>
This, O monarch, is my mission,—<br>
Win the gift that speeds the brave.” (23)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Then the great and mighty Arjun<br>
Stept forth reverent and slow,<br>
Bowing to Yudhishthir's mandate,<br>
Like a student meek and low.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-189"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="189" data-folio="151" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n188_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 151">[p. 151]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And the gift, the fiery <em>mantra</em>,<br>
Issuing from the holy saint,<br>
As the sunlight falls on lotus,<br>
So unto great Arjun went !<br>
And the <em>mantra's</em> sacred radiance,<br>
Which the hero proudly wore,<br>
Quickly oped his eye of reason,<br>
Taught him secrets of deep lore !<br>
And his form betokened glory,<br>
And his heart was fixed and strong,<br>
Urging penance pure and holy,<br>
Vyâsa spoke to Arjun young. (27)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Strengthened by this <em>mantra</em>, Arjun !<br>
Yielding thy own place to none,<br>
Girt in arms perform thy penance,<br>
Unto fasts, ablutions prone.<br>
Let this Yaksha lead thee, youth !<br>
To the lofty golden hill,—<br>
There you do your sacred penance,<br>
Please great Indra, do his will.” (29)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus speaking to the mighty chief<br>
The saint evanished from his view,<br>
Obedient to his sacred word<br>
Appeared the Yaksha, faithful, true.<br>
The Yaksha bowed and felt a love<br>
For Arjun, gentle in his speech ;<br>
The pure are quick in confidence,<br>
And friendship is not far to reach. (31)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-190"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="190" data-folio="152" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n189_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 152">[p. 152]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">As darkness fills Sumeru's bowers<br>
When slow the god of day departs,<br>
The parting from the mighty Arjun<br>
Filled with grief his brothers' hearts.<br>
Dispelled awhile by sense of duty,—<br>
Albeit so strong was brothers' love,—<br>
The sorrow of the parting brothers,<br>
Though keen, did not oppressive prove.<br>
And hope and trust in Arjun's might,<br>
And bitter hatred of the foe,<br>
And confidence in Arjun's power<br>
Dispelled the brothers' common woe.<br>
As darkness leaves the hours of day<br>
And seeks the stillness of the night,<br>
Thus sorrow left the mighty chiefs,<br>
And fell on Krishnâ<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-006" href="#sec-43-fn-006" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-006" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> in its might.<br>
As flakes of snow the lotus fills<br>
Spontaneous tear-drops filled her eye,<br>
Nor could she weep, for tear-drops shed<br>
Might be an inauspicious sign!<br>
One look she gave, 'twas dear to soul,—<br>
And Arjun caught the parting grace,—<br>
His treasure and memento dear<br>
Through pathless woods and weary days.<br>
By grief her tender heart was wrung,<br>
Like summer rills by tuskers soiled;<br>
Her voice was choked with tears restrained,<br>
She spoke in accents sweet and wild. (38)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-191"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="191" data-folio="153" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n190_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 153">[p. 153]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Restorer of our ancient fame,<br>
Now trailed in mire by foemen’s art,<br>
Until thy mighty penance ends,<br>
Our absence should not pain thy heart.<br>
In fame’s pursuit, in pleasure’s quest,<br>
In deeds which glorious records fill,<br>
Unfailing Fortune leans to him<br>
Who labours with a mighty will !<br>
To rule the world was Kshatriya made,<br>
His wealth is conquering power in strife !<br>
Disgrace, alas ! has quenched that power,—<br>
To true-born warriors dear as life !<br>
Disgrace, which kings in distant lands<br>
Have doubting heard, heads bent in shame !<br>
Which stains our former stainless worth,<br>
Our world embracing mighty fame !<br>
Disgrace, which wipes our former deeds,<br>
And hides in gloom our glory’s blaze !<br>
Which wipes our future prospects fair,<br>
As evening wipes the sun’s last rays !<br>
Disgrace by hated foemen dealt,—<br>
This bitter thought is cruel, smart !<br>
Disgrace which in thy absence, chief !<br>
Will freshen in this sorrowing heart. (44)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ How changed thou art ! like wounded tuskers,<br>
Prowess lost, and glory faded,<br>
Deprived of power by foemen’s wile,<br>
Like day by clouds of autumn shaded !<br>
Thy arms unused have lost their glow,<br>
Nor deck thee as they did of yore ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-192"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="192" data-folio="154" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n191_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 154">[p. 154]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thy form how changed, like summer lakes,<br>
Now faded by the loss of power !<br>
Duhsâsan dragged me by this hair !<br>
Untied they are, their lord is fate !<br>
On thy great fame they cast reproach,<br>
Oh ! art thou Dhananjay <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-43-fn-007" href="#sec-43-fn-007" data-fn-target="sec-43-fn-007" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> the great ?<br>
A Kshatriya he who can protect—<br>
A bow is useful in the war—<br>
But vain their import if they fail<br>
In virtues by their name they bear.<br>
Thy warlike virtues, all but dead,<br>
Ingloriously thy rise await,<br>
And seem to share our common grief,<br>
And imitate our common plight ! (49)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ But rash thy foemen thee insult,<br>
As tuskers touch a lion’s mane !<br>
Duty for thy worth elects thee,<br>
As the day elects the sun !<br>
A hero’s deeds all deeds excel,<br>
And fill the glorious rolls of fame ;<br>
A hero’s name holds foremost place<br>
When men their mighty chieftains name !<br>
Then be a hero ! do thy deeds,—<br>
And if perchance within thy heart,<br>
A thought of us awakes a pang,<br>
May Indra every grief avert !<br>
In sacred spots, from dangers free,<br>
May all thy time in safety pass ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-193"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="193" data-folio="155" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n192_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 155">[p. 155]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Beware the wicked, impure foes<br>
Who turn against the pure, alas!<br>
Thy duty calls thee! Arjun, go!<br>
Perform the saint's behest in peace;<br>
And all our dearest hopes fulfilling,<br>
Come thou to our dear embrace!" (54)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus spoke Drupad's noble daughter,<br>
Deepening his resentment high;<br>
And he crimsoned in his anger<br>
Like the sun in northern sky!<br>
Accoutred in his mighty weapons,<br>
Fancying all his foes before,<br>
As spells assume a power terrific,<br>
He a form terrific wore!<br>
The bow before which foemen trembled,<br>
Famed by many a mighty deed,<br>
The quivers never seen by foemen,<br>
And the long and shining blade,<br>
And his gem-bespangled armour,<br>
Like the star-decked sky,—he wore!<br>
Scars by Indra's darts inflicted,<br>
Hid by glory evermore!<br>
Guided by the faithful Yaksha<br>
He unto the mountains hied,<br>
Filled the hearts of saints with sorrow,<br>
As with tears the chief they eyed. (59)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">A heavenly music filled the realms on high,<br>
And fragrant blossoms gently fell from sky,<br>
And the sea with breakers ever restless<br>
Clasped the earth to whisper words of sweetness!
