<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-223"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="223" data-folio="185" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n222_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 185">[p. 185]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK VIII.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>THE ADVENT OF INDRA.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Arjun</span>, mighty in his strength,<br>
All impure passions overcame ;<br>
And to his holy hermitage,<br>
Rejoiced at heart, great Indra came.<br>
He came disguised,—as come the gods,—<br>
Even like an ancient anchorite,<br>
Wearied by a tiresome journey,<br>
Weak in limbs and weak in sight.<br>
And his crimson plaited locks<br>
Upon his white hair mingled fell,<br>
As the evening’s crimson radiance<br>
Mingles with the moonbeams pale.<br>
All wrinkled with the mark of age,<br>
His eyes by fleecy eyebrows shaded,<br>
Were like the lotus of the lake,<br>
Its petals by a snow-fall faded.<br>
But his limbs, though very slender,<br>
Seemed instinct with strength and life ;<br>
Like an aged man and hoary,<br>
Nourished by a careful wife !<br>
In feeble form concealed, great Indra<br>
Shone with more than mortal power ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-224"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="224" data-folio="186" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n223_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 186">[p. 186]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The radiant sun is hidden faintly<br>
By a light and passing shower!<br>
And a grace divine he wore,<br>
Albeit so ancient and so hoary,<br>
And o’er the hills and woodlands spread<br>
The lustre of his shaded glory!<br>
Prithâ’s son <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-48-fn-001" href="#sec-48-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-48-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> received the guest,<br>
And held him in a fond embrace;<br>
True friends an unknown joy inspire,<br>
Albeit unknown to us their face!<br>
And Indra, pleased at heart, received<br>
Obeisance which to guests is paid,<br>
And rested on a mat of grass,<br>
In gentle accents thus he said. (9)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Well hast thou in early age<br>
In these rites thy choice hast made;<br>
Aged mortals like myself<br>
Oft by worldly things are led.<br>
And thy penance, noble youth,<br>
Is endued with virtues fair,—<br>
Handsome forms we often meet,<br>
Handsome virtues,—they are rare!<br>
Transient as the autumn clouds,—<br>
Pride and pomp of humankind;<br>
Pleasures please us for a day,<br>
Bitter sorrows leave behind!<br>
Mortals’ days are full of evils,<br>
Death cuts short our life’s brief span;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-225"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="225" data-folio="187" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n224_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 187">[p. 187]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Therefore in this fleeting world<br>
Virtue seeks the holy man.<br>
Wise and nobly thou hast chosen<br>
Holy rites to virtue dear,<br>
But, belying thy great penance,<br>
Thy attire fills me with fear ! (14)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Like a warrior, on thy mien<br>
Wherefore wear’st this armour bright ?<br>
Skins and barks of forest trees<br>
Suit the holy anchorite.<br>
Void of earthly vain desires<br>
In the virtuous path you go ;<br>
Wherefore, then, O noble youth !<br>
Quivers and this mighty bow ?<br>
And by mortals ever feared,—<br>
Death’s right hand,—this mighty blade !<br>
Doth it in thy holy rites<br>
Unto peace thy feelings lead ?<br>
Sure against some mortal foe<br>
War and triumph you must seek ;<br>
Weapons are designed for war,<br>
In forgiveness dwell the meek !<br>
He who sighs for warlike fame<br>
Soils these rites and penance holy,<br>
As the spring’s pellucid water<br>
Soils the fool who acts in folly.<br>
Cherish not the lust of fame,<br>
For it leads to sinful deeds,<br>
Casts a stain on stainless rites,<br>
And from peace our heart misleads. (20)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-226"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="226" data-folio="188" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n225_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 188">[p. 188]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ He who strives with lust of glory,<br>
Wins awhile a vile success,—<br>
But as rivers end in oceans,—<br>
So he ends in dire distress !<br>
Wealth is won by evil measures,<br>
An l to evils leads each morrow,—<br>
Wealth is but a name for trouble,<br>
Leads to sufferings and to sorrow !<br>
Impure pleasures, joys of earth,<br>
Kill our peace and steadfast faith,<br>
And like snakes with poisoned<br>
fangs<br>
Lead to danger and to death !<br>
Fickle fortune, ever fleeting,<br>
Loves not with a lasting love ;<br>
Only fools her favours seek,<br>
Strange the motives, mortals move !<br>
Fortune ! if she spurned the fickle,<br>
’Twere no stain upon her fame,—<br>
But the worthless and the worthy<br>
To the fickle maid are same !<br>
Love ! it is an emptier sound,<br>
Ending in delusion, pain !<br>
Sad bereavement, death and loss<br>
Rend the heart of luckless men.<br>
When we meet the loved and true<br>
Solitude as peopled seems ;<br>
Penury hath charms to please,<br>
Sorrow is like happy dream !<br>
When we lose the loved and true,<br>
Pleasures mock us and delude ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-227"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="227" data-folio="189" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n226_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 189">[p. 189]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Life is like a poisoned dart,<br>
Company is solitude ! (28)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Thus each fleeting earthly object<br>
Ends in sorrow, ends in grief ;—<br>
Charity alone endureth,<br>
Unto others bring relief !<br>
And our life is vain and fleeting,<br>
Fortune’s fickle favours fly ;<br>
Righteousness alone endureth,<br>
Turn not from the righteous way !<br>
Stain not, youth, these holy rites,<br>
Do them not with lust of war ;<br>
Seek salvation’s stainless bliss,—<br>
Than war’s glory mightier far !<br>
Conquer lust and vain desires,—<br>
Born with mortals at their birth,—<br>
Conquest of thyself, good youth,<br>
Is the conquest of the earth !<br>
Weak are they and narrow-hearted,<br>
Earthly power who seek to wield ;<br>
Slaves of passions, slaves of impulse,<br>
Even like cattle in the field !<br>
Joys that pleased thee yesterday,<br>
In thy memory dwell alone !<br>
Pleasures are but fleeting dreams,<br>
Be not thou to pleasures prone !<br>
Ever wished, but still deceiving,<br>
Cherished but to cause us woe,<br>
Never present,—never leaving,—<br>
Earthly joy—our greatest foe !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-228"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="228" data-folio="190" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n227_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 190">[p. 190]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">In this holy mountain range,<br>
Where the Gangâ wanders far,<br>
Work thy own salvation, youth!<br>
Leave, O leave this lust of war.” (36)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus the mighty god, disguised,<br>
Spoke to Arjun,—paused awhile;<br>
And in humble words but strong,<br>
Arjun answered with a smile. (37)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Full of weight and wisdom, father,<br>
Are the peaceful words I hear;<br>
Full of import and suggestion<br>
Is thy utterance, deep yet clear.<br>
Like an independent Sâstra<br>
In its reasons strong in sooth,<br>
Like the ancient holy Vedas<br>
Mighty in its force and truth!<br>
Inviolate in its ample force,<br>
Like the vast inviolate seas;<br>
Gentle in its wealth of sense,<br>
Like a hermit’s soul of peace!<br>
Who could utter thoughts so noble<br>
Save a great and noble heart,—<br>
Words emphatic, peaceful, clear,<br>
Void of quibble, void of art!<br>
But perchance to thee unknown<br>
The object of my holy rites,<br>
Hence in accents sweet and peaceful<br>
Speakest thou of anchorites.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-229"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="229" data-folio="191" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n228_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 191">[p. 191]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">E’en the god of speech will err<br>
When he speaks of things unknown ;<br>
E’en the noblest efforts fail<br>
Against inviolate rules when done ! (43)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Father ! thy advice is holy,<br>
But, alas, it suits not me,<br>
As the starry sky of midnight<br>
Doth not suit the light of day !<br>
I am of the Kshatriya race,<br>
Pandu’s son, of Prithâ born ;<br>
Serve the mandates of my elder,<br>
By his foes of glory shorn.<br>
I these holy rites perform<br>
Obedient to great Vyâsa’s word ;<br>
Toiling still in ceaseless penance<br>
For great Indra, mighty lord ! (46)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Oh ! woeful are decrees of fate,<br>
And mortals’ bliss is often crossed !<br>
His kingdom, brothers, and his wife<br>
Our elder staked on dice and lost !<br>
And now in evenings long and drear,<br>
My brothers grieving at their fate,<br>
With proud Draupadî great of heart,<br>
The term of my devotions wait.<br>
They tore the garments from our backs,<br>
And shamed us in the palace hall ;<br>
They pierced our hearts,—our foemen vile,—<br>
With bitter taunts upon our fall.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-230"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="230" data-folio="192" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n229_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 192">[p. 192]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And in the presence of the chiefs<br>
They dragged Draupadî chaste and true !<br>
Death sealed in her disgrace a vow<br>
Of vengeance on our impious foe !<br>
Behind Duhsâsan,—impious chief !—<br>
Appeared Draupadî,—great in mind,<br>
Even as the shadow of a tall tree<br>
Is at sunset cast behind.<br>
‘ In vain I look upon my lords,<br>
Untrue to duty and to me,’—<br>
Such bitter thoughts her bosom rent,<br>
And checked the tear-drop in her eye !<br>
Our virtuous elder bore unmoved<br>
The insult dire, the blow unkind !<br>
Oh ! what is conquest over foemen<br>
To such conquest of one’s mind ?<br>
For noble hearts retain their peace<br>
Albeit by grief and passions riven ;<br>
The ocean steps not o’er its bounds<br>
Albeit by mighty tempests driven !<br>
’Twas friendship with our impious cousins<br>
Which unto this shame hath led,—<br>
Bitter is his untimely end<br>
Neath falling banks who sits for shade.<br>
Men who fear nor sin nor shame,<br>
Right and wrong who do not see,—<br>
Who can compass their designs ?<br>
Who can fathom fate’s decree ? (56)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Disgraced, insulted by our foe,<br>
My heart, my heart had ceased to beat,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-231"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="231" data-folio="193" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n230_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 193">[p. 193]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">If in this strong and vengeful arm<br>
I hoped not retribution great!<br>
Disgraced, insulted by our foes,—<br>
Low, low, as cattle on the plain,<br>
We shame to see each other’s face,<br>
Nor show our face to other men!<br>
Humbled by the loss of glory,<br>
Humbled by disgrace, alas!<br>
Mortals, when bereft of honour,<br>
Are like low and trodden grass! (59)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“But look aloft! Yon mighty peaks<br>
By living beings are not crossed!<br>
Loftiness is virtue rare,<br>
Honour is a mortal’s boast!<br>
Fickle Fortune smiles upon us,<br>
True and constant is our fame,<br>
And the name of man befits us,<br>
While high honour decks our name!<br>
Highest in the rolls of honour!—<br>
He is worthy of his fame;<br>
And the finger of the counter<br>
Pointeth not to worthier name!<br>
Even this lofty towering range<br>
Might be crossed by living wight,<br>
But the man of worth and honour<br>
Is inviolate in his might!<br>
Glory on their race they bring,<br>
Glory on the earth they spread,<br>
Whose effulgent stainless honour<br>
Casts the moonbeams in the shade!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-232"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="232" data-folio="194" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n231_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 194">[p. 194]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And their wrath like lurid lightning<br>
Cowering foemen soon will pierce;<br>
And their name in glory’s records<br>
Stands in pride through untold years! (65)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“I seek not joys, I seek not wealth,<br>
Fleeting as the water’s race,<br>
Nor trembling for the fear of death,<br>
Seek I Brahmâ’s holy grace!<br>
But I seek to wash the stain,—<br>
Stain for which this heart hath bled,—<br>
With the tear-drops for our foes,<br>
By their sorrowing widows shed!<br>
If the hope on which I’ve rested<br>
Be unreal, idle, vain,<br>
Be it so! Thy words are wasted,<br>
Pardon if I cause thee pain!<br>
Till I conquer,—crush my foes,<br>
Win again our long-lost fame,<br>
Salvation’s self to me were vain,—<br>
Hindrance to my lofty aim!<br>
For the man is yet unborn,<br>
Or is dead like trodden grass,<br>
Who will let his good sword sleep,<br>
Tamely let his glory pass!<br>
Whose warm blood moves not in ire,—<br>
The conquered loon,—the crouching slave,—<br>
Dost thou, holy anchorite,<br>
Call him man,—that abject knave?<br>
Beshrew the title of a man,<br>
Void of worth and manlike pride;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-233"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="233" data-folio="195" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n232_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 195">[p. 195]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Welcome is that honoured name<br>
Graced by worth and manlike deed !<br>
He whose name in wonder spoken<br>
Pales the name of other men,<br>
He whose deeds are known to foemen,<br>
He is <span class="sc">Man</span> among all men ! (73)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Yet more ! Our good, long-suffering elder<br>
Vows revenge against the foe,—<br>
Awaits my help, as thirsty travellers<br>
Wait the cooling draught in woe.<br>
Unmindful of his elder’s hest,<br>
Unmindful of his elder’s bliss,<br>
The man who shirks his task in trouble,<br>
Is a traitor to his race !<br>
And wherefore preachest to me, father !<br>
Life retired before my time,<br>
The ancients forest-life prescribed<br>
Not in our youth but after prime.<br>
My mother, living in the woods,<br>
My brothers in misfortunes dire,<br>
My duty, as by Vyâsa told,<br>
Forbid me, father, to retire !<br>
And honour’s maxims, holy saint,<br>
Forbid the noble and the true<br>
To seek for safety in retirement<br>
From a great and conquering foe !<br>
Then let me, father ! on these hills<br>
Like clouds of autumn waste away !<br>
Or pleasing Indra, mighty monarch,—<br>
Wipe our shame in battle’s fray ! ”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-234"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="234" data-folio="196" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n233_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 196">[p. 196]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Arjun spoke, and gracious Indra<br>
Stood in heavenly form and might,<br>
Clasped the young and pious hero,<br>
Bade him worship Siva great ! (80)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ To Siva, mighty god, thy worship pay,<br>
For he alone can help thee in the fray,<br>
And thou shalt be unconquered in thy might ; ”<br>
Thus Indra spoke, and vanished from his sight.
