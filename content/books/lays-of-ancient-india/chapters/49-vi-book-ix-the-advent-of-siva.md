<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-235"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="235" data-folio="197" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n234_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 197">[p. 197]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK IX.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>THE ADVENT OF SIVA.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">By</span> Indra’s mandate Arjun great<br>
Once more began his pious rites,<br>
And worship paid to saintly Siva<br>
Dwelling in far Kailâsa’s heights.<br>
Firm in his purpose, pure in heart,<br>
Unwearied in the sun’s hot ray,<br>
In rigid fasts, in penance long<br>
The pious chief passed many a day.<br>
Mortifying flesh and sense,<br>
In penance long he passed his hours,<br>
In pious rites, unmoved as rock,<br>
For high resolve hath wondrous power !<br>
Luscious fruits that ripened near him,<br>
Crystal rills that rippled by,—<br>
For righteous is sweetest nectar,—<br>
Drew from him nor wish nor sigh !<br>
He boasted not, he ne’er despaired,<br>
He never ceased from righteous toil,<br>
Nor wrath nor passions in his heart<br>
His noble steadfast faith could soil.<br>
He wore a world subduing power,<br>
Though pale with many a rigid rite,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-236"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="236" data-folio="198" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n235_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 198">[p. 198]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And saints beheld him with a fear,—<br>
The great in heart are great in might! (6)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Brighter than the nightly fires<br>
His radiance seemed the woods to fill,<br>
More mighty than the boundless sea,<br>
And loftier than the towering hill!<br>
His sacred <em>mantras</em> ever chanting,<br>
With a beauteous light he shone;<br>
And on his features fell a radiance<br>
Like the halo of the sun!<br>
Clad in armour dark, he wore<br>
His mighty bow across his chest;<br>
So wears the lofty wood-clad hill<br>
The glorious rainbow on his breast!<br>
When for ablutions,—fixed by rules,—<br>
He walked betimes, in morning’s hour,<br>
The mountain felt his mighty tread,<br>
For worth is might and worth is power!<br>
A wondrous lustre on him shone<br>
When Arjun stood serene and high,—<br>
It shone across the firmament,<br>
And flashed upon the upper sky!<br>
And on moonless nights there fell<br>
Upon the prince a wondrous ray,—<br>
And like the silver beams of moon<br>
It pierced the darkness of the sky!<br>
So bright, so clear the light celestial<br>
That the paléd orb of sun<br>
Marching through the cloudless sky<br>
Scarce with wonted radiance shone! (13)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-237"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="237" data-folio="199" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n236_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 199">[p. 199]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Holy hermits viewed in awe<br>
His crimson locks, his bow unbent!<br>
They whispered,—“ Is it Siva’s self<br>
On mighty Asur’s death intent ?<br>
Is he great Indra or the Sun ?<br>
Or Fire, who helps our pious rites ?<br>
No mortal he of woman born,<br>
Such glory decks no anchorites.”<br>
But unlike the tongues of Fire<br>
Serene was Arjun’s radiant light ;<br>
Unlike the scorching rays of Sun,<br>
’Twas gentle in its wondrous might !<br>
As virtues seek true gentleness,<br>
As moral rules seek peaceful thought,<br>
As righteous laws seek precepts pure,<br>
The troubled saints great Siva sought ! (17)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Dazzled, blinded, when they came,<br>
By Siva’s more than solar light,<br>
They sought in vain with mortal eyes<br>
To compass Siva’s glorious might !<br>
In humble prayer they sought the Lord<br>
Of times to come and times gone by,—<br>
And by its grace they faintly saw<br>
His glorious mien, his triple eye !<br>
Resting on his sacred bull,<br>
His glorious arm of wondrous might,<br>
Sweet Umâ’s lord, to Umâ dear,<br>
Stood forth upon the mountain’s height !<br>
Far distant from the living world<br>
He stood upon a snowy height ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-238"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="238" data-folio="200" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n237_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 200">[p. 200]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">But rock and ocean, earth and heaven<br>
Felt his presence and his might !<br>
Coiling serpents stretched their length<br>
Around the muscles of his feet,<br>
As on the vast and boundless earth<br>
High mountain ranges spread their height !<br>
And on his blue and ample neck<br>
Great coiling serpents, white as snow,—<br>
Even like the thread of twice-born mortals,—<br>
Caught its dark and tremulous glow !<br>
By his tresses partly hid,<br>
Like Gangâ’s ripples looked the moon,<br>
And on his fair and ample forehead<br>
With a gentle radiance shone !<br>
Permission gained, the holy saints<br>
Addressed the God with many a prayer,<br>
Told him how a mortal’s penance<br>
Filled the earth with mighty fear. (25)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Mighty Lord of all the worlds !<br>
A mortal wielding Vritra’s might,<br>
Unceasing penances performs,<br>
The sun obscuring by his light !<br>
A bow he wears and mighty quivers,<br>
Armour and a wondrous blade,<br>
And plaited locks and skin and barks,—<br>
A saint ! in arms accoutred dread !<br>
Earth trembles ’neath his mighty tread,<br>
And when he prays at evening’s glow,<br>
The starry skies are hushed to peace,<br>
The evening breezes cease to blow !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-239"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="239" data-folio="201" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n238_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 201">[p. 201]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">His force terrific could subdue<br>
This world with Gods and Asurs brave!<br>
What mighty task, what daring feat<br>
His matchless strength might not achieve!<br>
Or if he seeks to rule the world,—<br>
Or to destroy in wantonness,—<br>
Or to attain salvation pure,<br>
Great Lord of hosts! we may not guess!<br>
Thou knowest all, O mighty Lord!<br>
World hides no secret from thine eye!<br>
Thou knowest all, and thou canst save,<br>
And we are safe when thou art nigh!” (31)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Unto them great Siva spoke<br>
In accents deep and full of grace,<br>
Deep as the troubled ocean’s roar<br>
Resounding to the ends of space! (32)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Know ye, who with lofty rites<br>
Worships in Badrika’s heath?<br>
Earth-born man,—but part of Him,<br>
Who is life and who is death!<br>
Lofty penance he performs<br>
Foes to conquer and to quell,<br>
Foes who rule this boundless earth<br>
Indra’s mighty power assail!<br>
By the will of Lotus-Born,<br>
Krishna and great Arjun came,—<br>
Sons of men by mortals bred,—<br>
Men to save in Brahmâ’s name! (35)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-240"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="240" data-folio="202" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n239_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 202">[p. 202]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ But the wily Asur,—Muka—<br>
Dreaded by the gods in sky,<br>
Seeks to kill the mighty Arjun ;—<br>
I must to his safety hie.<br>
But to conquer mighty Arjun<br>
Openly ’twere vain to try ;<br>
Muka takes the form of wild-boar<br>
To achieve his purpose sly !<br>
I will take the form of hunter,<br>
Pierce the wild-boar in the heart,<br>
Claiming honour of the wild sport,<br>
Arjun too will send his dart !<br>
Pale with rigid rites and penance,<br>
Still he wields a wondrous might,<br>
Ere the mortal wins my favour<br>
He must prove his worth in fight ! ” (39)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus speaking to the holy saints<br>
A forester in guise he went !<br>
His ample chest bedewed with toil,<br>
With many a pearl and sandal paint !<br>
With flowering tendrils rudely tied<br>
His clustering manly locks he wore,<br>
And bright a peacock’s painted feather<br>
O’er his bloodshot eyes he bore !<br>
A mighty bow with arrows keen<br>
He carried in his brawny hand,—<br>
And like a rain-cloud dark he looked,<br>
The leader of a forest band !<br>
And all his hosts in various guise,<br>
Obedient to their Master’s word,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-241"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="241" data-folio="203" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n240_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 203">[p. 203]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Assembled like a hunting troop,<br>
With bow and arrow, lance and sword !<br>
They parcelled out the mountain wood,<br>
Obedient to their Leader’s will,<br>
Shaking the earth with mighty sound,<br>
Forward marched the hunters still ! (44)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Screams and sounds of birds and beasts<br>
Filled far and near the forest land,<br>
As if the woods and mountains quaked<br>
In terror of that hunter-band !<br>
And the flying beasts and birds<br>
Forgot awhile their mutual strife ;<br>
A common danger made them comrades,<br>
And a common fear of life !<br>
The timid Chamarî <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-49-fn-001" href="#sec-49-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-49-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> feign would fly,<br>
Bewildered by the hunter’s yell,—<br>
But in the jungles wild and thick<br>
Was caught his white and bushy tail.<br>
The mighty lion, forest-king,<br>
Owned in his heart no dastard fear !<br>
He calmly viewed the hunters pass,<br>
Through echoing woods, through gorges drear !<br>
The fish leap out from jungle lake,<br>
The wild beasts on its margin crowd,<br>
And tall trees by the tuskers broken<br>
With their juice its waters cloud.<br>
Buffaloes tearing through the forest<br>
Broke in twain the tangled trees,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-242"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="242" data-folio="204" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n241_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 204">[p. 204]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And many a wild flower, tossed and shaken,<br>
With their fragrance filled the breeze !<br>
Wild beasts, splashing through the water,<br>
Felled the plantains, crushed the grain,<br>
Dashed aside the water-lily,<br>
Like a summer storm and rain !<br>
Sweeping thus through forest lands<br>
At length the hunters came and stood,<br>
Where grazed in peace the gentle deer,<br>
Nor dreaded harm,—in Arjun’s wood !<br>
And Siva marked,—black as a cloud,—<br>
A wild boar in the covert rise ;<br>
Tearing the earth with angry tusks,<br>
It flew,—the Asur in disguise ! (53)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Leaving his hosts behind the forest lake,<br>
Concealed by creepers and by jungle-brake,<br>
The Lord of hosts, resistless in his force,<br>
Tracked the mighty wild boar in its course.
