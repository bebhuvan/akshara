during the hundred or two hundred years after the death of Gautama<br>
(Buddha), just as the four Christian Gospels were composed and settled<br>
within a century or two after the death of Jesus. . . . The Three Pitakas<br>
are known as the Sutta Pitaka, the Vinaya Pitaka, and the Abhidhamma<br>
Pitaka. The works comprised in the Sutta Pitaka profess to record<br>
the sayings and doings of Gautama Buddha himself. . . . The Vinaya<br>
Pitaka contains very minute rules, often on the most trivial subjects,<br>
for the conduct of monks and nuns. . . . And lastly, the Abhidhamma<br>
Pitaka contains disquisitions on various subjects, on the conditions of<br>
life in different worlds, on personal qualities, on the elements, on the<br>
causes of existence, &amp;c.”<br>
—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. pp. 315, 316.</p></blockquote></div>
