<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-82"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="82" data-folio="44" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n81_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 44">[p. 44]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>A FUNERAL HYMN.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">“There are some reasons, which Professor Max Müller explains with his usual eloquence, for supposing that the original conception of Yama in the Rig Veda is the conception of the departing sun. The sun sets and disappears, just as a man’s life ends; and the imagination of a simple race would easily conjure up an after-world where that departed deity would preside over departed spirits. . . . In the Veda, he is the beneficent king of the happy world where the virtuous live and enjoy themselves in after-life. Clothed in a glorious body, they sit by the side of Yama in the realms of light and sparkling waters, they enjoy endless felicity there, and are adored here below under the name of Pitris, or fathers.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 87.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The following hymn is a part of the funeral ritual of ancient times, and will be read with interest.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">I.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Awake</span> the song, the offerings bring,<br>
To mighty Yama, gracious king !<br>
For he has cleared for us the way<br>
To realms of bliss and light of day !<br>
And pious souls to Yama take their flight,<br>
To realms of sparkling waters and of light !</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Yama showed to us the way !<br>
The path that never fades away !<br>
Our fathers by that path have gone<br>
To the realms of setting sun !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-83"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="83" data-folio="45" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n82_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 45">[p. 45]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And every being, after life's brief day,<br>
Shall travel by the same eternal way !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-001" href="#sec-17-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Then come thou, Yama ! gracious King,<br>
And with thee our forefathers bring !<br>
The feast we spread, the hymns we sing,<br>
Then consecrate these rites, O King !<br>
Pleased with our gifts, partake our ample feast,<br>
And with our fathers in this mansion rest !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Then come thou, Yama ! gracious King,<br>
And with thee our forefathers<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-002" href="#sec-17-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> bring !<br>
Vivasvat, thy mighty sire,<br>
Comes he to our sacred fire !<br>
On the soft and sacred <em>varhis</em><sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-003" href="#sec-17-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">‡</a></sup> rest,<br>
Pleased with our gifts, partake our ample feast !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The Angirasas, Bhrigus great,<br>
And Atharvans<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-004" href="#sec-17-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">§</a></sup> on us wait !<br>
From the regions of the blest<br>
Come to share our ample feast !<br>
Then pour libations to our fathers great,<br>
And may our rites their approbation meet !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-84"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="84" data-folio="46" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n83_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 46">[p. 46]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">[<em>To the Dead Person :</em>]</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">By the path our fathers know,<br>
Go, departed being ! go !<br>
In realms where sparkling rivers roll,<br>
Live, and happy be thy soul !<br>
Varuna great, and Yama, gracious king,<br>
Dwell in these realms, then hasten on thy wing !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Haste thee on thy unseen wing,<br>
Flee to Yama, gracious king !<br>
Meet our fathers who have gone,<br>
Reap the virtues you have won !<br>
Leave sins behind,—follow the setting sun,<br>
Leave mortal shape,—a brighter form put on !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-005" href="#sec-17-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, X. 14.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-17-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-17-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">One verse after this has been omitted.
