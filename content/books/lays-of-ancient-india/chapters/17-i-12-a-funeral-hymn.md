<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-82"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="82" data-folio="44" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n81_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 44">[p. 44]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">“There are some reasons, which Professor Max Müller explains with his usual eloquence, for supposing that the original conception of Yama in the Rig Veda is the conception of the departing sun. The sun sets and disappears, just as a man’s life ends; and the imagination of a simple race would easily conjure up an after-world where that departed deity would preside over departed spirits. . . . In the Veda, he is the beneficent king of the happy world where the virtuous live and enjoy themselves in after-life. Clothed in a glorious body, they sit by the side of Yama in the realms of light and sparkling waters, they enjoy endless felicity there, and are adored here below under the name of Pitris, or fathers.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 87.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The following hymn is a part of the funeral ritual of ancient times, and will be read with interest.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">I.</span><br>
<span class="line"><span class="sc">Awake</span> the song, the offerings bring,</span><br>

<span class="line">For he has cleared for us the way</span><br>
<span class="line">To realms of bliss and light of day!</span><br>
<span class="line">And pious souls to Yama take their flight,</span><br>
<span class="line">To realms of sparkling waters and of light!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>

<span class="line">The path that never fades away!</span><br>

<span class="line">Then consecrate these rites, O King!</span><br>
<span class="line">Pleased with our gifts, partake our ample feast,</span><br>
<span class="line">And with our fathers in this mansion rest!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">Then come thou, Yama! gracious King,</span><br>
<span class="line">And with thee our forefathers<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-002" href="#sec-17-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> bring!</span><br>

<span class="line">On the soft and sacred <em>varhis</em><sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-17-fn-003" href="#sec-17-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-17-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">‡</a></sup> rest,</span><br>
