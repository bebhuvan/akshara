<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-58"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="58" data-folio="20" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n57_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 20">[p. 20]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>AGNI, OR THE FIRE.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote class="epigraph-block"><p>“Agni is the god of Fire ; the Ignis of the Latins, the Ogni of the<br>
Sclavonians.”—Muir’s <em>Sanscrit Texts</em>,</p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote class="epigraph-block"><p>“All the names of the Fire and the Fire-gods were carried away by<br>
the Western Aryans ; and we have Prometheus answering to Pramantha,<br>
Phoronus to Bharanyu, and the Latin Vulcanus to the Sanscrit Ulka.”<br>
—Cox’s <em>Mythology of Aryan Nations</em>.</p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The hymn translated below is one of peculiar interest, because it is said to have been composed by a pious lady, Visvavârâ.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">I.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">LIGHTED</span> Agni flames forth high,<br>
Flings a radiance on the sky,<br>
And his lustre, glorious, bright,<br>
Mingles with the morning light.<br>
And Visvavârâ chants her holy prayer,<br>
Faces the east, and brings her gifts to Fire !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Lord of Eternity ! Lord of skies !<br>
Presiding Lord of sacrifice !<br>
Agni ! Whoso worships thee,<br>
By him with thy blessings be !<br>
Whose blazing altar sacred Agni lights,—<br>
Affluence dwells with him and pious rites.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-59"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="59" data-folio="21" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n58_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 21">[p. 21]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Agni! On the altar shine,<br>
Flames and radiance bright be thine!<br>
Grant us wealth with thy red flame,<br>
Quell our foes, increase our fame!<br>
Invest our homes with blessings from above,<br>
And link our men and wives in bonds of love!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Agni! On the altar shine,<br>
Flames and radiance bright be thine!<br>
And I lift my pious hymn<br>
To thy bright effulgent beam!<br>
Bounteous god! Red lustre e’er be thine,<br>
Flame on our altar, glorious and divine!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">With pious hands we light thy flame,<br>
With pious lips we chant thy name!<br>
Invoke unto our sacrifice<br>
The glorious bright gods of the skies!<br>
For thou art Priest<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-10-fn-001" href="#sec-10-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-10-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> in every pious rite,<br>
And makest gifts to gods with radiance bright!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-60"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="60" data-folio="22" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n59_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 22">[p. 22]</button></div><h3 class="sec-h2">6.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Then let us unto Agni pray,<br>
And he our offerings will convey !<br>
And let us unto Agni sing,<br>
And he the radiant gods will bring !<br>
Priest and God ! to thee we humbly pray,<br>
Our pious offerings to the gods convey !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, V. 28.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-10-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-10-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">“As no sacrifice could be performed without fire, Agni, or Fire, was called the invoker of the gods.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 85.</span></li></ol></section>
