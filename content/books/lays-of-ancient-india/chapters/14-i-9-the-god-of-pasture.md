<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-72"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="72" data-folio="34" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n71_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 34">[p. 34]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE GOD OF PASTURE.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote class="epigraph-block"><p>“Pûshan is the sun as viewed by shepherds in their wanderings in<br>
quest of fresh pasture-lands. He travels in a chariot yoked with goats,<br>
guides men and cattle in their travels and migrations, and knows and<br>
protects the flocks. The hymns to Pûshan, therefore, often breathe a<br>
simplicity which is truly pastoral.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>,<br>
vol. i. pp. 84, 85.</p></blockquote></div><h3 class="sec-h2">1.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Pûshan</span>! radiant god of day!<br>
Lead us o’er the dangerous way.<br>
Vanquish every hurtful foe,<br>
Free our path from every woe,<br>
Child of the Clouds! Oh lead us evermore,—<br>
We follow,—as our fathers did before!</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Smite the skulking robber vile,<br>
Lurking in the dark defile;<br>
Smite the murderer and the thief<br>
Who wait in stealth to take our life.<br>
Remove the wily foes who lurk before,<br>
And Pûshan! lead us safe for evermore!<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-14-fn-001" href="#sec-14-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-14-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-73"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="73" data-folio="35" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n72_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 35">[p. 35]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thou the pathless waste dost know,<br>
And Pûshan ! thou canst quell the foe !<br>
Thou didst lead our fathers right<br>
In their wanderings, Pûshan bright !<br>
Then lead us, radiant god ! for evermore,—<br>
We follow,—as our fathers did before !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And thou hast treasures manifold,<br>
Radiant weapons, arms of gold,<br>
Foremost of the sons of light,<br>
Bounteous leader, Pûshan bright !<br>
Grant us wealth from thy unfailing store,<br>
And we will praise thy bounty evermore !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Lead us safely in thy might,<br>
Where the wily foemen wait ;<br>
Lead us over pleasant ways,<br>
Help us with thy bounteous grace.<br>
Oh ! lead us, Pûshan, in this trackless shore,<br>
And lead us, radiant god ! for evermore !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Where the grass is rich and green,<br>
Where the pasture's beauteous seen,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-74"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="74" data-folio="36" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n73_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 36">[p. 36]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Lead us o'er such pleasant way,<br>
Chase all ills and woes away !<br>
Oh ! lead us, Pûshan ! in this trackless shore,<br>
And lead us, radiant god ! for evermore !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Grant us blessings rich and good,<br>
Fill our homes with plenteous food ;<br>
Grant us pastures far and near,<br>
Make us, Pûshan ! strong in war !<br>
Oh ! lead us, Pûshan ! in this trackless shore,<br>
And lead us, radiant god ! for evermore !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">We lift our hearts and sing his praise,<br>
To Pûshan chant our pious lays,<br>
And from the good and gracious god<br>
We ask for wealth and ask for food.<br>
Oh ! lead us, Pûshan ! in this trackless shore,<br>
And lead us, radiant god ! for evermore !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, I. 42.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-14-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-14-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">The third and fourth verses are almost a repetition of what precedes, and have been omitted in the translation.</span></li></ol></section>
