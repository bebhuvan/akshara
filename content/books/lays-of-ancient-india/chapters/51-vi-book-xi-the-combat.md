<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-249"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="249" data-folio="211" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n248_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 211">[p. 211]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK XI.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>THE COMBAT.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">TOWERING</span> like a forest tree<br>
Stood the God of the lofty mind,<br>
And his darksome hosts remained<br>
Like his shadow cast behind.<br>
And against the warlike Arjun<br>
Siva shook his sounding bow,—<br>
And the mountains seemed to split,<br>
And the skies re-echoed low!<br>
Arjun shot his countless arrows<br>
With his more than mortal skill,—<br>
But by Siva’s shafts averted<br>
Arjun’s arrows useless fell!<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-51-fn-001" href="#sec-51-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-51-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Thick and fast across the sky<br>
Siva’s wingéd arrows fly,<br>
And with a lurid lustre shine,—<br>
Like the lightning’s lurid ray!<br>
Speeding through great Arjun’s shafts,<br>
On that warlike chief they fell,<br>
But the haughty warrior stood<br>
Dauntless and unshaken still!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-250"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="250" data-folio="212" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n249_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 212">[p. 212]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Still he proudly stood and fought,—<br>
Vain his skill and vain his ire !<br>
For his weapons shattered fell,<br>
And his eyes flashed angry fire !<br>
Wondering at the hunter’s skill,<br>
Arjun, conqueror of his foes,<br>
Paused in silence and in doubt,—<br>
In his heart these thoughts arose. (7,</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Warriors great of matchless power<br>
I have met and beaten all !<br>
Doth the sun bow to the moon ?<br>
Before the swain will Arjun fall ?<br>
Is this all magic, is this dream ?<br>
Or am I mighty Arjun still ?<br>
Why conquers not my matchless power<br>
This mountaineer’s untutored skill ?<br>
Rending the sky as if in twain,<br>
Shaking the wide earth’s solid frame,<br>
How fights this boorish mountaineer !<br>
Such deeds a man disguised proclaim !<br>
Not Bhîshma’s self nor Drona owns<br>
Such skill to shield, to send the dart !<br>
And can a simple mountain swain<br>
Possess such superhuman art ?<br>
Whoe’er he be, his mighty power<br>
By <em>shafts celestial</em> <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-51-fn-002" href="#sec-51-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-51-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> I will quell !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-251"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="251" data-folio="213" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n250_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 213">[p. 213]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Nor shall a forest hunter’s skill<br>
’Gainst Arjun’s matchless art prevail ! ” (12)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Pondering thus he quickly sent<br>
Mighty shaft of death-like sleep ;—<br>
And a shadow filled the sky,<br>
And the gloom of midnight deep !<br>
Faint and powerless Siva’s forces<br>
Fell into a death-like sleep,<br>
As a flippant youthful speaker<br>
Falls abashed when questioned deep !<br>
But the spreading gloom dispelling,<br>
Siva sent a radiance bright,—<br>
And as learning gloom dispelleth,—<br>
Waked his hosts to new-born light !<br>
Sorrowing Arjun thus beheld<br>
His weapon lost and vain his art !<br>
Fired with wrath the hero sent<br>
The shaft of snakes,—a dreaded dart !<br>
Pouring from their poisonous tongues<br>
Liquid fire like lightning bright,<br>
Countless myriad winged serpents<br>
In the blue vault took their flight !<br>
With their hue of molten gold<br>
Reddening all the livid sky,<br>
Flew the snakes across the view,<br>
As the flashing meteors fly !<br>
But unnumbered golden eagles<br>
Issued from great Siva’s dart !<br>
And the serpents fled those birds,<br>
Vain again was Arjun’s art !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-252"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="252" data-folio="214" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n251_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 214">[p. 214]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Burning with a mighty anger<br>
At the hunter’s skill, he sped<br>
A flaming shaft of fatal force,<br>
The shaft of fire, not fuel-fed !<br>
With a sound like bursting rocks,<br>
Hiding the sun with flashes dire,<br>
Casting crimson sparks around,<br>
Terrific rose the mighty fire !<br>
Towering high like mountain peaks,<br>
Like molten gold, before, behind,<br>
Flaming like the Kinsuk flower<br>
Spread the fire before the wind !<br>
Rolled the red fire’s tongues of flame,<br>
As on the world’s destruction bent !<br>
Siva quelled the mighty flame<br>
By the shaft of clouds he sent.<br>
Quick and bright the lightning gleamed,<br>
And the rain-clouds, mountain high,<br>
Deep incessant torrents poured<br>
Like Gangâ pouring from the sky !<br>
The fire was quenched, but Arjun still<br>
Sent other shafts of wondrous might ;<br>
But vain his efforts,—vain our toil,<br>
When struggling ’gainst an adverse fate ! (25)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Long waged the fierce and mighty war,<br>
Till Siva, pleased with Arjun’s might,<br>
Withdrew all shafts from Arjun’s quivers,<br>
Ended thus the unequal fight !<br>
And aye withdrew from Arjun’s mien<br>
His armour streaked with rays of gold,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-253"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="253" data-folio="215" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n252_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 215">[p. 215]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">So from the sun the wind withdraws<br>
The lightning clouds in many a fold.<br>
Like shining blade from scabbard drawn,<br>
Like cobra darting from his skin,<br>
Or like the tusker wild and furious,<br>
Breaking from his cord and chain,<br>
Or like the lion, with wild roar<br>
Springing from his desert cave,<br>
Or like the flame which leaps from smoke,<br>
All armour-less, shone Arjun brave !<br>
Undaunted chief ! He little recked<br>
His person streaked with purple blood !<br>
Forward he leaped with angry shout,<br>
And shook the hills beneath his tread !<br>
And with his bow, like Indra’s lightning,<br>
Wielding it with all his might,<br>
He struck his foe, as strikes a tusker<br>
At some tree of ancient height !<br>
But vain the effort, vain the toil,<br>
The shattered weapon useless flew ;—<br>
Last refuge of his dauntless pride,<br>
His mighty sword at last he drew ! (32)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Shielding self from Siva’s darts,<br>
Marching forward bold and free,<br>
Arjun came with measured step,<br>
Glorious as the sunlit sea !<br>
But the good and trusty falchion<br>
By great Siva’s arrows driven,<br>
Broke and fell with sound of thunder,<br>
As from clouds the bolt of heaven !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-254"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="254" data-folio="216" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n253_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 216">[p. 216]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Reft of bow and shining blade,<br>
Reft of armour golden bright,<br>
Like the monster of the sea,<br>
Darted Arjun in his might! (35)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Viewing Arjun reft of weapons,<br>
Siva cast his arms aside,<br>
Hand to hand the warriors wrestle<br>
Like two tuskers in their pride!<br>
And the sound of mutual blows,<br>
The hunter and the hero gave,<br>
Like the sound of bursting rocks,<br>
Was echoed from the mountain cave!<br>
And the blows which Siva dealt<br>
Left their impress on the chief!<br>
Proudly Arjun bore the wounds,<br>
Felt no pain and felt no grief!<br>
But the blows which Arjun gave<br>
Were beaten back from Siva’s chest,<br>
As the surges of the ocean<br>
From the mountain’s rocky breast!<br>
Reeling from the hunter’s blows,<br>
Charging with a mighty rage,<br>
Arjun grasped him in his arms,<br>
In close fight the chiefs engage!<br>
With their arms and with their feet<br>
They tug and strain and wrestle still,<br>
And beneath their mighty tread<br>
Shakes the everlasting hill!<br>
Siva’s hosts bewildered view<br>
The wondrous fight,—with terror faint!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-255"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="255" data-folio="217" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n254_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 217">[p. 217]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Who’s above and who below ?<br>
Is that Siva or the saint ?<br>
Freed at last from mutual grasp,<br>
Sounding their arms they spring in air !<br>
The bank gives way beneath their tread,<br>
And falls into the streamlet fair !<br>
Siva springs into the air,<br>
Arjun pulls him by the feet,<br>
And to throw him on the ground,<br>
Holds them close with all his might. (44)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Mercy moves the heart of Sambhu great,<br>
For pious Arjun holds him by the feet !<br>
The god relents,—his heart is full of grace,<br>
He holds the hero in a dear embrace !
