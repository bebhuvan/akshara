<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-156"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="156" data-folio="118" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n155_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 118">[p. 118]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>MUKTÂLATÂ, PRINCESS OF CEYLON.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Kshemendra is a voluminous but not a great poet, and flourished in Kashmir in the eleventh century after Christ. His versification is fluent and graceful, and he seems to have condensed all that was best in older Sanscrit literature—poetry, rhetoric, tales, and fiction—into compact readable works. Buddhist stories too did not come amiss to him, and in his <em>Kalpalatâ</em> he gives us 108 Buddhist tales, one of which is given below. This work, <em>Kalpalatâ</em>, like most other Buddhist works, was lost in India, but my respected friend, Mr. Sarat Chandra Das, has during his journeys in Thibet recovered a copy of the work with a Thibetan translation, which is being published now by the Asiatic Society of Bengal.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">1.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Like</span> the bright moon’s golden crescent,<br>
Rising from the milky sea,<br>
She was born with heavenly beauty<br>
In Siṃhala<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-39-fn-001" href="#sec-39-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-39-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> bright and free.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">On her birth a shower of bright pearls<br>
From the skies auspicious fell;<br>
Hence they named her Muktâlatâ,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-39-fn-002" href="#sec-39-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-39-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup><br>
Princess of Siṃhala’s isle.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-157"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="157" data-folio="119" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n156_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 119">[p. 119]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">3.<br>
And she grew in grace and brightness,<br>
Lankâ’s royal house to bless ;<br>
And as worth brings sweet contentment,<br>
So her years brought loveliness.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">4.<br>
Merchants from the famed Srâvasti,<br>
Happily it so befell,<br>
Crossed the vast and perilous ocean,<br>
Came to trade in Lankâ’s isle.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">5.<br>
And they sung the sacred <em>Gâthâs</em>,<br>
As their nightly sleep they sought ;<br>
Sung the holy lay which teaches<br>
Precepts that our Master taught.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">6.<br>
From her inner palace chambers<br>
Muktâ heard the beauteous song ;<br>
Bade the merchants to her presence,<br>
Asked them what it was they sung.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">7.<br>
And they told the raptured maiden,<br>
“ Princess ! it is Buddha’s word !<br>
He is bounteous to all creatures,<br>
Of all creatures he is Lord ! ”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-158"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="158" data-folio="120" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n157_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 120">[p. 120]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And the gentle-hearted princess<br>
Raptured heard great Buddha’s name ;<br>
And a brightness flushed her forehead,<br>
And a tremor shook her frame !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Eagerly the pea-fowl listens<br>
To the cloud, presaging rain ;<br>
Eagerly the princess listened,<br>
Who this Lord ?—she asked again.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To the princess, pious-hearted,<br>
By her questions gratified,<br>
Spoke the merchants of great Buddha,<br>
Lord of all creation wide.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">11.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">By the merchants’ tale reminded<br>
Of her previous humble birth,<br>
Unto them she gave a letter<br>
For great Buddha, Lord of earth.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">12.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And the traders crossed the ocean,<br>
Reached their own, their native land,<br>
Spoke to Buddha of the princess,<br>
Gave her letter in his hand.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-159"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="159" data-folio="121" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n158_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 121">[p. 121]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">13.<br>
And our Master, all fore-knowing,—<br>
Knowing all by power of thought,—<br>
Moved by tenderness and mercy,<br>
Thus perused the maiden’s note!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">14.<br>
“Thy remembrance merit bringeth,<br>
Cures the longings of this world,<br>
And imparting righteous knowledge<br>
Is like nectar to me, Lord!”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">15.<br>
Thus our Master, blessed Buddha,<br>
Briefly read the loving note,<br>
And a gentle smile betokened<br>
All the workings of his thought.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">16.<br>
And with skill and knowledge wondrous,<br>
Which the painters never knew,<br>
For the princess of Siṃhala<br>
On a sheet his likeness drew.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">17.<br>
By his mandate all the merchants<br>
In their vessels sailed anew,<br>
Reached Siṃhala,—to the princess<br>
Gave the sheet our Master drew.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-160"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="160" data-folio="122" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n159_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 122">[p. 122]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">18.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And the people viewed the painting<br>
Placed upon a golden shelf,<br>
And with honour contemplating<br>
Seemed t’ attain the Lord himself !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">19.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Written under that sweet likeness,<br>
All the people, wondering truly,<br>
Saw the holy Three Asylums,<br>
Saw the Five Instructions holy !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">20.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And the Noble Eightfold Path,<br>
Sweetly writ, with wisdom rife,<br>
With the Doctrine of Causation,—<br>
Life to death, and death to life !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">21.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Blazing bright in golden letters,<br>
On it shone the Holy Word,—<br>
Truth explained in beauteous language,<br>
Written by our blessed Lord.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">22.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Sufferers from life’s bitter sorrows !<br>
Issue from your mansions drear,<br>
Worldly objects snake-like strangle !<br>
Trust in Buddha, cast all fear.”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-161"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="161" data-folio="123" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n160_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 123">[p. 123]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">23.<br>
And the monarch’s noble daughter<br>
Viewed the likeness fair and holy,<br>
And was freed from all the longings<br>
Bred of ignorance and folly.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">24.<br>
Tall and fair his golden likeness,<br>
Broad his shoulders, mighty arms,<br>
Eyes in contemplation closéd,<br>
Stately nose and manly charms!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">25.<br>
Beauteous all in nature’s beauty,<br>
Ears and locks by art unaided,<br>
Clad in russet bark,—like mountain<br>
By the evening’s red cloud shaded!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">26.<br>
Teaching duty by his bearing,<br>
By his bright face teaching good,<br>
Mercy and forbearance teaching,—<br>
Such the form the princess viewed!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">27.<br>
Bowing, till the budding blossoms<br>
From her ears and ringlets rained,<br>
With them earthly joys discarding,<br>
ruth supreme the princess gained.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-162"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="162" data-folio="124" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n161_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 124">[p. 124]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">28.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">In a moment’s time achieving<br>
Truth divine and knowledge rare,<br>
Lost in joy and pious wonder<br>
Thus in gladness spoke the fair.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">29.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Chaser of the world’s illusions,<br>
Saintly Buddha, dwelling far,<br>
Present by this radiant likeness,<br>
Lovelier than the lotus fair!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">30.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“I have crossed the world’s illusions,<br>
Heart’s true concentration found,<br>
Passions, troubles are discarded,<br>
Peace, like nectar, flows around!”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">31.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus she spoke, and unto Buddha<br>
Pearls and costly jewels sent;<br>
For the use of holy <em>Sangha</em><br>
With these gifts the merchants went.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">32.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">O’er the sea, unto our Master,<br>
In their ships the merchants came,<br>
Bowing unto him they rendered<br>
Pearls and many a costly gem.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-163"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="163" data-folio="125" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n162_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 125">[p. 125]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">33.<br>
And our Master kindly listened<br>
To the tale the merchants told.<br>
Questioned by the monk Ânanda<br>
Unto him he thus revealed.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">34.<br>
“Rohiká, a servant maiden,<br>
In a Sâkya’s house who stayed,<br>
She is born as Muktâlatâ,<br>
By her worth a princess made !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">35.<br>
“Mahâdhana, princely merchant,<br>
In Benares lived of yore,<br>
And his wife, a virtuous woman,<br>
Ratnâvatî name she bore.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">36.<br>
“And when died this Mahâdhana,<br>
Ratnâvatî, childless fair,<br>
On a lofty holy <em>stûpa</em><br>
Placed a necklace rich and rare.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">37.<br>
“For this action, elevated,<br>
She as Muktâlatâ shone,<br>
Born as Lankâ’s beauteous princess<br>
Now she has <em>Nirvâna</em> won !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-164"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="164" data-folio="126" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n163_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 126">[p. 126]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">38.<br>
“She it was, whom pride of treasures<br>
Maddened in a previous birth,<br>
Hence, a while, as servant maiden<br>
She was born and served on earth!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">39.<br>
“Deeds that are by mortals rendered,<br>
Good or bad on this our earth,<br>
Yield their fruits in equal measure<br>
Ripened in a future birth!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">40.<br>
“Virtue is a beauteous creeper,<br>
Rooted well in noble worth,<br>
Bearing fame as scented blossoms,<br>
Bearing fruits most sweet on earth!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">41.<br>
“Evil life is poisonous creeper,<br>
Rooted in dark deeds of blame,<br>
Yielding dire delusions only,<br>
Endless misery and shame!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">42.<br>
“In this life’s wild dreary desert,<br>
Shun, ye men, all deeds of sin;<br>
Bitter are the woes that follow,<br>
Sorrows and repentance keen!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-165"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="165" data-folio="127" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n164_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 127">[p. 127]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">43.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“In the acts of grace rejoicing,<br>
Ever deeds of virtue do,<br>
Moistened by the dew of Mercy<br>
Sweet the fruits of Virtue grow !”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>From Kshemendra’s Kalpalâtâ,</em> VII.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-39-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-39-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">Ceylon.</span></li><li id="sec-39-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-39-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">†.</a> <span class="fn-body">Which means a Pearl Creeper.</span></li></ol></section>
