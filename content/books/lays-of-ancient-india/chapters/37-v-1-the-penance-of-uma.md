<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-143"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="143" data-folio="105" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n142_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 105">[p. 105]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Umâ, in the extract given before from the Kena Upanishad, is divine intelligence, explaining to the celestial powers the nature of Brahman or God. In later times, however, she acquired a more distinct personality, and was considered to be the daughter of the god of the Himâlayas, and the consort of Siva or Sambhu, one of the Hindu Trinity. Her marriage with Siva is the subject of the beautiful poem of Kâlidâsa from which the following portion has been translated.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">I.</span><br>
<span class="line"><span class="sc">Long</span> through sultry Summer evenings</span><br>
<span class="line">Did her mighty penance last,</span><br>
<span class="line">Long through Rains and through the Autumn,</span><br>
<span class="line">In the Dews and Winter’s blast.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>
<span class="line">And when Spring the green woods mantled <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-37-fn-001" href="#sec-37-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-37-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></span><br>
<span class="line">With its verdure soft and green,</span><br>
<span class="line">To the hermitage a Brâhman</span><br>
<span class="line">Came, attired in barks and skin.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">3.</span><br>
<span class="line">And in language sweet and courteous</span><br>
<span class="line">Questioned Umâ of her rite,</span><br>
<span class="line">Asked her of her plants and creepers,</span><br>
<span class="line">Asked her of the rills so bright.</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-144"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="144" data-folio="106" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n143_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 106">[p. 106]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not by Gangâ’s holy waters</span><br>
<span class="line">Are these sacred mountains graced,</span><br>
<span class="line">Umâ! as by righteous actions,</span><br>
<span class="line">By thy holy penance blest!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">5.</span><br>
<span class="line">“But a doubt in me ariseth,</span><br>
<span class="line">Pardon if too far I go,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Brâhmans are by nature curious,</span><br>
<span class="line">Seek for hidden thoughts to know.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">6.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Thou art born of noble parents,</span><br>
<span class="line">Decked with world-surpassing beauty!</span><br>
<span class="line">In thy youth and in thy splendour</span><br>
<span class="line">Why this penance,—this sad duty?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">7.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Noble ladies, stung with sorrow,</span><br>
<span class="line">Oft engage in pious rite,—</span><br>
<span class="line">In thy father’s happy mansions,</span><br>
<span class="line">Can grief touch thee, maiden bright?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">8.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Wherefore leaves and barks of wild trees</span><br>
<span class="line">Dim thy blooming beauty bright?</span><br>
<span class="line">Does the morning’s paler glamour</span><br>
<span class="line">Suit the moonlit starry night?</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-145"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="145" data-folio="107" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n144_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 107">[p. 107]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">9.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not for joys of heaven this penance,</span><br>
<span class="line">For this realm is like the sky;</span><br>
<span class="line">And for worthy noble suitor</span><br>
<span class="line">Need a beauteous maiden sigh?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">10.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Yet that sigh, that heaving bosom</span><br>
<span class="line">Tells of disappointed love!</span><br>
<span class="line">To a maiden of thy beauty</span><br>
<span class="line">Can a suitor cruel prove?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">11.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Can he heartless view that beauty</span><br>
<span class="line">By the red sun tinged and shaded?</span><br>
<span class="line">Can he view thy toil-worn visage</span><br>
<span class="line">Like moon’s crescent pale and faded?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">12.</span><br>
<span class="line">“But, perchance, my gentle Umâ,</span><br>
<span class="line">Of some service I may be,</span><br>
<span class="line">I have done some holy penance,</span><br>
<span class="line">Thine its fruit and blessings be!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">13.</span><br>
<span class="line">Silent Umâ dropped a tear-drop,</span><br>
<span class="line">Told her maid the tale to tell,</span><br>
<span class="line">And the maid unto the Brâhman</span><br>
<span class="line">Told the sad and piteous tale.</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-146"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="146" data-folio="108" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n145_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 108">[p. 108]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">14.</span><br>
<span class="line">“List, O youth! The pious maiden,</span><br>
<span class="line">Spurning all the gods of sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">Lifts her heart, with love so tender,</span><br>
<span class="line">To great Sambhu, lord on high!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">15.</span><br>
<span class="line">“In her father’s glorious mansions</span><br>
<span class="line">Found the maid no peace, no rest,</span><br>
<span class="line">In the cool and snow-decked grottoes</span><br>
<span class="line">Ever burnt with love her breast!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">16.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Oft in midnight’s lonely silence</span><br>
<span class="line">Weeping, she of Sambhu sung,</span><br>
<span class="line">Till the nymphs of woods and mountains</span><br>

<span class="line">“And when came the dreary morning,</span><br>

<span class="line">“Brooding on her luckless passion,</span><br>

<span class="line">In this wilderness to roam!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-147"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="147" data-folio="109" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n146_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 109">[p. 109]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">19.</span><br>
<span class="line">Smiling answered then the Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Is it true, my lovely maid,</span><br>
<span class="line">All this penance pure and holy</span><br>
<span class="line">That unlovely god to wed?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">20.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Will thy hand so soft and tender</span><br>
<span class="line">Clasp his hands where snakes entwine?</span><br>
<span class="line">Will thy rich and bridal garment</span><br>
<span class="line">On his red skins beauteous shine?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">21.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Wilt thou with such bridegroom wander</span><br>
<span class="line">Over funeral sites unblest?</span><br>
<span class="line">With the impure funeral ashes</span><br>
<span class="line">Wilt thou stain thy gentle breast?</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">22.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Obscure his birth! Unknown his wealth!</span><br>

<span class="line">On holy sites, not funeral places,</span><br>
<span class="line">Pious men their altars raise!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">23.</span><br>
<span class="line">Angry Umâ heard the Brâhman</span><br>

<span class="line">Shone her dark and flashing eye!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-148"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="148" data-folio="110" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n147_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 110">[p. 110]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">24.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Little knowest of great Sambhu,”</span><br>

<span class="line">“Lofty souls of unknown splendour</span><br>

<span class="line">“Mighty Refuge of Creation!—</span><br>

<span class="line">“Wandering in dark funeral places,—</span><br>

<span class="line">Uprose the maiden in her anger,</span><br>

<span class="line">“Won by love and won by penance,</span><br>
