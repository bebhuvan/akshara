<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-143"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="143" data-folio="105" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n142_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 105">[p. 105]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE PENANCE OF UMÂ.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Umâ, in the extract given before from the Kena Upanishad, is divine intelligence, explaining to the celestial powers the nature of Brahman or God. In later times, however, she acquired a more distinct personality, and was considered to be the daughter of the god of the Himâlayas, and the consort of Siva or Sambhu, one of the Hindu Trinity. Her marriage with Siva is the subject of the beautiful poem of Kâlidâsa from which the following portion has been translated.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">I.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Long</span> through sultry Summer evenings<br>
Did her mighty penance last,<br>
Long through Rains and through the Autumn,<br>
In the Dews and Winter’s blast.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And when Spring the green woods mantled <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-37-fn-001" href="#sec-37-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-37-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
With its verdure soft and green,<br>
To the hermitage a Brâhman<br>
Came, attired in barks and skin.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And in language sweet and courteous<br>
Questioned Umâ of her rite,<br>
Asked her of her plants and creepers,<br>
Asked her of the rills so bright.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-144"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="144" data-folio="106" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n143_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 106">[p. 106]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Not by Gangâ’s holy waters<br>
Are these sacred mountains graced,<br>
Umâ! as by righteous actions,<br>
By thy holy penance blest!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“But a doubt in me ariseth,<br>
Pardon if too far I go,—<br>
Brâhmans are by nature curious,<br>
Seek for hidden thoughts to know.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Thou art born of noble parents,<br>
Decked with world-surpassing beauty!<br>
In thy youth and in thy splendour<br>
Why this penance,—this sad duty?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Noble ladies, stung with sorrow,<br>
Oft engage in pious rite,—<br>
In thy father’s happy mansions,<br>
Can grief touch thee, maiden bright?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Wherefore leaves and barks of wild trees<br>
Dim thy blooming beauty bright?<br>
Does the morning’s paler glamour<br>
Suit the moonlit starry night?</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-145"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="145" data-folio="107" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n144_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 107">[p. 107]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Not for joys of heaven this penance,<br>
For this realm is like the sky ;<br>
And for worthy noble suitor<br>
Need a beauteous maiden sigh ?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Yet that sigh, that heaving bosom<br>
Tells of disappointed love !<br>
To a maiden of thy beauty<br>
Can a suitor cruel prove ?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">11.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Can he heartless view that beauty<br>
By the red sun tinged and shaded ?<br>
Can he view thy toil-worn visage<br>
Like moon’s crescent pale and faded ?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">12.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“But, perchance, my gentle Umâ,<br>
Of some service I may be,<br>
I have done some holy penance,<br>
Thine its fruit and blessings be !”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">13.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Silent Umâ dropped a tear-drop,<br>
Told her maid the tale to tell,<br>
And the maid unto the Brâhman<br>
Told the sad and piteous tale.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-146"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="146" data-folio="108" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n145_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 108">[p. 108]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">14.<br>
“List, O youth! The pious maiden,<br>
Spurning all the gods of sky,<br>
Lifts her heart, with love so tender,<br>
To great Sambhu, lord on high!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">15.<br>
“In her father’s glorious mansions<br>
Found the maid no peace, no rest,<br>
In the cool and snow-decked grottoes<br>
Ever burnt with love her breast!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">16.<br>
“Oft in midnight’s lonely silence<br>
Weeping, she of Sambhu sung,<br>
Till the nymphs of woods and mountains<br>
Wept to hear her plaintive song!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">17.<br>
“And when came the dreary morning,<br>
Starting from a loving dream,<br>
Umâ clasped the empty shadow,<br>
Called for Sambhu in a scream!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">18.<br>
“Brooding on her luckless passion,<br>
She has left her father’s home,<br>
To engage in mighty penance,<br>
In this wilderness to roam!”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-147"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="147" data-folio="109" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n146_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 109">[p. 109]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">19.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Smiling answered then the Brâhman,<br>
“Is it true, my lovely maid,<br>
All this penance pure and holy<br>
That unlovely god to wed?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">20.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Will thy hand so soft and tender<br>
Clasp his hands where snakes entwine?<br>
Will thy rich and bridal garment<br>
On his red skins beauteous shine?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">21.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Wilt thou with such bridegroom wander<br>
Over funeral sites unblest?<br>
With the impure funeral ashes<br>
Wilt thou stain thy gentle breast?</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">22.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Obscure his birth! Unknown his wealth!<br>
Unworthy he of thy bright grace!<br>
On holy sites, not funeral places,<br>
Pious men their altars raise!”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">23.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Angry Umâ heard the Brâhman<br>
Speaking thus of Sambhu high;<br>
Her red lips quivered in her anger,<br>
Shone her dark and flashing eye!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-148"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="148" data-folio="110" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n147_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 110">[p. 110]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">24.<br>
“Little knowest of great Sambhu,”<br>
Thus the angry maid replied ;<br>
“Lofty souls of unknown splendour<br>
Flippant mortals will deride !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">25.<br>
“Mighty Refuge of Creation !—<br>
Will he seek for paltry gain ?<br>
Arbiter of other’s fortunes !—<br>
Doth he reck what is his own ?</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">26.<br>
“Wandering in dark funeral places,—<br>
Yet of worlds the Lord is he !<br>
Wearing shape, sublime, terrific,—<br>
Yet he showers his blessings free !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">27.<br>
“Unknown his birth ! For mighty Brahmâ<br>
Does he know of Sambhu’s birth ?<br>
Unknown his power ! For gods celestial,<br>
Do they know of Sambhu’s worth ?</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">28.<br>
“But cease these words,—and, learned Brâhman !<br>
Leave me, and in peace depart !<br>
If small his virtues, great his failings,—<br>
Sambhu still doth fill this heart !”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-149"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="149" data-folio="111" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n148_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 111">[p. 111]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">29.<br>
Uprose the maiden in her anger,<br>
Rose and quickly turned away ;<br>
But the Brâhman held her garment,<br>
Boldly stopped the maiden’s way.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">30.<br>
She turned to chide,—it was her Sambhu,—<br>
Now in glorious form divine !<br>
“Won by love and won by penance,<br>
Gentle Umâ ! I am thine !”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from Kâlidâsa’s Kumâra-sambhava,</em> V.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-37-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-37-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">The Hindus reckon six seasons in the year, viz., Spring, Summer, Rains, Autumn, Dews, and Winter, each season lasting two months.</span></li></ol></section>
