<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-114"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="114" data-folio="76" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n113_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 76">[p. 76]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE LEGEND OF BÂLÂKI.</em></h2><h3 class="sec-h2">1.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">BÂLÂKI, a learned Brâhman,<br>
Proud of knowledge, proud of lore,<br>
Versed he was in many a sâstra,<br>
Travelled many a distant shore.<br>
In the land of Usînara,<br>
And in Matsya he had been,<br>
Panchâla and the Kuru kingdoms,<br>
Videha and the Kâsî seen.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-27-fn-001" href="#sec-27-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-27-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Ajâtasatru, learned monarch,<br>
Ruled in Kâsî’s mighty land,<br>
Unto him repaired the Brâhman,<br>
In his palace rich and grand.<br>
“Blessed be thy rule, O monarch !<br>
O’er this kingdom rich and broad,<br>
I will speak to thee of Brahman,<br>
I will speak to thee of God !”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-115"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="115" data-folio="77" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n114_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 77">[p. 77]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Welcome, welcome, learned priest!”<br>
Ajâtasatru thus replied,—<br>
“For thy holy speech accept<br>
A thousand kine of finest breed!<br>
Every learned Brâhman hies<br>
To Janaka, holy king,—<br>
Welcome thou to Kâsî’s court,<br>
And a Brâhman’s blessings bring!”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“List then, king! to words of wisdom,”—<br>
Proud Bâlâki thus began,—<br>
“Knowest thou the radiant Soul<br>
Dwelleth in the radiant sun?<br>
Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.<br>
“Not so!”—answered thus the king,<br>
“For the sun is mighty glorious,<br>
But is a created thing!”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Knowest thou the lunar crescent,<br>
Shining in the starry sky?<br>
Knowest thou the beauteous Soul,<br>
Dwelleth there serene and high?<br>
Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.<br>
“Not so!”—answered thus the king,<br>
“For the moon is bright and beauteous,<br>
But is a created thing!”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-116"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="116" data-folio="78" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n115_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 78">[p. 78]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Dost thou know the forked lightning,<br>
Flashing through the lurid sky ?<br>
Dost thou know the dreaded Soul,<br>
Dwelleth there, terrific, high ?<br>
Him I worship !”—said Bâlâki.<br>
“Not so !”—answered thus the king,<br>
“For the lightning is terrific,<br>
But is a created thing !”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Dost thou know the deep-voiced thunder,<br>
Pealing through the echoing sky ?<br>
Dost thou know the soul that dwelleth<br>
In that sound, terrific, high ?<br>
Him I worship !”—said Bâlâki.<br>
“Not so !”—answered thus the king,<br>
“For the thunder is terrific,<br>
But is a created thing !”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Long he toiled, the learned Brâhman,<br>
Power Almighty to explain,<br>
Quoted he from holy sâstras,<br>
Argued long, but argued vain !<br>
To his reasons, to his learning,<br>
Ever answered thus the king,<br>
“Mighty wondrous is all nature,<br>
But it is created thing !”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-117"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="117" data-folio="79" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n116_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 79">[p. 79]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Still he toiled, the learned Brâhman,<br>
To explain the God on high,—<br>
Spoke of fire and spoke of ether,<br>
Spoke of water and of sky,<br>
Spoke of shadow and reflection,<br>
Spoke of echo and of sound,<br>
Argued about dreams and slumber,<br>
But solution none he found!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Silently the boastful Brâhman<br>
Bent his head in grief and shame,<br>
Sad he was,—the learned Brâhman,—<br>
To a wiser king he came !<br>
“Thus far,”—said the monarch gently,<br>
“Thus far doth thy wisdom go !”<br>
“Thus far ! wise and learned monarch !<br>
Teach me what you further know !”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">11.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">With the fuel, meek and humble,<br>
Bâlâki as student came,<br>
Seeking knowledge from the monarch,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-27-fn-002" href="#sec-27-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-27-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Great in learning as in fame.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-118"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="118" data-folio="80" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n117_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 80">[p. 80]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“He who made the sun and moon,<br>
And the sky and earth so broad,<br>
He who breathed all Nature forth,—<br>
He is Brahman, He is God!”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from the Kanshîtaki Upanishad.</em></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">the rites laid down by priests, gave a start to healthier speculations, and inquired about the destination of the Soul and the nature of the Supreme Being.
