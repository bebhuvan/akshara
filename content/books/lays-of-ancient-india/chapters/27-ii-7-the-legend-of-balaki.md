<span class="line">BÂLÂKI, a learned Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">Proud of knowledge, proud of lore,</span><br>
<span class="line">Versed he was in many a sâstra,</span><br>
<span class="line">Travelled many a distant shore.</span><br>
<span class="line">In the land of Usînara,</span><br>
<span class="line">And in Matsya he had been,</span><br>
<span class="line">Panchâla and the Kuru kingdoms,</span><br>
<span class="line">Videha and the Kâsî seen.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-27-fn-001" href="#sec-27-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-27-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>
<span class="line">Ajâtasatru, learned monarch,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ruled in Kâsî’s mighty land,</span><br>
<span class="line">Unto him repaired the Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">In his palace rich and grand.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Blessed be thy rule, O monarch!</span><br>
<span class="line">O’er this kingdom rich and broad,</span><br>
<span class="line">I will speak to thee of Brahman,</span><br>
<span class="line">I will speak to thee of God!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-115"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="115" data-folio="77" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n114_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 77">[p. 77]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">3.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Welcome, welcome, learned priest!”</span><br>
<span class="line">Ajâtasatru thus replied,—</span><br>
<span class="line">“For thy holy speech accept</span><br>
<span class="line">A thousand kine of finest breed!</span><br>
<span class="line">Every learned Brâhman hies</span><br>
<span class="line">To Janaka, holy king,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Welcome thou to Kâsî’s court,</span><br>
<span class="line">And a Brâhman’s blessings bring!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">“List then, king! to words of wisdom,”—</span><br>
<span class="line">Proud Bâlâki thus began,—</span><br>
<span class="line">“Knowest thou the radiant Soul</span><br>
<span class="line">Dwelleth in the radiant sun?</span><br>
<span class="line">Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not so!”—answered thus the king,</span><br>
<span class="line">“For the sun is mighty glorious,</span><br>
<span class="line">But is a created thing!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">5.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Knowest thou the lunar crescent,</span><br>
<span class="line">Shining in the starry sky?</span><br>
<span class="line">Knowest thou the beauteous Soul,</span><br>
<span class="line">Dwelleth there serene and high?</span><br>
<span class="line">Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not so!”—answered thus the king,</span><br>
<span class="line">“For the moon is bright and beauteous,</span><br>
<span class="line">But is a created thing!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-116"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="116" data-folio="78" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n115_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 78">[p. 78]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">6.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Dost thou know the forked lightning,</span><br>
<span class="line">Flashing through the lurid sky?</span><br>
<span class="line">Dost thou know the dreaded Soul,</span><br>
<span class="line">Dwelleth there, terrific, high?</span><br>
<span class="line">Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not so!”—answered thus the king,</span><br>
<span class="line">“For the lightning is terrific,</span><br>
<span class="line">But is a created thing!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">7.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Dost thou know the deep-voiced thunder,</span><br>

<span class="line">Dost thou know the soul that dwelleth</span><br>
<span class="line">In that sound, terrific, high?</span><br>
<span class="line">Him I worship!”—said Bâlâki.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not so!”—answered thus the king,</span><br>
<span class="line">“For the thunder is terrific,</span><br>
<span class="line">But is a created thing!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">8.</span><br>

<span class="line">Quoted he from holy sâstras,</span><br>
<span class="line">Argued long, but argued vain!</span><br>
<span class="line">To his reasons, to his learning,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ever answered thus the king,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Mighty wondrous is all nature,</span><br>
<span class="line">But it is created thing!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-117"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="117" data-folio="79" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n116_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 79">[p. 79]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">9.</span><br>
<span class="line">Still he toiled, the learned Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">To explain the God on high,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke of fire and spoke of ether,</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke of water and of sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke of shadow and reflection,</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke of echo and of sound,</span><br>
<span class="line">Argued about dreams and slumber,</span><br>
<span class="line">But solution none he found!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">10.</span><br>
<span class="line">Silently the boastful Brâhman</span><br>
<span class="line">Bent his head in grief and shame,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sad he was,—the learned Brâhman,—</span><br>
<span class="line">To a wiser king he came!</span><br>
<span class="line">“Thus far,”—said the monarch gently,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Thus far doth thy wisdom go!”</span><br>
<span class="line">“Thus far! wise and learned monarch!</span><br>
<span class="line">Teach me what you further know!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">11.</span><br>

<span class="line">Bâlâki as student came,</span><br>
