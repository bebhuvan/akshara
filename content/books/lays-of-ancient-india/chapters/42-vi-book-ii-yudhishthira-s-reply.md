<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-176"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="176" data-folio="138" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n175_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 138">[p. 138]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK II.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>YUDHISHTHIRA'S REPLY.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">LISTENING</span> to her speech so noble,—<br>
Speech with force and sweetness graced,<br>
Bhîma thus with words of wisdom<br>
Virtuous Yudhishthir addressed. (1)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Pleading with her eyes so gentle,<br>
Drupad’s noble daughter speaks<br>
Words unmatched by god of wisdom,—<br>
And my soul in wonder wakes!<br>
Easy steps can lead to knowledge,<br>
As the <em>Ghat</em> leads to the stream ;<br>
Few are those in graceful accents<br>
Who those easy steps can frame !<br>
Like some potent drug her utterance<br>
Force and mighty vigour blends ;<br>
Though displeasing to the weakly,<br>
Still it serveth mighty ends !<br>
May her words of noble import<br>
Please thee, great and virtuous lord !<br>
Wise men cull each word of wisdom,<br>
Do not reck who speaks that word.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-177"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="177" data-folio="139" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n176_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 139">[p. 139]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Trained in all the fourfold science<br>
Is thy lofty mind, great sire!<br>
Wherefore then it sinks in error<br>
Like a tusker in the mire? (6)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Fallen to this state deploréd,<br>
Mighty sovereign! can it be?<br>
And thy power, by gods admiréd,<br>
Can it, monarch! sink with thee?<br>
We may brook a foeman’s prowess<br>
While his ruin we devise;<br>
But a foeman’s fall is painful<br>
If it leads to future rise!<br>
Striving still their foes to conquer,<br>
And to seize the victor’s prize,<br>
Men of deeds and mighty valour<br>
Fate’s reverses still despise!<br>
Who in danger and misfortune<br>
From great deeds of daring turn;<br>
Fortune shuns such nerveless monarchs,<br>
Moved by hatred, moved by scorn!<br>
Who in danger and misfortune<br>
With their daring onward press,<br>
Fortune helps them in the struggle,<br>
Men their mighty valour bless!<br>
Righteous laws and rules of duty,<br>
They can lead to wealth and state;<br>
But they wait on earnest effort,<br>
As we still depend on fate.<br>
Men of deeds and men of honour,<br>
Bent on lofty aims with pride,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-178"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="178" data-folio="140" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n177_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 140">[p. 140]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Still rely on man-like effort,<br>
Death and danger turn aside !<br>
Men of feeble hearts surrender<br>
To the ills which fortune brings ;<br>
Lost their glory, lost their future !<br>
Lost their pride and state of kings ! (14)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Then spurn this apathy and weakness,<br>
To our fame a fatal bar ;<br>
On our effort, not vain sorrow,<br>
Ever waits fair fortune’s star !<br>
Waitest thou for term of treaty ?<br>
Will the wicked wily foe,<br>
Feasting long on royal splendour,<br>
Peacefully their state forego ?<br>
Waitest thou to get from foemen<br>
As a gift thy kingdom then ?<br>
Woe unto our might and prowess,<br>
Shame on us, O king of men !<br>
Proud, the monarch of the forest<br>
Feeds on tuskers by him slayed !<br>
Mighty chiefs who quell this wide earth<br>
Seek not gifts and foemen’s aid !<br>
They surrender life that fleeteth<br>
For undying glory great ;<br>
Fortune, fickle as the lightning,<br>
Will unchanging on them wait !<br>
Men will spurn a heap of ashes,<br>
Not the bright and flaming fire !<br>
Hence the proud their life surrender,<br>
Not their glory, not their ire !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-179"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="179" data-folio="141" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n178_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 141">[p. 141]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Wherefore roars the forest-monarch,<br>
Till his roar the clouds repeat ?<br>
His proud soul will brook no rival,<br>
Mighty hearts know no defeat !<br>
Set thy heart on glory, brother !<br>
Spurn this slothful life of shame,<br>
Sure, from our inglorious weakness<br>
Springs our foeman's mighty fame !<br>
Let thy brothers, strong as tuskers,<br>
In the dubious battle throng,<br>
Who will face their mighty valour,<br>
Who will face their prowess strong ?<br>
And the fire the foes have lighted,—<br>
Making drear thy righteous life,—<br>
Let the tears of foemen's widows<br>
Quench that great, that vengeful grief !” (24)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Bhîma, like a noble tusker,<br>
Spoke in wrath and mighty ire ;<br>
But the righteous Yudhishthira<br>
Strove to quench the flaming fire. (25)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Wise thy reasons, noble brother,<br>
Blameless, mighty as thy might !<br>
In thy words thy mind reflected<br>
Shines as on a mirror bright !<br>
Strong thy sense, unveiled by jargon,<br>
Import deep yet manifest !<br>
And thy purpose put forth nobly,<br>
And by native vigour graced !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-180"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="180" data-folio="142" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n179_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 142">[p. 142]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Reasons urged with force inviolate,<br>
Depth of lore unsoiled by art !<br>
Who can speak so well and nobly<br>
But a chief of noble heart ?<br>
But insatiate yet my bosom<br>
Seeks for reason’s holy light ;<br>
And thy wealth of precepts, brother,<br>
Fails to teach me what is right ! (29)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Thoughtless action leads to evil,<br>
And our dangers <em>we</em> create ;<br>
Fortune ever waits on wisdom,<br>
On the wise her favours wait !<br>
Sprinkle Wisdom’s drops that nourish<br>
On our Duty’s fruitful seed,<br>
And as autumn brings its harvests,<br>
Duty will to glory lead !<br>
Sacred lore bedecks a mortal,<br>
Righteous peace befits our lore ;<br>
Prowess dwells in righteous conduct,<br>
Leads to glory evermore !<br>
On the wise man’s path of duty,<br>
When doubt’s gloomy shadows lower,<br>
Sacred lore then sheds a radiance<br>
Like a lamp of wondrous power !<br>
Dost thou cherish lofty purpose ?<br>
Righteously let it be done !<br>
Dangers then will bring no sorrow,<br>
Failure will be glory won !<br>
Dost thou sigh for mighty conquests ?<br>
Angry passions lay aside !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-181"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="181" data-folio="143" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n180_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 143">[p. 143]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Blamelessly thy ends pursue,<br>
And by blameless means abide !<br>
Dost thou seek to rise in glory ?<br>
Quell by wisdom passion’s gloom ;<br>
As the sun quells impure darkness<br>
Ere he rises in his bloom !<br>
Till thou quellest impure passions,<br>
Vain thy prowess, vain thy might,<br>
Where is bright moon’s silver glory<br>
In the dark and moonless night ?<br>
Act with pride and strength when needed,<br>
Or thy gentleness display ;<br>
Lord of passions,—rule the wide earth,<br>
Like the glorious orb of day !<br>
But if thou be’st passions’ minion,<br>
Fame nor glory wilt thou find,—<br>
Fortune, fleeting as the white clouds,<br>
Favours not the inconstant mind. (39)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Wherefore, brother, doth thy great heart<br>
This untimely wrath display ?<br>
Deep as the ocean is the bosom,<br>
Strong and firm be reason’s sway !<br>
Men who, versed in noble learning,<br>
Fail their passions to repress,—<br>
They, my brother, to Dame Fortune,<br>
Give a name for fickleness !<br>
Times nor means are ready, brother,<br>
Let not thy untimely wrath<br>
Still inflame thee, like the vulgar,<br>
Nor mislead from duty’s path !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-182"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="182" data-folio="144" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n181_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 144">[p. 144]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Patience brightens up our future,<br>
Crowns our efforts with success,<br>
Moves obstruction from our path,<br>
Leads us unto happiness! (43)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“The mighty chiefs of Yadu’s race,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-42-fn-001" href="#sec-42-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-42-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Who yield the foremost place to none,<br>
Will not in their love forsake us,<br>
Will not bow to Suyodhan.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-42-fn-002" href="#sec-42-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-42-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup><br>
Other friends and loving kinsmen<br>
Follow as the Yadus lead ;<br>
Not for love they serve Suyodhan,<br>
But to serve their present need.<br>
If against our plighted treaty<br>
Now we fall upon the foe,<br>
All the chiefs will round him gather,<br>
Like the flowers at morning’s glow.<br>
Grant them time, and all the chieftains<br>
Will the proud Suyodhan leave,<br>
Chiefs will never brook those insults,<br>
Which his subjects often grieve,<br>
For the pride of haughty monarchs,<br>
Scarcely trained in virtue’s path,<br>
Ill concealed by sense of duty,<br>
Oft breaks out in native wrath !<br>
And the king with pride inflated<br>
Ever stoops to sin and folly ;<br>
Shuns the righteous path of wisdom<br>
As we shun the path unholy. (49)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-183"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="183" data-folio="145" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n182_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 145">[p. 145]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“A people's discontent will shake<br>
A mighty monarch's power and throne,<br>
Till like a tree by tempest moved,<br>
With ease his rule is overthrown.<br>
For people's discontent, though small,<br>
Doth wreck a mighty monarch's rule,<br>
As far-extending woods are burnt<br>
By sparks which from a dead branch spring.<br>
Therefore, brother, we endure<br>
A haughty foeman's sudden rise;<br>
His haughtiness will sap his prowess,<br>
And his fall is like his rise!<br>
By pride estranged, he soon is left<br>
By mighty chiefs and men of rank;<br>
Then comes the foe and overwhelms him<br>
As the waves the loosened bank!” (53)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus spake the king unto his brother,—<br>
Wise and peaceful counsel lent;<br>
When lo! approached,—like peace incarnate,—<br>
Holy Vyâsa,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-42-fn-003" href="#sec-42-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-42-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup>—mighty saint!<br>
His gentle look imparting love<br>
To every being animate;<br>
His gentle visage softly beaming<br>
With a sin-destroying might!<br>
Destroyer of all mortal sins,<br>
The source of every holy rite,<br>
The saint appeared, serene and calm,<br>
Like virtue in its native might!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-184"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="184" data-folio="146" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n183_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 146">[p. 146]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Attired in wild and ruddy barks,<br>
The king respectful stood, and shone,<br>
As on Sumeru’s lofty peaks<br>
Doth shine the ruddy rising sun !<br>
And when was done obeisance meet,<br>
The king resumed his wonted place,<br>
And shone as shines our sacred learning,<br>
Graced by righteous holy peace ! (58)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">His beauteous lips bedecked with beaming smile,<br>
The monarch sat before the saint awhile,<br>
And shone in splendour, like the full moon bright,<br>
With Brihaspati in conjunction sweet !
