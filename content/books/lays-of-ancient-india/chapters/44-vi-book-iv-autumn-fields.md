<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-194"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="194" data-folio="156" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n193_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 156">[p. 156]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK IV.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>AUTUMN FIELDS.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">Now Arjun came to peopled lands,</span><br>
<span class="line">All fresh and blooming as a bride;</span><br>
<span class="line">The tuneful birds flew in the sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">The crops were rich in golden pride!</span><br>
<span class="line">And autumn’s charms, before him spread,</span><br>
<span class="line">A scene of beauty seemed to make;</span><br>
<span class="line">The corn that bent with golden ears,</span><br>
<span class="line">The winding path, the lotus lake!</span><br>
<span class="line">And fish whose glancing movements stole</span><br>
<span class="line">The beauty of a woman’s glance;</span><br>
<span class="line">And lakes whose lotus blossoms seemed</span><br>
<span class="line">Like eyes uplifted in a trance!</span><br>
<span class="line">And stretching fields of growing rice</span><br>
<span class="line">Still standing on a watery sheet,</span><br>
<span class="line">Where oft the lily raised its head,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Beauteous things harmonious met!</span><br>
<span class="line">So still the water, pollen decked,</span><br>
<span class="line">On land the lilies seemed to grow,</span><br>
<span class="line">Until some glancing fish disturbed</span><br>
<span class="line">The sweet illusion of the show!</span><br>
<span class="line">And Arjun saw the beauteous sands,</span><br>
<span class="line">All pure and white as unstained sheet,</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-195" data-page="195" data-folio="157" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n194_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 157">[p. 157]</button>And marked with waving lines by water,</span><br>
<span class="line">Receding in the autumn’s heat! (6)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">The rustic maid upon her locks</span><br>
<span class="line">The scarlet blossom placed with pride;</span><br>
<span class="line">Her eyebrows caught the pollen dust,</span><br>
<span class="line">Her bright red lips its colour vied!</span><br>
<span class="line">And bright she stood in morning’s light,</span><br>
<span class="line">And wore a lotus on her breast;</span><br>
<span class="line">Her skin was moist with honest toil,</span><br>
<span class="line">And fragrant with the lotus dust!</span><br>
<span class="line">And on her ears she hung two lilies,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sweet as glances from her eye;</span><br>
<span class="line">And Arjun in her saw the charms</span><br>
<span class="line">Of autumn in their full display! (9)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">The lowing herds returned from fields,</span><br>
<span class="line">Where they had grazed the previous night;</span><br>
<span class="line">Their eagerness to meet their calves,</span><br>
<span class="line">Their streaming udders pleased his sight!</span><br>
<span class="line">Fed in autumn’s pastures rich,</span><br>
<span class="line">The bull victorious in the fight,</span><br>
<span class="line">Bellowing, breaking banks of rivers,</span><br>
<span class="line">Seemed,—incarnate form of might!</span><br>
<span class="line">Slowly from the banks of rivers</span><br>
<span class="line">Herds of snow-white cows withdrew,</span><br>
<span class="line">As if the beauteous silver streams</span><br>
<span class="line">White garments from their persons drew.</span><br>
<span class="line">With brother’s love their cattle tending,</span><br>
<span class="line">In woods a home’s affections bearing,</span><br>
<span class="line">The cow-herds lived among the cattle,</span><br>
<span class="line">All their simple habits sharing! (13)</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-196" data-page="196" data-folio="158" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n195_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 158">[p. 158]</button>The village maiden churned the milk,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Her face was like the lotus fair,</span><br>
<span class="line">Her teeth were like its beauteous leaves,</span><br>
<span class="line">Like restless bees oft moved her hair!</span><br>
<span class="line">She held her breath, her red lips moved,</span><br>
<span class="line">And like a creeper soft she stood,</span><br>
<span class="line">And gently moved her beauteous frame</span><br>
<span class="line">As with her hands she turned the rod!</span><br>
<span class="line">A deep sound from the vessel rose,</span><br>
<span class="line">Still as the churning rod went round;</span><br>
<span class="line">The startled pea-hen in the yard</span><br>
<span class="line">Half thought it was the thunder’s sound!</span><br>
<span class="line">With toil her heaving bosom throbbed,</span><br>
<span class="line">And languid was her eye’s soft glance,</span><br>
<span class="line">And thus she vied in rustic beauty</span><br>
<span class="line">Courtly maidens at a dance! (17)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">He went by paths no longer winding,</span><br>
<span class="line">Through the crops by cattle eaten;</span><br>
<span class="line">The thickened mud was marked by cart wheels,</span><br>
<span class="line">And by steps the path was beaten.</span><br>
<span class="line">Beauteous as a saint’s retreat</span><br>
<span class="line">He saw the rustic homesteads smile,</span><br>
<span class="line">Where, simple in their thoughts and ways,</span><br>
<span class="line">The rustics lived by honest toil.</span><br>
<span class="line">Thus autumn’s varied charms to scan,</span><br>
<span class="line">With gladdened eyes pleased Arjun sought;</span><br>
<span class="line">With gladdened heart the Yaksha spoke,</span><br>
<span class="line">Reading his companion’s thought. (20)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">“This cloudless time with crystal waters,</span><br>
<span class="line">Comes the tiller’s toil to bless;</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-197" data-page="197" data-folio="159" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n196_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 159">[p. 159]</button>Like a happy life’s fruition,</span><br>
<span class="line">May it bring to thee success!</span><br>
<span class="line">The corn assumes its golden hue,</span><br>
<span class="line">The streams are clear, the earth is dry;</span><br>
<span class="line">From charms the rainy time displays,</span><br>
<span class="line">To autumn’s fresher charms we fly!</span><br>
<span class="line">What though no white cranes deck the sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">Nor are the skies with rainbows graced;</span><br>
<span class="line">The autumn sky hath beauty rare,</span><br>
<span class="line">And beauty unadorned is best!</span><br>
<span class="line">What though the god of rains is gone,</span><br>
<span class="line">Nor lightnings deck the clouds now pale;</span><br>
<span class="line">Like sorrowing wives the autumn clouds</span><br>
<span class="line">In paler grace are lovely still!</span><br>
<span class="line">What though the peacock’s loveless notes <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-44-fn-001" href="#sec-44-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-44-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></span><br>
<span class="line">Are loud and harsh, nor please the ear;</span><br>
<span class="line">The loving notes of autumn songsters</span><br>
<span class="line">To the lover’s heart are dear!</span><br>
<span class="line">What golden crops,—rich in their beauty,</span><br>
<span class="line">With their load of ears bent low!</span><br>
<span class="line">They bend as if to feel the fragrance</span><br>
<span class="line">Of the flowers that spring below!</span><br>
<span class="line">What beauteous lakes,—green with their plants,</span><br>
<span class="line">And scarlet with the lotus bloom,</span><br>
<span class="line">And golden with the ripened corn,</span><br>
<span class="line">Like molten rainbow varied seem!</span><br>
<span class="line">What nymph-like groves,—they smile in flowers,</span><br>
<span class="line">And in the blue buds ope their eyes,</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-198" data-page="198" data-folio="160" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n197_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 160">[p. 160]</button>And lotus dust by zephyrs driven</span><br>
<span class="line">Clothe them in a fairy dress!</span><br>
<span class="line">What snow-white clouds make cool the day,</span><br>
<span class="line">In balmy skies no lightning lowers;</span><br>
<span class="line">How cool the breeze with watery spray,</span><br>
<span class="line">How sweet with fragrance from the flowers!</span><br>
<span class="line">Above, the happy white-winged birds</span><br>
<span class="line">Are flying, twittering as they fly!</span><br>
<span class="line">Or are the quarters of the heavens</span><br>
<span class="line">Holding converse in the sky? (30)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">“From the rich autumnal pastures,</span><br>
<span class="line">Eager to their village home,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sweet milk from their udders streaming,</span><br>
<span class="line">How the cows to young ones come!</span><br>
<span class="line">Noble, gentle, tender beings,</span><br>
<span class="line">Nourishers of the humankind!</span><br>
<span class="line">How the mothers join their young ones,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Like sweet hymns to offerings joined!</span><br>
<span class="line">Blithe the song the milkmaid singeth,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sweet as strains the Kokils weave!</span><br>
<span class="line">How entranced yon herd of antlers,</span><br>
<span class="line">Listen,—and their pastures leave!</span><br>
<span class="line">In the lakes the reeds are bending,</span><br>
<span class="line">As to court yon lotus fair!</span><br>
<span class="line">But disdainful is that beauty,</span><br>

<span class="line">Sweet the breeze with lotus pollen,</span><br>

<span class="line">Their beaks in glorious crimson shine;</span><br>

<span class="line">Is it a checkered rainbow’s line?”</span><br>
<span class="line">While thus he spoke, before him stood,</span><br>
<span class="line">Concealing the bright god of day,</span><br>
