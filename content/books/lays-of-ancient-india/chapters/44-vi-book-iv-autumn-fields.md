<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-194"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="194" data-folio="156" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n193_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 156">[p. 156]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK IV.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>AUTUMN FIELDS.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Now Arjun came to peopled lands,<br>
All fresh and blooming as a bride ;<br>
The tuneful birds flew in the sky,<br>
The crops were rich in golden pride !<br>
And autumn’s charms, before him spread,<br>
A scene of beauty seemed to make ;<br>
The corn that bent with golden ears,<br>
The winding path, the lotus lake !<br>
And fish whose glancing movements stole<br>
The beauty of a woman’s glance ;<br>
And lakes whose lotus blossoms seemed<br>
Like eyes uplifted in a trance !<br>
And stretching fields of growing rice<br>
Still standing on a watery sheet,<br>
Where oft the lily raised its head,—<br>
Beauteous things harmonious met !<br>
So still the water, pollen decked,<br>
On land the lilies seemed to grow,<br>
Until some glancing fish disturbed<br>
The sweet illusion of the show !<br>
And Arjun saw the beauteous sands,<br>
All pure and white as unstained sheet,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-195"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="195" data-folio="157" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n194_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 157">[p. 157]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And marked with waving lines by water,<br>
Receding in the autumn's heat ! (6)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The rustic maid upon her locks<br>
The scarlet blossom placed with pride ;<br>
Her eyebrows caught the pollen dust,<br>
Her bright red lips its colour vied !<br>
And bright she stood in morning's light,<br>
And wore a lotus on her breast ;<br>
Her skin was moist with honest toil,<br>
And fragrant with the lotus dust !<br>
And on her ears she hung two lilies,<br>
Sweet as glances from her eye ;<br>
And Arjun in her saw the charms<br>
Of autumn in their full display ! (9)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The lowing herds returned from fields,<br>
Where they had grazed the previous night ;<br>
Their eagerness to meet their calves,<br>
Their streaming udders pleased his sight !<br>
Fed in autumn's pastures rich,<br>
The bull victorious in the fight,<br>
Bellowing, breaking banks of rivers,<br>
Seemed,—incarnate form of might !<br>
Slowly from the banks of rivers<br>
Herds of snow-white cows withdrew,<br>
As if the beauteous silver streams<br>
White garments from their persons drew.<br>
With brother's love their cattle tending,<br>
In woods a home's affections bearing,<br>
The cow-herds lived among the cattle,<br>
All their simple habits sharing ! (13)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-196"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="196" data-folio="158" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n195_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 158">[p. 158]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The village maiden churned the milk,—<br>
Her face was like the lotus fair,<br>
Her teeth were like its beauteous leaves,<br>
Like restless bees oft moved her hair !<br>
She held her breath, her red lips moved,<br>
And like a creeper soft she stood,<br>
And gently moved her beauteous frame<br>
As with her hands she turned the rod !<br>
A deep sound from the vessel rose,<br>
Still as the churning rod went round ;<br>
The startled pea-hen in the yard<br>
Half thought it was the thunder’s sound !<br>
With toil her heaving bosom throbbed,<br>
And languid was her eye’s soft glance,<br>
And thus she vied in rustic beauty<br>
Courtly maidens at a dance ! (17)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">He went by paths no longer winding,<br>
Through the crops by cattle eaten ;<br>
The thickened mud was marked by cart wheels,<br>
And by steps the path was beaten.<br>
Beauteous as a saint’s retreat<br>
He saw the rustic homesteads smile,<br>
Where, simple in their thoughts and ways,<br>
The rustics lived by honest toil.<br>
Thus autumn’s varied charms to scan,<br>
With gladdened eyes pleased Arjun sought ;<br>
With gladdened heart the Yaksha spoke,<br>
Reading his companion’s thought. (20)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“This cloudless time with crystal waters,<br>
Comes the tiller’s toil to bless ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-197"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="197" data-folio="159" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n196_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 159">[p. 159]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Like a happy life’s fruition,<br>
May it bring to thee success !<br>
The corn assumes its golden hue,<br>
The streams are clear, the earth is dry ;<br>
From charms the rainy time displays,<br>
To autumn’s fresher charms we fly !<br>
What though no white cranes deck the sky,<br>
Nor are the skies with rainbows graced ;<br>
The autumn sky hath beauty rare,<br>
And beauty unadorned is best !<br>
What though the god of rains is gone,<br>
Nor lightnings deck the clouds now pale ;<br>
Like sorrowing wives the autumn clouds<br>
In paler grace are lovely still !<br>
What though the peacock’s loveless notes <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-44-fn-001" href="#sec-44-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-44-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Are loud and harsh, nor please the ear ;<br>
The loving notes of autumn songsters<br>
To the lover’s heart are dear !<br>
What golden crops,—rich in their beauty,<br>
With their load of ears bent low !<br>
They bend as if to feel the fragrance<br>
Of the flowers that spring below !<br>
What beauteous lakes,—green with their plants,<br>
And scarlet with the lotus bloom,<br>
And golden with the ripened corn,<br>
Like molten rainbow varied seem !<br>
What nymph-like groves,—they smile in flowers,<br>
And in the blue buds ope their eyes,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-198"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="198" data-folio="160" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n197_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 160">[p. 160]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And lotus dust by zephyrs driven<br>
Clothe them in a fairy dress !<br>
What snow-white clouds make cool the day,<br>
In balmy skies no lightning lowers ;<br>
How cool the breeze with watery spray,<br>
How sweet with fragrance from the flowers !<br>
Above, the happy white-winged birds<br>
Are flying, twittering as they fly !<br>
Or are the quarters of the heavens<br>
Holding converse in the sky ? (30)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ From the rich autumnal pastures,<br>
Eager to their village home,<br>
Sweet milk from their udders streaming,<br>
How the cows to young ones come !<br>
Noble, gentle, tender beings,<br>
Nourishers of the humankind !<br>
How the mothers join their young ones,—<br>
Like sweet hymns to offerings joined !<br>
Blithe the song the milkmaid singeth,<br>
Sweet as strains the Kokils weave !<br>
How entranced yon herd of antlers,<br>
Listen,—and their pastures leave !<br>
In the lakes the reeds are bending,<br>
As to court yon lotus fair !<br>
But disdainful is that beauty,<br>
Pale the reeds with chill despair !<br>
Sweet the breeze with lotus pollen,<br>
Saturate with cooling spray ;<br>
And like giddy thoughtless men<br>
Yon wind-blown bee has lost his way !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-199"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="199" data-folio="161" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n198_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 161">[p. 161]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">How beauteous green yon flight of birds,<br>
Their beaks in glorious crimson shine;<br>
How golden is the corn they bear,<br>
Is it a checkered rainbow’s line ?”<br>
While thus he spoke, before him stood,<br>
Concealing the bright god of day,<br>
Like fleecy clouds in masses piled,<br>
The towering range of Himalay ! (37)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Darksome forests in his valleys spread,<br>
But snow eternal graced his lofty head !—<br>
