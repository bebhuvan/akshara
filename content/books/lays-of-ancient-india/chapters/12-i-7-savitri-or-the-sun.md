<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-67"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="67" data-folio="29" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n66_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 29">[p. 29]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>SAVITRI, OR THE SUN.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">“Sûrya and Savitri are the most common names of the sun in the Rig Veda, the former word answering to the Greek Helios, the Latin Sol, and the Iranian Khorshed. Commentators draw a distinction between Savitri, the rising or the unrisen sun, and Sûrya, the bright sun of the day.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 84.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The following three verses are taken from a somewhat long hymn. The first of these verses is the famous <em>Gayatrî</em>, the morning prayer of the Brâhmans. It has a deep significance, and pious worshippers in invoking the sun invoke the Higher Power whose radiance and glory are outwardly manifested in the sun.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">1.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Savitri</span>! effulgent, bright!<br>
Who fosters every pious rite,<br>
Savitri! whose ruddy rays<br>
Invite our holy hymns and lays,<br>
With humble hearts we contemplate his rays,<br>
And faintly see our God in his bright face!</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Savitri will bring us food,<br>
And Bhaga, bounteous and good.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-12-fn-001" href="#sec-12-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-12-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-68"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="68" data-folio="30" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n67_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 30">[p. 30]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Savitri will hear our lays,<br>
And Bhaga listens to our praise.<br>
The radiant gods will bring us food and wealth,<br>
And shield us from all harm, and grant us health.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Let our pious men of lore<br>
In our rites libations pour,<br>
Let them in their wisdom raise<br>
Holy hymns and songs of praise<br>
For Savitri ! Our god, our friend thou art !<br>
And we will greet thee with a pious heart !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, III. 62.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-12-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-12-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">“There is much confusion in the Rig Veda as to who the Âdityas are—the sons of this Celestial Light (Aditi). In II. 27, Aryaman and Bhaga and Daksha and Ansa are named, besides Varuna and Mitra. . . . When in course of time the year was divided into twelve months, the number of the Âdityas was fixed at twelve, and they were the suns of the twelve months.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. pp. 83, 84.</span></li></ol></section>
