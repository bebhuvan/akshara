<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-243"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="243" data-folio="205" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n242_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 205">[p. 205]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK X.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>THE BOAR HUNT.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">The</span> Boar with wondrous strength endowed<br>
In its deep tusks and mighty jaw,—<br>
As if to tear the firm set rocks,—<br>
With glaring eyes great Arjun saw !<br>
“ Uprooting trees with his great tusks,<br>
And piercing rocks with dreadful might,<br>
He comes at me,”—thus Arjun thought,—<br>
“ As if to challenge me to fight !<br>
Beasts of prey forget their strife<br>
In this my peaceful hermitage,<br>
Wherefore then on death intent<br>
Intrudes this wild boar in its rage ?<br>
Was he my foe in previous life ?<br>
Does cherished hatred speed him still ?<br>
Despising every living creature<br>
Death to me he seeks to deal !<br>
Much my doubting heart misgives,—<br>
No beast,—he is a deadly foe !<br>
Spontaneously a friend or foe<br>
The instincts of our nature know ! ” (5)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Pondering thus he took his bow,<br>
Fit emblem of his matchless might,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-244"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="244" data-folio="206" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n243_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 206">[p. 206]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And on it quickly placed his arrow,<br>
Ever true in many a fight!<br>
Admiring Siva marked his mien,<br>
His circling bow, his stately height,—<br>
“The mortal stands, as once I stood,<br>
And slew Tripurâ in my might!”<br>
And Siva bent his sounding bow,<br>
The mountain sank beneath his tread!<br>
And the snake that formed the bowstring<br>
Shot forth sparks, all flaming red!<br>
Frightened tuskers fled the sound<br>
Of Siva’s bow, terrific, high,<br>
As lightning from rain-cloud falls,<br>
The arrow shot across the sky!<br>
The Boar’s tough hide, like seasoned Tamâl,<br>
Pierced the dart like flakes of snow!<br>
Unchecked, in earth it disappeared<br>
As sharks in water sink below!<br>
That instant as the lightning’s radiance<br>
Shortening distance, as by spell,<br>
And quick as thought, great Arjun’s arrow<br>
On the mighty wild boar fell!<br>
Red in hot blood fell the Boar,<br>
Tearing rocks in rage and pride;<br>
Glared at Arjun in its anger,<br>
Groaned in agony, and died. (12)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Though rich in darts, great Arjun sought<br>
The shaft the mighty prize had won;<br>
For noble hearts are grateful ever,<br>
And remember service done!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-245"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="245" data-folio="207" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n244_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 207">[p. 207]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">He saw a hunter near the beast,<br>
A bow across his ample chest,—<br>
A messenger who came to tell<br>
His mighty Leader’s high behest! (14)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Thy gentle mien thy worth proclaims,<br>
These rites proclaim thy holy peace,<br>
And aye, thy lofty presence speaketh<br>
Of a glorious god-like race!<br>
Thy glory shames the sun’s bright ray,<br>
Thy mighty power is known to men,<br>
Then wherefore takest thou this dart<br>
With which our Chief this boar hath slain?<br>
Or perchance our Leader’s arrow<br>
To thy arrow is akin,<br>
And unconscious thou proceedest,<br>
Erring, into paths of sin!<br>
But not alone ’tis base to steal<br>
Another’s arrow from the field,<br>
A chief like thee might blush to send<br>
His shaft on game by others killed!<br>
Or if, in sooth, thou seek’st this dart,<br>
Come, ask our Leader openly;<br>
Mighty Ruler!—he will yield<br>
His dart to gain a friend like thee!<br>
Unto our kind and gracious Chief<br>
A humble prayer is never lost,<br>
He knows the pain the lowly suffer,<br>
When their humble prayer is crossed.<br>
Or hast thou with a Brâhman’s folly<br>
On dead beast thy arrow driven?</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-246"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="246" data-folio="208" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n245_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 208">[p. 208]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Ignorance doth cover sin,<br>
Our Chief thy folly hath forgiven !<br>
Beshrew such folly, honest friend !<br>
Depart in peace, nor sin again,<br>
For who will pardon frequent sins,—<br>
Incessant winds stir up the main !<br>
My Chief forgives this foolish act,<br>
Nor seeks to cause a hermit pain ;<br>
Come, yield this arrow to our Lord,<br>
And from him wealth and grace obtain !<br>
Yonder, by that lofty tree,<br>
Stands he,—our Chief, thou mark’st him plain,<br>
Obey his will, his favours seek,<br>
And thou shalt all thy wish obtain !” (24)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Like a rock by surges beaten,<br>
Angered by this bitter taunt,<br>
Arjun still thus gently answered,<br>
Great in patience and restraint ! (25)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Well skilled in words ! Why seek’st thou not<br>
To turn thy master from this strife ?<br>
Knowest thou not that menials perish<br>
When their chief surrenders life ?<br>
Thy Leader’s arrow may have gone<br>
Among yon rugged rocks astray ;—<br>
Nor insult thus an anchorite,<br>
For pride to ruin leads the way !<br>
Many a bright and piercing arrow<br>
In my ample quiver lies ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-247"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="247" data-folio="209" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n246_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 209">[p. 209]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">I value not celestial darts,<br>
And scarce a mountaineer’s would prize !<br>
Beasts range the wood, to none belong,<br>
Whoever kills them takes the game ;<br>
Then let thy Chief his pride forego,<br>
And peacefully forego his claim.<br>
You counselled me to beg the dart<br>
As favour from your Chief in peace ;<br>
To win by prowess, not to ask,<br>
Is the proud custom of my race.<br>
You counselled friendship ;—Kshatriya I,—<br>
And he a hunter in this range !<br>
Mighty tuskers scarce would seek<br>
In skulking jackals friendship strange !<br>
You counselled me to court his grace,<br>
Presumptuous words !—I pardon free ;<br>
Then let thy Chief give up this shaft,<br>
Nor seek with taunts to anger me !” (32)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thus unto the hunter wild<br>
Arjun spoke in angry mood,<br>
And the forest messenger<br>
Went where mighty Siva stood !<br>
And the ample chested Chief,<br>
With the bow across his breast,<br>
Stood against the lofty sky,<br>
Seemed the Lord of all the host !<br>
Stood before him warlike Arjun,<br>
Pale with penances, but proud,<br>
Swelling with a mighty passion,<br>
Like a fire in smoky shroud !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-248"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="248" data-folio="210" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n247_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 210">[p. 210]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Resting on his mighty bow,<br>
Calm in strength, and patiently,<br>
Mighty in his matchless power,<br>
Like the calm inviolate sea!<br>
With a more than mortal form,<br>
Dark in hue, of stately size,<br>
Like the world-protecting Vishnu,<br>
Hidden in a mortal's guise! (37)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Saintly in his penance and his rite,<br>
Glorious in his prowess and his might!<br>
To him the Lord of arméd hunters came,<br>
As comes the dark cloud with the lightning's gleam! (38)</div></div>
