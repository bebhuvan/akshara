<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-169"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="169" data-folio="131" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n168_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 131">[p. 131]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK I.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>DRAUPADÎ’S REMONSTRANCE.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Sent</span> in hermit’s guise to spy<br>
How Kuru’s monarch ruled his men,<br>
A forester, his task performed,<br>
Yudhishthir met in Dvaita’s glen.<br>
He bowed and spoke, nor pained his bosom<br>
Of the foeman’s rule to tell,<br>
Nor seek those men with accents false<br>
To please our ear who wish us well.<br>
On vengeful thoughts intent, the monarch<br>
Bade the spy his tale unfold ;<br>
And sweet in language, rich in import,<br>
Clear in sense, the words he told. (3)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Great monarchs see but through their spies,<br>
And spies should ne’er deceive their lord ;<br>
Forgive my speech if plain or pleasing,<br>
Seldom true is honied word !<br>
False friend ! who teaches not his king,<br>
False king ! who hears not what they tell ;<br>
Where king and friends in truth agree,<br>
Fortune’s favours love to dwell !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-170"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="170" data-folio="132" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n169_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 132">[p. 132]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">We foresters are simple men,<br>
And hard a king’s designs to trace ;<br>
And if thy foeman’s plans I ken,<br>
’Tis by thy kindness, by thy grace ! (6)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Seated on his throne he trembles<br>
At thee,—dwelling in the wood !<br>
The realm he won by trick of dice<br>
He rules by righteous laws and good.<br>
Deep in wiles, he would surpass thee<br>
In his fame for righteousness ;<br>
Better far to war with true men<br>
Than to consort with the base !<br>
His passions veiled, by Manu’s laws<br>
He seeks the virtuous path to tread ;<br>
By night and day his task dividing,<br>
Seeks a righteous fame to spread.<br>
He treats his menials as his friends,<br>
And as his kinsmen friends withal ;<br>
Veiling his wrath he seeks to prove<br>
How conquering love rules over all !<br>
With love impartial, equal care,<br>
He cultivates all virtues well ;<br>
And by his worth all varied virtues<br>
In harmonious concert dwell !<br>
His kindness never lacks in gifts,<br>
His gifts with courteous manners flow,<br>
And his courtesy and his favours<br>
Only men of virtue know.<br>
Not for wealth and not in anger,<br>
Ever seeking righteous cause,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-171"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="171" data-folio="133" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n170_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 133">[p. 133]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">On his foe or on his children<br>
Visits sins with equal laws.<br>
Placing trusty guards around him,<br>
Ever wears a fearless mien ;<br>
And wealth bestowed at sacrifices<br>
Speaks his gratitude to men.<br>
And by careful thought devised,<br>
His plans, with steady toil pursued,<br>
Open out a prosperous future,<br>
And conjointly lead to good ! (15)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Cars and horses of great chieftains<br>
Throng his palace court around ;<br>
Tuskers sent by mighty monarchs<br>
With their perfume<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-41-fn-001" href="#sec-41-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-41-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> moist the ground.<br>
And Kuru’s lands are rich in harvests<br>
Ripening without tiller’s toil ;<br>
Ask no rain, since Kuru’s monarch<br>
Showers his blessings on the soil.<br>
His bounteous and his peaceful rule<br>
To plenty and to wealth give birth ;<br>
Attracted by his godlike worth<br>
Spontaneous yields her wealth the earth.<br>
Mighty chiefs, by wealth attracted,<br>
Warriors famed in many a strife,<br>
Against their monarch never muster,<br>
Serve his cause even with their life.<br>
His task performed, by trusty spies<br>
He learns the plans of other kings ;</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-172"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="172" data-folio="134" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n171_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 134">[p. 134]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><em>His</em> secret plan is only known,<br>
Like Heaven’s designs, by fruits it brings !<br>
His bow is never strung nor raised,<br>
Nor doth a frown his face o’erspread ;<br>
His kindly rule the chieftains cherish<br>
Like a garland on their head !<br>
Strong in his rule, he now hath placed<br>
Duhsasan <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-41-fn-002" href="#sec-41-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-41-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> young in kingly power ;<br>
With offerings due, in holy rites,<br>
By Indra told, he worships Fire !<br>
His foes are quelled, his future glorious,<br>
And his realm extends to sea ;<br>
But strife with great men ends in sorrow,—<br>
And Duryodhan quakes at thee !<br>
If thy name is spoke in converse,<br>
Quakes at Arjun’s might of arm,<br>
Bends in grief his anguished forehead,<br>
Like a snake by <em>Mantra’s</em> charm !<br>
Prepared he is to do thee battle ;<br>
Take then, lord, precautions meet ;<br>
We gather words by others spoken,—<br>
Such, I lay them at thy feet.” (25)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">This said, the woodman went away,<br>
By Yudhishthir honoured well ;<br>
And the king in Bhîma’s presence<br>
To Draupadî told the tale.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-41-fn-003" href="#sec-41-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-41-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-173"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="173" data-folio="135" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n172_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 135">[p. 135]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">She heard the tale of foemen’s glory,<br>
Thought of wrongs which they had done,<br>
And to rouse her husband’s anger<br>
Drupad’s daughter thus began. (27)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Counsel to a saintly monarch<br>
Is rebuke from woman weak ;<br>
But ignoring woman’s duty,<br>
Pardon if my feelings speak !<br>
Of thy race the godlike monarchs<br>
Held their rule o’er all this land ;<br>
But as tuskers hurl their garlands,<br>
Thou hast hurled it from thy hand !<br>
Weak are they who with the wily<br>
Deal not with responding wile ;<br>
For like shafts on mail-less warriors,<br>
Artful foemen on them steal !<br>
Glory, in thy race begotten,<br>
Won by valour, dear as wife,<br>
Who but thee such glory forfeits,<br>
Girt by warriors tried in strife ? (31)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Godlike man ! now sadly treading<br>
Paths despised by proud and free,<br>
Doth not raging wrath consume thee<br>
Even as flames the sapless tree ?<br>
Men spontaneous yield to heroes<br>
Who in might subdue all ills,<br>
For an all-forgiving creature<br>
Love nor friend nor foeman feels !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-174"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="174" data-folio="136" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n173_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 136">[p. 136]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Sandal decked great Bhîma's person !<br>
Dusty, now, he roams the hills !<br>
Scarce I know, O soul of virtue !<br>
If my lord compassion feels !<br>
Arjun, conquering northern nations,<br>
To the poor all treasures gave !<br>
God-like Arjun, clad in wild barks,<br>
Doth he thy compassion move ?<br>
Does my lord in patience witness<br>
Yonder twins,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-41-fn-004" href="#sec-41-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-41-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> so great, so good ?—<br>
Dwelling uncouth in this forest<br>
Even like tuskers of the wood !<br>
Scarce I guess thy feelings, monarch,<br>
Strange and various are our hearts,<br>
But the thought of thy great sorrow<br>
Cruel grief to me imparts.<br>
Erst you slept on couches sumptuous,<br>
Waked to hear the song of praise ;<br>
Now on thorny earth reclining,<br>
Hear the sound the jackals raise !<br>
Erst on rice by Brâhmans tasted,<br>
Lived my lord of handsome frame ;<br>
Now he lives on forest produce,<br>
Thin and pale even like his fame !<br>
On his feet, on jewels resting,<br>
Wreaths of kings their pollen dropped ;<br>
Now they stray in forest jungle,<br>
By the deer or Brâhmans cropped.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-41-fn-005" href="#sec-41-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-41-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-175"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="175" data-folio="137" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n174_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 137">[p. 137]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And thy foes thy fall have compassed!<br>
Thoughts like this my bosom rend;<br>
Sufferings of unconquered heroes<br>
Brighter glory on them lend! (41)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Then spurn this sloth, assume thy prowess,<br>
Dire destruction quick devise;<br>
Hermits saintly, not proud monarchs,<br>
Ever-during patience prize!<br>
If great kings, whose wealth is glory,<br>
Such great insult meekly bore,<br>
Fame and chivalry be banished,<br>
Honour’s voice be heard no more!<br>
If forgiveness thou wilt cherish,<br>
Quelling pride and noble ire,<br>
Leave this bow of royal glory,<br>
Plait thy locks and worship fire!<br>
Plighted faith with faithless foemen<br>
Should not fetter thee for shame!<br>
Artful monarchs bent on conquests<br>
Fasten on their foes the blame! (45)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Fettered by oath, and pale through glory lost,<br>
Of power bereft, on seas of troubles tossed,<br>
Arise, O king! thy mighty foemen quell,<br>
As wakes the sun, night’s darkness to dispel!”<br>
