<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-208"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="208" data-folio="170" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n207_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 170">[p. 170]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK VI.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>ARJUNA’S PENANCE.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line"><span class="sc">Arjun</span>, on his penance bent,</span><br>
<span class="line">Followed Gangâ’s rocky course,</span><br>
<span class="line">Scaled the hill, as Vishnu<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-46-fn-001" href="#sec-46-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-46-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> mounts,</span><br>
<span class="line">The golden bird which heavenward soars.</span><br>
<span class="line">Forest trees like worshippers</span><br>
<span class="line">Sang his praise with hum of bees,</span><br>
<span class="line">Bent their heads and rained their blossoms,</span><br>
<span class="line">Gently shaken by the breeze!</span><br>
<span class="line">And the sweet and scented zephyrs,</span><br>
<span class="line">Saturate with Gangâ’s spray,</span><br>
<span class="line">Softly blew on Arjun’s forehead,</span><br>
<span class="line">And embraced him in their play!</span><br>
<span class="line">And the roar of wild cascades,</span><br>
<span class="line">Wafted o’er the woodland hum,</span><br>
<span class="line">And the notes of forest birds</span><br>
<span class="line">Stirred him like the beat of drum! (4)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">Cascades and rapid mountain torrents</span><br>
<span class="line">Sweep the tall trees in their course,</span><br>
<span class="line">But the gentle bending creeper</span><br>
<span class="line">Escapes their wild resistless force.</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-209"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="209" data-folio="171" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n208_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 171">[p. 171]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">And tuneful ducks before him fly,</span><br>
<span class="line">And with their voices fill the brake,</span><br>
<span class="line">In burnished gold swim on the waves,—</span><br>
<span class="line">A golden mantle on the lake!</span><br>
<span class="line">In deepest wilds the tuskers roam,</span><br>
<span class="line">And mark the banks with many a scar;</span><br>
<span class="line">The swarming bees fly to the spot,</span><br>
<span class="line">And streaming moisture scents the air!</span><br>
<span class="line">The golden Chakravâk is lost</span><br>
<span class="line">Midst waves in golden tints that shine,</span><br>
<span class="line">And Arjun hears the tender female</span><br>
<span class="line">Call her mate in gentle tone!</span><br>
<span class="line">Varying tints upon the stream</span><br>
<span class="line">Proclaim the gems in rocks below,</span><br>
<span class="line">As colours on the changeful face</span><br>
<span class="line">The workings of the mind oft show.</span><br>
<span class="line">But oft the surges, lashed by wind,</span><br>
<span class="line">Spread far and wide their foam awhile,—</span><br>
<span class="line">White as Ketak’s snowy flower,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Brightening the woods with their bright smile!</span><br>
<span class="line">But mark those spots, like peacock’s feather,</span><br>
<span class="line">Floating on the lucid wave;</span><br>
<span class="line">Wild elephants their juice exude,</span><br>
<span class="line">As they within the waters lave.</span><br>
<span class="line">And Arjun views the yawning oysters,</span><br>
<span class="line">Lying on their sandy bed,</span><br>
<span class="line">Beauteous with their glistening pearls,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-46-fn-002" href="#sec-46-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-46-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></span><br>
<span class="line">Or are they tears in sorrow shed?</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-210"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="210" data-folio="172" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n209_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 172">[p. 172]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">The creeper’s crimson budding shoot,</span><br>
<span class="line">Which oft the snowy dewdrop tips,</span><br>
<span class="line">Recalls unto the lover’s mind</span><br>
<span class="line">His nymph’s fair teeth, her maddening lips!</span><br>
<span class="line">And tuskers gambol in the wave,</span><br>
<span class="line">And frolic in the streamlets fair;</span><br>
<span class="line">The waters own their mighty lords,</span><br>
<span class="line">And odours scent the loaded air!</span><br>
<span class="line">But often from the placid lake</span><br>
<span class="line">The coily snake darts in the air,</span><br>
<span class="line">And breathes its poisoned breath in bubbles</span><br>
<span class="line">White as clouds in autumn fair. (15)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">Arjun many a streamlet crossed,—</span><br>
<span class="line">With fishes, bright as woman’s eye,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Like maidens waiting on their queen,</span><br>
<span class="line">The streamlets to great Gangâ hie!</span><br>
<span class="line">And then he scaled the towering hill,</span><br>
<span class="line">And found a spot, alone, apart,</span><br>
<span class="line">Begirt by many a flowering tree,</span><br>
<span class="line">And pure as purity of heart!</span><br>
<span class="line">The creepers decked with forest flowers,</span><br>
<span class="line">And trees where fruits in clusters hung,</span><br>
<span class="line">Inclined great Arjun’s pious heart</span><br>
<span class="line">To pious rites and penance long.</span><br>
<span class="line">By rules ordained he fixed his heart</span><br>
<span class="line">On penance which the saintly know;—</span><br>
<span class="line">What though the penance was severe,</span><br>
<span class="line">Who conquers self can feel no woe.</span><br>
<span class="line">He quelled his passions by his will,</span><br>
<span class="line">Dispelled all sins by virtue’s light,</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-211" data-page="211" data-folio="173" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n210_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 173">[p. 173]</button>And rose in merit day by day</span><br>
<span class="line">Like waxing moon of autumn night.</span><br>
<span class="line">All thoughts and cravings of the flesh</span><br>
<span class="line">By contemplation were dispelled,</span><br>
<span class="line">Until a righteous holy peace</span><br>
<span class="line">Great Arjun’s saintly bosom filled.</span><br>
<span class="line">By holy hymns and worship due</span><br>
<span class="line">He sought the mighty King of skies,</span><br>
<span class="line">And in his heart harmonious dwelt,</span><br>
<span class="line">A lofty power,—a holy peace! (22)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">He wore his plaited manlike locks</span><br>
<span class="line">Red as anchorites should wear;</span><br>
<span class="line">The tall tree wears its crest of leaves</span><br>
<span class="line">Fringed by morning’s red beams fair!</span><br>
<span class="line">Begirt in arms!—but like great saints</span><br>
<span class="line">In holy deed and gentle soul!</span><br>
<span class="line">The forest creatures knew his love,</span><br>
<span class="line">For love of heart endeareth all!</span><br>
<span class="line">The breezes softly blew on him,</span><br>
<span class="line">And shed a fragrance as it went;</span><br>
<span class="line">The tropic sun forgot its heat,</span><br>
<span class="line">And but a chastened radiance lent.</span><br>
<span class="line">And when the hero plucked the flowers,</span><br>
<span class="line">The stately tall trees bent their head;</span><br>
<span class="line">The earth put forth her tender grass</span><br>
<span class="line">To make the hero’s nightly bed!</span><br>
<span class="line">The cloudless sky auspicious sent</span><br>
<span class="line">Untimely rain the dust to lay;</span><br>
<span class="line">And kindly nature helped his toil,</span><br>
<span class="line">And nursed the hero night and day.</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-212"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="212" data-folio="174" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n211_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 174">[p. 174]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">The blossom of his glorious fate</span><br>
<span class="line">By laws divine its fruit thus bore;</span><br>
<span class="line">But signs like these moved not his heart,</span><br>
<span class="line">The great are firm for evermore! (28)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">Great Indra’s menials in the forest</span><br>
<span class="line">Heard of Arjun’s rites severe,</span><br>
<span class="line">His mighty merit, lofty virtue,—</span><br>
<span class="line">To their monarch went in fear.</span><br>
<span class="line">Before the monarch of the sky</span><br>
<span class="line">The menials their obeisance made,</span><br>
<span class="line">And in a humble graceful speech</span><br>
<span class="line">Thus of the unknown hermit said. (30)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">“Like a luminary of the sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">Though clad in barks, on yonder hill,</span><br>
<span class="line">A man intent on purpose high</span><br>
<span class="line">Doth penances! And earth is still!</span><br>
<span class="line">In arms, whose muscles snake-like coil,</span><br>
<span class="line">He holds a mighty powerful bow;</span><br>
<span class="line">But gentle are his deeds and rites,</span><br>
<span class="line">No gentler hermit lives below.</span><br>
<span class="line">The wind blows soft, the sward is green,</span><br>
<span class="line">And gentle rains the dust allay;</span><br>
<span class="line">By worth subdued the elements</span><br>
<span class="line">In one accord obeisance pay.</span><br>
<span class="line">The forest beasts their strife forget,</span><br>
<span class="line">And listen to his beck and word;</span><br>
<span class="line">For him the trees with blossoms wait,</span><br>
<span class="line">The mountains own him as their lord!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-213"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="213" data-folio="175" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n212_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 175">[p. 175]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">His toil bespeaks a purpose high,</span><br>
<span class="line">His mien denotes success is near;</span><br>
<span class="line">A gentle hermit! but his eye</span><br>
<span class="line">Instils a sense of secret fear!</span><br>
<span class="line">If from great saints he counts descent,</span><br>
<span class="line">From Daityas sprung or kingly line,</span><br>
<span class="line">We know not, Lord! Nor why in woods</span><br>
<span class="line">He penance doth and rites divine.</span><br>
<span class="line">Perchance he toils for purpose high,</span><br>
<span class="line">Perchance it is our ignorance,</span><br>
<span class="line">But as we feel, so speak we, Lord!</span><br>
<span class="line">We foresters are poor in sense!” (37)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">Dear to Indra, what they told</span><br>
<span class="line">Of holy rites which Arjun did;</span><br>
<span class="line">But wishing still his faith to try,</span><br>
<span class="line">His rising joy within him hid.</span><br>
<span class="line">Awhile he thought; and then pretending</span><br>
<span class="line">Not to know great Arjun’s mind,</span><br>
<span class="line">He sought for means to try the saint,</span><br>
<span class="line">And to the nymphs spoke soft and kind. (39)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">“Sweetly subtle, gently piercing,</span><br>
<span class="line">Heavenly nymphs! your eye’s keen dart!</span><br>
<span class="line">No arrow speedeth in its flight</span><br>
<span class="line">So true, unerring, to the heart!</span><br>
<span class="line">For righteous lore seek anchorites</span><br>
<span class="line">To conquer sins and ills of life;</span><br>
<span class="line">That potent lore, obtained by toil,</span><br>
<span class="line">Before your merry glance is weak!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-214"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="214" data-folio="176" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n213_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 176">[p. 176]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">All lovely things in universe,</span><br>
<span class="line">All beauteous form and winning charm,</span><br>
<span class="line">Combine to shape your heavenly grace!</span><br>
<span class="line">Then wield that grace, the saint disarm!</span><br>
<span class="line">Go forth with sweet musicians skilled,</span><br>
<span class="line">Destroy the mortal’s useless rite;</span><br>
<span class="line">Great anchorites have felt thy power,</span><br>
<span class="line">A youth will feel thy conquering might!</span><br>
<span class="line">’Tis sure he seeks for joys of earth</span><br>
<span class="line">But by the conquest of his foe;</span><br>
<span class="line">Who seeks for heaven strives not so,</span><br>
<span class="line">Who seeks for peace wields not the bow!</span><br>
<span class="line">And dread not from a mighty warrior</span><br>
<span class="line">Curse, as from an anchorite;</span><br>
<span class="line">For kind to females, full of sweetness</span><br>
<span class="line">Are great warriors in their might.”</span><br>
<span class="line">Honoured thus with high behest</span><br>
<span class="line">The nymphs bowed to great Indra’s will,</span><br>
<span class="line">And with a lovelier beauty shone,</span><br>
<span class="line">For honours heightened powers instil! (46)</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="line">The nymphs obeisance made, and slow retired,</span><br>
<span class="line">With lovelier charms and pleasing hopes inspired;</span><br>
<span class="line">And Indra with his lotus eyes gazed still</span><br>
<span class="line">Upon their swelling charms, nor gazed his fill!
