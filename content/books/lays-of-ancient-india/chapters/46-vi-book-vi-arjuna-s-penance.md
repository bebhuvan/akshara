<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-208"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="208" data-folio="170" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n207_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 170">[p. 170]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK VI.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>ARJUNA'S PENANCE.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Arjun</span>, on his penance bent,<br>
Followed Gangâ’s rocky course,<br>
Scaled the hill, as Vishnu<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-46-fn-001" href="#sec-46-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-46-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> mounts,<br>
The golden bird which heavenward soars.<br>
Forest trees like worshippers<br>
Sang his praise with hum of bees,<br>
Bent their heads and rained their blossoms,<br>
Gently shaken by the breeze!<br>
And the sweet and scented zephyrs,<br>
Saturate with Gangâ’s spray,<br>
Softly blew on Arjun’s forehead,<br>
And embraced him in their play!<br>
And the roar of wild cascades,<br>
Wafted o’er the woodland hum,<br>
And the notes of forest birds<br>
Stirred him like the beat of drum! (4)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Cascades and rapid mountain torrents<br>
Sweep the tall trees in their course,<br>
But the gentle bending creeper<br>
Escapes their wild resistless force.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-209"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="209" data-folio="171" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n208_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 171">[p. 171]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And tuneful ducks before him fly,<br>
And with their voices fill the brake,<br>
In burnished gold swim on the waves,—<br>
A golden mantle on the lake !<br>
In deepest wilds the tuskers roam,<br>
And mark the banks with many a scar ;<br>
The swarming bees fly to the spot,<br>
And streaming moisture scents the air !<br>
The golden Chakravâk is lost<br>
Midst waves in golden tints that shine,<br>
And Arjun hears the tender female<br>
Call her mate in gentle tone !<br>
Varying tints upon the stream<br>
Proclaim the gems in rocks below,<br>
As colours on the changeful face<br>
The workings of the mind oft show.<br>
But oft the surges, lashed by wind,<br>
Spread far and wide their foam awhile,—<br>
White as Ketak’s snowy flower,—<br>
Brightening the woods with their bright smile !<br>
But mark those spots, like peacock’s feather,<br>
Floating on the lucid wave ;<br>
Wild elephants their juice exude,<br>
As they within the waters lave.<br>
And Arjun views the yawning oysters,<br>
Lying on their sandy bed,<br>
Beauteous with their glistening pearls,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-46-fn-002" href="#sec-46-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-46-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Or are they tears in sorrow shed ?</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-210"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="210" data-folio="172" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n209_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 172">[p. 172]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The creeper’s crimson budding shoot,<br>
Which oft the snowy dewdrop tips,<br>
Recalls unto the lover’s mind<br>
His nymph’s fair teeth, her maddening lips!<br>
And tuskers gambol in the wave,<br>
And frolic in the streamlets fair;<br>
The waters own their mighty lords,<br>
And odours scent the loaded air!<br>
But often from the placid lake<br>
The coily snake darts in the air,<br>
And breathes its poisoned breath in bubbles<br>
White as clouds in autumn fair. (15)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Arjun many a streamlet crossed,—<br>
With fishes, bright as woman’s eye,—<br>
Like maidens waiting on their queen,<br>
The streamlets to great Gangâ hie!<br>
And then he scaled the towering hill,<br>
And found a spot, alone, apart,<br>
Begirt by many a flowering tree,<br>
And pure as purity of heart!<br>
The creepers decked with forest flowers,<br>
And trees where fruits in clusters hung,<br>
Inclined great Arjun’s pious heart<br>
To pious rites and penance long.<br>
By rules ordained he fixed his heart<br>
On penance which the saintly know ;—<br>
What though the penance was severe,<br>
Who conquers self can feel no woe.<br>
He quelled his passions by his will,<br>
Dispelled all sins by virtue’s light,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-211"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="211" data-folio="173" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n210_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 173">[p. 173]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And rose in merit day by day<br>
Like waxing moon of autumn night.<br>
All thoughts and cravings of the flesh<br>
By contemplation were dispelled,<br>
Until a righteous holy peace<br>
Great Arjun’s saintly bosom filled.<br>
By holy hymns and worship due<br>
He sought the mighty King of skies,<br>
And in his heart harmonious dwelt,<br>
A lofty power,—a holy peace ! (22)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">He wore his plaited manlike locks<br>
Red as anchorites should wear ;<br>
The tall tree wears its crest of leaves<br>
Fringed by morning’s red beams fair !<br>
Begirt in arms !—but like great saints<br>
In holy deed and gentle soul !<br>
The forest creatures knew his love,<br>
For love of heart endeareth all !<br>
The breezes softly blew on him,<br>
And shed a fragrance as it went ;<br>
The tropic sun forgot its heat,<br>
And but a chastened radiance lent.<br>
And when the hero plucked the flowers,<br>
The stately tall trees bent their head ;<br>
The earth put forth her tender grass<br>
To make the hero’s nightly bed !<br>
The cloudless sky auspicious sent<br>
Untimely rain the dust to lay ;<br>
And kindly nature helped his toil,<br>
And nursed the hero night and day.</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-212"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="212" data-folio="174" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n211_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 174">[p. 174]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The blossom of his glorious fate<br>
By laws divine its fruit thus bore ;<br>
But signs like these moved not his heart,<br>
The great are firm for evermore ! (28)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Great Indra’s menials in the forest<br>
Heard of Arjun’s rites severe,<br>
His mighty merit, lofty virtue,—<br>
To their monarch went in fear.<br>
Before the monarch of the sky<br>
The menials their obeisance made,<br>
And in a humble graceful speech<br>
Thus of the unknown hermit said. (30)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Like a luminary of the sky,<br>
Though clad in barks, on yonder hill,<br>
A man intent on purpose high<br>
Doth penances ! And earth is still !<br>
In arms, whose muscles snake-like coil,<br>
He holds a mighty powerful bow ;<br>
But gentle are his deeds and rites,<br>
No gentler hermit lives below.<br>
The wind blows soft, the sward is green,<br>
And gentle rains the dust allay ;<br>
By worth subdued the elements<br>
In one accord obeisance pay.<br>
The forest beasts their strife forget,<br>
And listen to his beck and word ;<br>
For him the trees with blossoms wait,<br>
The mountains own him as their lord !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-213"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="213" data-folio="175" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n212_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 175">[p. 175]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">His toil bespeaks a purpose high,<br>
His mien denotes success is near ;<br>
A gentle hermit ! but his eye<br>
Instils a sense of secret fear !<br>
If from great saints he counts descent,<br>
From Daityas sprung or kingly line,<br>
We know not, Lord ! Nor why in woods<br>
He penance doth and rites divine.<br>
Perchance he toils for purpose high,<br>
Perchance it is our ignorance,<br>
But as we feel, so speak we, Lord !<br>
We foresters are poor in sense ! ” (37)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Dear to Indra, what they told<br>
Of holy rites which Arjun did ;<br>
But wishing still his faith to try,<br>
His rising joy within him hid.<br>
Awhile he thought ; and then pretending<br>
Not to know great Arjun's mind,<br>
He sought for means to try the saint,<br>
And to the nymphs spoke soft and kind. (39)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Sweetly subtle, gently piercing,<br>
Heavenly nymphs ! your eye's keen dart !<br>
No arrow speedeth in its flight<br>
So true, unerring, to the heart !<br>
For righteous lore seek anchorites<br>
To conquer sins and ills of life ;<br>
That potent lore, obtained by toil,<br>
Before your merry glance is weak !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-214"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="214" data-folio="176" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n213_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 176">[p. 176]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">All lovely things in universe,<br>
All beauteous form and winning charm,<br>
Combine to shape your heavenly grace!<br>
Then wield that grace, the saint disarm!<br>
Go forth with sweet musicians skilled,<br>
Destroy the mortal’s useless rite;<br>
Great anchorites have felt thy power,<br>
A youth will feel thy conquering might!<br>
’Tis sure he seeks for joys of earth<br>
But by the conquest of his foe;<br>
Who seeks for heaven strives not so,<br>
Who seeks for peace wields not the bow!<br>
And dread not from a mighty warrior<br>
Curse, as from an anchorite;<br>
For kind to females, full of sweetness<br>
Are great warriors in their might.”<br>
Honoured thus with high behest<br>
The nymphs bowed to great Indra’s will,<br>
And with a lovelier beauty shone,<br>
For honours heightened powers instil! (46)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">The nymphs obeisance made, and slow retired,<br>
With lovelier charms and pleasing hopes inspired;<br>
And Indra with his lotus eyes gazed still<br>
Upon their swelling charms, nor gazed his fill!
