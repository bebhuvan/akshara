<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-99"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="99" data-folio="61" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n98_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 61">[p. 61]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">1.</span><br>
<span class="line"><span class="sc">Janaka</span>, the holy monarch,</span><br>
<span class="line">Erst in wide Videha<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-001" href="#sec-23-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> reigned,</span><br>
<span class="line">Made a mighty sacrifice,</span><br>
<span class="line">And an ample feast ordained!</span><br>
<span class="line">Brâhmans from the Kuru kingdom</span><br>
<span class="line">And from broad Panchâla<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-002" href="#sec-23-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> came,</span><br>
<span class="line">For they knew Janaka’s bounty,</span><br>
<span class="line">And they heard Janaka’s fame.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>
<span class="line">Who of these assembled Brâhmans,</span><br>
<span class="line">To the sacrifice who came,</span><br>
<span class="line">Is the wisest,—deepest learnéd,</span><br>
<span class="line">Worthiest of a Brâhman’s name?</span><br>
<span class="line">Such the thought that stirred the bosom</span><br>
<span class="line">Of Janaka, monarch bold,</span><br>
<span class="line">And he penned a thousand milch kine,</span><br>
<span class="line">On each horn was hung some gold!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-100"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="100" data-folio="62" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n99_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 62">[p. 62]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">3.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Saintly teachers, learned Brâhmans!</span><br>
<span class="line">Welcome to my sacrifice!</span><br>
<span class="line">Whoso of you be the wisest</span><br>
<span class="line">Let him stand and win the prize!</span><br>
<span class="line">Who is deepest in his lore,</span><br>
<span class="line">Let him take the cows and gold,”</span><br>
<span class="line">Thus unto the learned Brâhmans</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke Janaka, monarch bold!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">Brâhmans heard the royal mandate,</span><br>
<span class="line">Durst not stand and claim the prize,</span><br>
<span class="line">Where so many wise had gathered,</span><br>
<span class="line">Who could claim to be most wise?</span><br>
<span class="line">Up stood noble Yâjnavalkya,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Janaka’s great priest was he,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Boldly spake unto his pupil,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Drive the cattle home for me!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">5.</span><br>
<span class="line">Wrathful were the other Brâhmans,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Asvala, th’ invoking priest,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ârtabhâga, wise and saintly,</span><br>
<span class="line">Lâhyâyani, great at feast,</span><br>
<span class="line">Châkrâyana, Kaushîtaki,</span><br>
<span class="line">Uddâlaka full of pride,</span><br>
<span class="line">Many other wrathful sages</span><br>
<span class="line">Yâjnavalkya’s knowledge tried!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-101"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="101" data-folio="63" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n100_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 63">[p. 63]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">6.</span><br>
<span class="line">Proudly stood great Yâjnavalkya,</span><br>
<span class="line">Answered them with haughty pride,</span><br>
<span class="line">As a great and towering mountain</span><br>
<span class="line">Beateth back the surging tide!</span><br>
<span class="line">Then stood Gârgî, saintly lady,</span><br>
<span class="line">Noble hearted, fair and tall,</span><br>
<span class="line">Learned as the wisest Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">Noblest in th’ assembled hall!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">7.</span><br>
<span class="line">“As a warrior of the Kâsîs</span><br>
<span class="line">Or Videha’s warlike land</span><br>
<span class="line">Lifts his bow with sound of thunder,</span><br>
<span class="line">Lifts two arrows in his hand,</span><br>
<span class="line">Even so, great Yâjnavalkya,</span><br>
<span class="line">In this hall I challenge thee,</span><br>
<span class="line">I will ask you but two questions,</span><br>
<span class="line">Listen then and answer me.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">8.</span><br>
<span class="line">“On the earth, so far-extending,</span><br>
<span class="line">In the firmament and sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">In the present, past, and future,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ever present, ever nigh,</span><br>
<span class="line">Like the warp and like the woof</span><br>
<span class="line">Woven in the space and sky,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Wherein rests this universe,—</span><br>
<span class="line">What is present, ever nigh?”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-102"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="102" data-folio="64" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n101_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 64">[p. 64]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">9.</span><br>
<span class="line">To her answered Yâjnavalkya,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Deep the question thou hast made!</span><br>
<span class="line">It is ether, noble lady,</span><br>
<span class="line">Which doth sky and earth pervade.”</span><br>
<span class="line">To him spake the noble lady,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Good the answer you have given!</span><br>
<span class="line">But this ether, Yâjnavalkya,</span><br>
<span class="line">Wherein is it subtly woven?”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">10.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Listen, Gârgî, to my answer,</span><br>
<span class="line">In th’ <span class="sc">Immortal</span> it is woven!</span><br>
<span class="line">Of th’ <span class="sc">Immortal</span> who shall speak?</span><br>
<span class="line">Listen, as in sâstras<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-003" href="#sec-23-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> given.</span><br>
<span class="line">Not corporeal and not ether,</span><br>
<span class="line">He is neither short nor long,</span><br>
<span class="line">Flames not like the flaming red fire,</span><br>
<span class="line">Nor like water flows along.</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">11.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Without shadow, without darkness,</span><br>
<span class="line">He is neither air nor sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">Void of sensuous taste and feeling,</span><br>
<span class="line">Dwells alone sublime and high!</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-103" data-page="103" data-folio="65" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n102_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 65">[p. 65]</button>Without organs, without senses,</span><br>
<span class="line">All the world He hears and sees,</span><br>
<span class="line">Void of limbs, but ever active,</span><br>
<span class="line">Void of form, but measureless!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">12.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Gârgî! wouldst then further fathom?</span><br>
<span class="line">His great arm holds earth and sky!</span><br>
<span class="line">Days and hours His mandates listen,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sun and moon His hests obey!</span><br>
<span class="line">He has shaped the snowy mountains,</span><br>
<span class="line">Rolled the rivers swift and broad,</span><br>
<span class="line">He directs all nature’s actions,</span><br>
<span class="line">He is Brahman, He is God!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from the Brihadâranyaka Upanishad</em>, III. 1, 8.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-23-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-23-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">Tirhoot, or North Behar.</span></li><li id="sec-23-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-23-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">†.</a> <span class="fn-body">The Panchâla kingdom extended along the Ganges, round about the site of modern Kanouj.
