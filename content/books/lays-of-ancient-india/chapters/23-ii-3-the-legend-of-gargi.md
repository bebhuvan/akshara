<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-99"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="99" data-folio="61" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n98_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 61">[p. 61]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE LEGEND OF GÂRGÎ.</em></h2><h3 class="sec-h2">1.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Janaka</span>, the holy monarch,<br>
Erst in wide Videha<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-001" href="#sec-23-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> reigned,<br>
Made a mighty sacrifice,<br>
And an ample feast ordained!<br>
Brâhmans from the Kuru kingdom<br>
And from broad Panchâla<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-002" href="#sec-23-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> came,<br>
For they knew Janaka's bounty,<br>
And they heard Janaka's fame.</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Who of these assembled Brâhmans,<br>
To the sacrifice who came,<br>
Is the wisest,—deepest learnéd,<br>
Worthiest of a Brâhman's name ?<br>
Such the thought that stirred the bosom<br>
Of Janaka, monarch bold,<br>
And he penned a thousand milch kine,<br>
On each horn was hung some gold !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-100"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="100" data-folio="62" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n99_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 62">[p. 62]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Saintly teachers, learned Brâhmans !<br>
Welcome to my sacrifice !<br>
Whoso of you be the wisest<br>
Let him stand and win the prize !<br>
Who is deepest in his lore,<br>
Let him take the cows and gold,”<br>
Thus unto the learned Brâhmans<br>
Spoke Janaka, monarch bold !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Brâhmans heard the royal mandate,<br>
Durst not stand and claim the prize,<br>
Where so many wise had gathered,<br>
Who could claim to be most wise ?<br>
Up stood noble Yâjnavalkya,—<br>
Janaka’s great priest was he,—<br>
Boldly spake unto his pupil,<br>
“ Drive the cattle home for me !”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Wrathful were the other Brâhmans,—<br>
Asvala, th’ invoking priest,<br>
Ârtabhâga, wise and saintly,<br>
Lâhyâyani, great at feast,<br>
Châkrâyana, Kaushîtaki,<br>
Uddâlaka full of pride,<br>
Many other wrathful sages<br>
Yâjnavalkya’s knowledge tried !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-101"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="101" data-folio="63" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n100_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 63">[p. 63]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Proudly stood great Yâjnavalkya,<br>
Answered them with haughty pride,<br>
As a great and towering mountain<br>
Beateth back the surging tide !<br>
Then stood Gârgî, saintly lady,<br>
Noble hearted, fair and tall,<br>
Learned as the wisest Brâhman,<br>
Noblest in th’ assembled hall !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ As a warrior of the Kâsîs<br>
Or Videha’s warlike land<br>
Lifts his bow with sound of thunder,<br>
Lifts two arrows in his hand,<br>
Even so, great Yâjnavalkya,<br>
In this hall I challenge thee,<br>
I will ask you but two questions,<br>
Listen then and answer me.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ On the earth, so far-extending,<br>
In the firmament and sky,<br>
In the present, past, and future,<br>
Ever present, ever nigh,<br>
Like the warp and like the woof<br>
Woven in the space and sky,—<br>
Wherein rests this universe,—<br>
What is present, ever nigh ? ”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-102"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="102" data-folio="64" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n101_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 64">[p. 64]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To her answered Yâjnavalkya,<br>
“Deep the question thou hast made!<br>
It is ether, noble lady,<br>
Which doth sky and earth pervade.”<br>
To him spake the noble lady,<br>
“Good the answer you have given!<br>
But this ether, Yâjnavalkya,<br>
Wherein is it subtly woven?”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Listen, Gârgî, to my answer,<br>
In th’ <span class="sc">Immortal</span> it is woven!<br>
Of th’ <span class="sc">Immortal</span> who shall speak?<br>
Listen, as in sâstras<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-23-fn-003" href="#sec-23-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-23-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> given.<br>
Not corporeal and not ether,<br>
He is neither short nor long,<br>
Flames not like the flaming red fire,<br>
Nor like water flows along.</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">11.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Without shadow, without darkness,<br>
He is neither air nor sky,<br>
Void of sensuous taste and feeling,<br>
Dwells alone sublime and high!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-103"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="103" data-folio="65" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n102_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 65">[p. 65]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Without organs, without senses,<br>
All the world He hears and sees,<br>
Void of limbs, but ever active,<br>
Void of form, but measureless!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">12.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Gârgî ! wouldst then further fathom ?<br>
His great arm holds earth and sky !<br>
Days and hours His mandates listen,<br>
Sun and moon His hests obey !<br>
He has shaped the snowy mountains,<br>
Rolled the rivers swift and broad,<br>
He directs all nature’s actions,<br>
He is Brahman, He is God !”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from the Brihadâranyaka Upanishad</em>, III. 1, 8.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-23-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-23-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">Tirhoot, or North Behar.</span></li><li id="sec-23-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-23-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">†.</a> <span class="fn-body">The Panchâla kingdom extended along the Ganges, round about the site of modern Kanouj.
