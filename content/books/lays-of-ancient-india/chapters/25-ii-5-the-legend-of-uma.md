<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-108"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="108" data-folio="70" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n107_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 70">[p. 70]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE LEGEND OF UMÂ.</em></h2><h3 class="sec-h2">I.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Gods</span> and men and lower beings,—<br>
All from mighty Brahman flow!<br>
But in vanity and blindness<br>
Brahman great, we do not know!<br>
Once in past and ancient ages<br>
Vanity the gods o’ercame,—<br>
Boastful of their power and glory,<br>
Knew not whence that prowess came!</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>2.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Brahman to the gods appeared,<br>
And they knew not who was He,—<br>
Unto Agni thus they spake,—<br>
“Know thou who this stranger be.”<br>
Brahman placed a blade of straw,<br>
And the mighty god of Fire<br>
Vainly strove to burn the blade,<br>
Went back in his shame and ire!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-109"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="109" data-folio="71" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n108_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 71">[p. 71]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Unto Vâyu, god of tempests,<br>
Strong in might, resistless, free,<br>
Spake the gods in secret terror,<br>
“Know thou who this stranger be.”<br>
Brahman placed a blade of light straw,<br>
On it mighty Vâyu came,<br>
Vain he strove the blade to carry,<br>
Went back in his ire and shame!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Unto Indra, king of bright gods,<br>
Highest in the realms or sky,<br>
Spake the gods with soft entreaty,<br>
“Know thou who this stranger be.”<br>
Indra unto Brahman came,<br>
Knew not Brahman in his might,<br>
Saw no stranger god before him,—<br>
He had vanished from his sight!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Radiant in her god-like splendour,<br>
Umâ Haimavatî came!<br>
Umâ of the northern mountains,—<br>
Heavenly lore in mortal frame!<br>
Of her Indra humbly questioned,<br>
Who the stranger god might be,—<br>
To him Umâ gently answered,<br>
God of all the gods was He!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-110"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="110" data-folio="72" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n109_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 72">[p. 72]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ As the lightning’s mighty splendour<br>
Fills the eye with dazzling light,<br>
Knowledge of the mighty Brahman<br>
Fills the heart with radiance bright !<br>
From Him flows, ye gods ! your prowess,<br>
From Him worlds and oceans broad,<br>
To Him lift your humble prayers,<br>
He is Brahman, He is God.”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from Kena Upanishad</em>, III., V.</p></div>
