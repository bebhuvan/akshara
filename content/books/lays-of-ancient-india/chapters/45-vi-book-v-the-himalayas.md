<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-200"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="200" data-folio="162" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n199_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 162">[p. 162]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">BOOK V.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>THE HIMALAYAS.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">The</span> towering Himalaya rose,<br>
Surpassing mighty Meru high,<br>
To view the limits of wide space,<br>
Perchance to scale the lofty sky!<br>
Eternal sunshine on his face,<br>
His back in ceaseless gloomy night,<br>
Like Siva in dark tusker-skin,<br>
His face with radiant smiles all bright!<br>
Dwellers of earth and sky and heaven<br>
Mutually unseen here dwell;<br>
This range, itself a perfect world,<br>
Displaying Sambhu's mighty skill!<br>
All white the heaven-kissing peaks,<br>
With glittering gold the plateaus streaked,<br>
And bright as autumn's fleecy clouds<br>
With golden glittering lightning decked!<br>
Like peopled towns the mountain plateaus<br>
Streaked with gems which silk-like beam,<br>
And creeper grots by nymphs frequented<br>
Which like sylvan dwellings seem!<br>
Aloft, the rainless, sparkless clouds,<br>
In white and fleecy masses piled,<br>
Are like the mountain's giant wings,<br>
Far-extending, vast and wild! (6)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-201"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="201" data-folio="163" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n200_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 163">[p. 163]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Wild tuskers gore the rocky banks,<br>
Which many a costly mine conceal ;<br>
And sacred rills their waters pour,<br>
Which every pious wish fulfil !<br>
And mountain gems shine with a ray,<br>
As of the soft-hued crimson flower,<br>
And shed o'er plateaus, streaked with gold,<br>
The chastened light of evening hour !<br>
Untold Kadambas grace the scene,<br>
The Tamal lends its cooling shade,<br>
And mighty tuskers range the wood<br>
Where melting snow falls in cascade !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-001" href="#sec-45-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
In priceless gems the peaks are rich,<br>
The vales with beauteous creepers blessed ;<br>
Wild torrents flow through flowery banks,<br>
And trees are with wild blossoms graced !<br>
And mountain nymphs bedecked in gold<br>
Oft frolic in the sparkling rills ;<br>
And Nâgas, fond of trees and creepers,<br>
Love to dwell in wooded hills.<br>
And fleecy clouds with rainbow graced<br>
Oft rest the gem-decked peaks around,<br>
And seem like snowy mountain peaks<br>
Until ye hear the thunder's sound ! (12)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-202"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="202" data-folio="164" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n201_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 164">[p. 164]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Sweet-voiced ducks and lilies grace<br>
Pure Mânasa ! thy waters cool,<br>
Where Umâ often meets her lord,<br>
Begirt by troops, but calm in soul !<br>
And oft his worshippers recall<br>
Tripurâ burnt by Siva’s ire,<br>
When lighting planets in the sky<br>
Flames forth at night the forest fire !<br>
And rolling down from mighty plateaus,<br>
Spreading in a mass of spray,<br>
And winding through deep rocky gorges,<br>
Gangâ shines like Châmar<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-002" href="#sec-45-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup> gay !<br>
And Arjun’s mind was struck with wonder<br>
At this great, this glorious sight ;<br>
And unto him thus spoke the Yaksha,<br>
Words appropriate, soft and sweet. (16)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Sacred range ! whose snow-white turrets<br>
Pierce the lofty vault of heaven,<br>
Sinful mortals view these mountains,<br>
And their sins are all forgiven !<br>
Its deeper parts like Brahmâ’s Self,<br>
In sacred Sâstras faintly told,<br>
Are unto mortals all unknown,—<br>
The outer ranges they behold !<br>
Here creeper grots are rich in flowers,<br>
And lucid lakes in lilies blown ;<br>
And haughty nymphs, wooed by their swains,<br>
A sweet and soft emotion own !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-203"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="203" data-folio="165" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n202_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 165">[p. 165]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And aye, this mountain fills the earth<br>
With wealth above all worlds, I ween,<br>
And mortals by Kuvera’s gift <sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-003" href="#sec-45-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Their dearest wishes here obtain!<br>
The wealth of all the worlds combined<br>
The sacred Himalay excels,<br>
For here in grace and power divine<br>
Great Sambhu with his Umâ dwells!<br>
Who freed from sins and mortal life<br>
Seek Brahmâ’s holy mansion pure,<br>
They seek it here in contemplation,<br>
As in Sâstra’s sacred lore! (22)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Oft in these vales celestial footprints,<br>
Garlands cast away and pale,<br>
And beds of flowers in sweet disorder,<br>
Tales of secret love they tell!<br>
And on these hills by worlds adored<br>
The plant at night its flame gives forth,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-004" href="#sec-45-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup><br>
As glory shines on virtuous kings<br>
Attained by might and pious worth!<br>
And in the groves which bend with flowers<br>
His lay the sweet-voiced Kokil trills,<br>
And tuskers feed on watery plants<br>
Beside the cool and shaded rills!<br>
And oft they rub their scented heads<br>
Upon the gnarléd forest tree,<br>
The mango scent excites the Kokil,<br>
And attracts the maddened bee!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-204"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="204" data-folio="166" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n203_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 166">[p. 166]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Beside these sweetly sounding rills<br>
Celestial maidens often rove,<br>
And drink the drink of gods,—the nectar,<br>
Which the gods and Nâgas love !<br>
And as they rest in creeper grots,<br>
And soft the gentle breeze sweeps by,<br>
Faint with love’s toil, they feel its charm,<br>
Nor seek their homes in upper sky ! (28)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ Here sweet-eyed Umâ sought her lord,<br>
By rites and penance duly made ;<br>
And Siva here in love’s sweet tremor<br>
Clasped the blushing mountain maid !<br>
Gods and Asurs, for the nectar,<br>
Erst with Mandâr churned the sea,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-005" href="#sec-45-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Bearing still the serpent’s trail,<br>
Here Mandâr scales the lofty sky !<br>
And crystal rocks in masses piled<br>
Reflect the red sun’s radiant beams,<br>
And chastened by the sapphires blue<br>
The day like softer moonlight seems.<br>
And on the ripples of the lake,<br>
As sweet as woman’s flashing eye,<br>
The lotus bud is gently moved<br>
By gentle zephyrs from the sky ! (32)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ When sweet-eyed Umâ gave her hand<br>
To Siva by these mountain lakes,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-205"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="205" data-folio="167" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n204_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 167">[p. 167]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Her hand was twined with sacred grass,<br>
And Siva’s hand was twined with snakes !<br>
And crystal rocks with sunbeams lit<br>
Sent forth a bright and dazzling ray,<br>
And heightened more the light of sun<br>
And beautified the sacred day !<br>
And on Kailâsa’s lofty peak,<br>
Round which revolves th’ unwearied sun,<br>
To please great Siva, lord of hosts,<br>
Kuvera built his beauteous town !<br>
There sparkling rays of crystal rocks<br>
In brilliant tints harmonious blend,<br>
And to the eye of the beholder<br>
Strange delusions often lend !<br>
Eternal verdure decks the fields,<br>
The soft green woods enchant the view,<br>
And trees with blossoms ever crowned<br>
Nor fade nor wear the yellow hue !<br>
The emerald’s rays, like fresh green grass,<br>
Deceive and tempt the forest deer,<br>
And with a soft and chastened radiance<br>
Mingle with the sunbeams clear !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-006" href="#sec-45-fn-006" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-006" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
And the pollen from the blossoms,<br>
Wafted from the flowering trees,<br>
Like a golden canopy<br>
Floats upon the evening breeze ! (39)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-206"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="206" data-folio="168" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n205_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 168">[p. 168]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Oft at early dawn are seen<br>
Unequal footprints by the rills,<br>
For Umâ with the mighty Siva<br>
Loves to haunt these sacred hills!<br>
And oft at noon, through leafy trees,<br>
The day-god’s struggling, radiant beam<br>
Strikes on the bright and crystal rock,<br>
And makes it like a mirror gleam!<br>
And oft at night, on distant peaks,<br>
Great Siva’s snow-white bull is seen,<br>
And wondering females gaze and ask,<br>
Is it the full-orbed rising moon?<br>
What though the rainbow’s broken arch<br>
On fleecy clouds is faint and pale,<br>
Borrowing fulness from the gems<br>
It bends sublime o’er rock and vale!<br>
What though the gloom in moonless<br>
nights<br>
O’ershadows valley, hill and lea,<br>
The moon on Siva’s ample crest<br>
Pours forth a light on flower and tree! (44)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Yonder hill with golden caverns<br>
Is to mighty Indra dear;<br>
How its peaks with golden lustre<br>
Woods and spreading forests cheer!<br>
And oft when mountain breezes blow,<br>
And move luxuriant creepers thick,<br>
The golden beams of that bright hill<br>
Even like the flashing lightning break!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-207"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="207" data-folio="169" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n206_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 169">[p. 169]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Celestial tuskers range that hill,<br>
And shake the scented Chandan tree ;<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-45-fn-007" href="#sec-45-fn-007" data-fn-target="sec-45-fn-007" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Before their might all earthly creatures<br>
In their terror turn and flee !<br>
And sapphires here with azure tints<br>
Make soft the sun's effulgent ray ;<br>
And darksome clouds in glorious masses<br>
Temper here the light of day ! (48)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“ There Kshatriya-like thy penance do,<br>
As by the saintly Vyâsa told ;—<br>
By noble efforts, constant toil,<br>
Achieve their fame the great and bold !<br>
May Siva grant thee strength and might,<br>
Bestow on thee his blessed grace ;<br>
And be the gods thy constant help,<br>
And bless thy labours with success ! ”<br>
Thus spoke, and hied unto his home<br>
The Yaksha, gentle, kind, and good ;<br>
And Arjun pensive saw him part,<br>
And for a moment sorrowing stood. (51)</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">He reached the hill, uncrossed by living wight,<br>
With luscious fruits and blossoms on its height ;<br>
And as he gazed upon the lofty hill,<br>
Great thoughts of noble deeds his heart did fill ! (52)</div></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-45-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-45-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">This verse, like many other verses of this Book, is, in the original, unsurpassed in the beauty of its alliteration.
