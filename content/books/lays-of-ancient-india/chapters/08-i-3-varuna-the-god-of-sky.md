<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-51"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="51" data-folio="13" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n50_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 13">[p. 13]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>VARUNA, GOD OF SKY.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">“The eminent German scholar, Dr. Roth, is of opinion that before the Indo-Aryans and the Iranians separated, Varuna was the highest and holiest of the gods of their ancestors, and represented the spiritual side of their religion. After the separation had taken place, this deity of righteousness was translated in Iran into Ahura Mazd, the Supreme Deity. And although in India, Varuna yielded the foremost place among gods to the young and vigorous rain-giver, Indra, still he never became divested of that sanctity and holiness which entered into his first conception, and the holiest hymns of the Rig Veda are his, not Indra’s.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 76.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The first five verses of this hymn are addressed to the Fire and to the Sun, and are omitted in the translation.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">I.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Varuna</span>, Lord of righteousness !<br>
Thy wondrous power invites our lays !<br>
The birds that sail across the sky,<br>
Not with thy matchless speed can fly !<br>
Nor the swift winds in their unceasing course,<br>
Nor rapid torrents in resistless force !</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>2.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Glorious King of righteousness !<br>
Thy seat is in ethereal space !<br>
Thy radiance dwells in skies above,<br>
And thence descends on us in love.<br>
Mighty monarch of the upper sky !<br>
Protect your humble worshippers from high !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-52"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="52" data-folio="14" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n51_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 14">[p. 14]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">In heaven’s expanse thy hand hath laid<br>

Destroy, O mighty King, the foeman’s power,<br>
And save, oh save us in the evil hour !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Thine, the power to heal and bless !<br>
Thine, deep wisdom, righteousness !<br>
Keep us, Lord, from impious deeds,<br>
From wicked thought that oft misleads.<br>
Teach us, O King ! the ways of vice to shun,<br>
And save, oh save us from the sins we’ve done !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Yon stars that spangle night’s dark sky,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-001" href="#sec-08-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>

Nations speak thy mercy and thy power,<br>
Then spare, oh spare me in this evil hour !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">My friends, they tell me, night and day,<br>

Your chains and fetters he will soon remove,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-002" href="#sec-08-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
For Varuna is mercy ! He is love !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Son of the Eternal Light !<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-003" href="#sec-08-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup><br>

Obedient to thy laws and free from sin !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, I. 24.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-08-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-08-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">“The word used in the text is Riksha, which may either mean stars generally, or the stars of the constellation Great Bear. The root <em>rich</em> means to shine, whence in course of time the word Riksha came to have two meanings—the shining stars of a particular constellation, and an animal with bright eyes and shining glossy hair. By a natural confusion of ideas, therefore, the constellation itself ultimately came to be called the Bear. The question is discussed with remarkable eloquence and learning by Max Müller in his ‘Science of Language,’ and he explains that ‘the surprise with which many a thoughtful observer has looked at these seven bright stars, wondering why they were ever called the Bear, is removed by reference to the early annals of human speech.’ ”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 77.</span></li><li id="sec-08-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-08-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">The poet Sunahsepa is supposed to be bound to the sacrificial post, and prays to Varuna to remove his chains and to pardon his sins.</span></li><li id="sec-08-fn-003" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-08-fn-003" aria-label="Return to text">†.</a> <span class="fn-body">The word used in the text is Aditi, the parent of the bright gods. “Aditi means the undivided, the unlimited, the eternal. It is in reality, as has been stated, the earliest name invented by man to express the Infinite—the visible infinite, the endless expanse, beyond the <span class="folio-turn">[p. 16]</span> earth, beyond the clouds, beyond the sky. . . . It means, according to the eminent German scholar, Dr. Roth, the eternal and inviolable principle, the celestial light.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 83.</span></li></ol></section>
