<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-127"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="127" data-folio="89" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n126_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 89">[p. 89]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>TEN PRECEPTS FROM DHAMMAPADA.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote class="epigraph-block"><p>“The whole of the <em>Dhammapada</em> is a string of 423 moral precepts<br>
which for their beauty and moral worth are unsurpassed by any similar<br>
collection of precepts made in any age or country. . . . Who is not<br>
struck by the remarkable coincidence of these noble precepts with<br>
those preached five hundred years after in Palestine by the gentle<br>
and pure-souled Jesus Christ?”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i.<br>
p. 366, 367.</p></blockquote></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>Return Love for Hatred.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Hate</span> for hatred if ye render,<br>
Hatred lives and mortal strife ;<br>
Love return for bitter hatred,<br>
Hatred dies, and sweet is life ! (5)</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>Precepts without Acts.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Pious precepts, gentle friend,<br>
Never acted, wisely meant,<br>
Are like gay and coloured flowers,—<br>
Without fragrance, without scent ! (51)</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><em>The Golden Rule.</em></h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">As you dread all pain and suffering,<br>
Love your life and death abhor,<br>
So doth every living creature,<br>
Harm not things that live and breathe.<br>
(129, 130)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-128"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="128" data-folio="90" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n127_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 90">[p. 90]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Live without Hatred among Men you Hate.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">With the men who live in hatred<br>
Ye shall live devoid of hate,<br>
Unto men who smite in anger<br>
Show your love and meekness great. (197)</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Good Works survive.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Good works done endure for ever,<br>
And in higher life will meet,<br>
E'en as gentle loving kinsmen<br>
Home-returning kinsmen greet! (200)</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Overcome Anger by Love.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Anger by your love o'ermaster,<br>
Good for evil acts return;<br>
By charity the miser conquer,<br>
By your truth let false men learn! (223)</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>The Faults of other Men.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Faults of other men ye question,<br>
Not the fault that ye have done!<br>
Like chaff your neighbour's vices winnow,<br>
Like a false die hide your own! (252)</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-129"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="129" data-folio="91" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n128_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 91">[p. 91]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>The Elder and the Sage.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Not an Elder, not a Sage,<br>
Is the man advanced in age;<br>
Truth and virtue, love and pureness,<br>
Make the Elder and the Sage. (260, 261)</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Assumed and True Holiness.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Not by skins and plaited hair,<br>
Not by family or birth,<br>
But by truth and righteousness<br>
Is the Brâhman known on earth. (393)</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>There is Ravening within.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Wherefore wear the plaited hair,<br>
Wherefore garment wild of skin,<br>
What avails this outward penance<br>
When there's ravening within! (394)</div></div>
