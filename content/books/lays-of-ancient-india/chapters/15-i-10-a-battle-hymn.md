<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-75"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="75" data-folio="37" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n74_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 37">[p. 37]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>A BATTLE HYMN.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote><p>“Sudâs was an Aryan king and conqueror, and we are frequently told that various Aryan tribes and kings combined against him, and he was victorious over them all. The allusions to these internecine wars among Aryan races, and to the particular tribes who fought against Sudâs, are historically among the most important passages in the Rig Veda. . . . The poet Tritsu or Vasishtha, who sang these deeds of Sudâs’s glory, was not unrewarded for his immortal verse. For in verses 22 and 23 (Hymn VII. 18) he acknowledges with gratitude that the valiant Sudâs rewarded him with two hundred cows and two chariots and four horses with gold trappings.”—<em>Civilisation in Ancient India</em>, vol. i. p. 55.</p></blockquote></div><h4 class="sec-h3">1.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Varuna</span> glorious! Indra bright!<br>
Our troops have eastward marched to fight.<br>
Shining javelins in their hand,<br>
They’ll conquer cattle, conquer land!<br>
Quell mighty gods, the Dâsa and the foe,<br>
And save our King Sudâs from every woe!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">When mustered nations lift on high<br>
Their banners floating in the sky,<br>
When warriors anxious lift their gaze<br>
From reddened field to sky’s dark haze,<br>
Ye mighty gods! be then our hope and stay,<br>
And help our King Sudâs in dubious fray!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-76"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="76" data-folio="38" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n75_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 38">[p. 38]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">3.<br>
When to the bewildered eye<br>
The broad earth mingles with the sky,<br>
When the deafening shouts arise<br>
And assail the echoing skies,<br>
And when the foeman’s marshalled troops appear,<br>
Then save our King Sudâs, and be ye near !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">4.<br>
Gods auspicious ! you have quelled<br>
The mighty foe, yet unassailed ;—<br>
You have been Sudâs’s stay,<br>
Helped him in the dubious fray !<br>
And you have heard the Tritsus’ holy lay,<br>
Their priestly work bore fruit in battle’s day !</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">5.<br>
Our foemen in the battle’s day,<br>
Strong and stubborn in the fray,<br>
Adept in all the warlike arts,<br>
Pierced us with their countless darts.<br>
Ye mighty gods ! to you we raised our lay,<br>
And you have helped our King, and were our stay.</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">6.<br>
Our foemen called on you in vain,<br>
Ye listen not to impious men !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-77"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="77" data-folio="39" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n76_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 39">[p. 39]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">In vain they sigh for wealth and lands,<br>
For impious hearts make feeble hands!<br>
Ten hostile kings, combined, their troops did bring;<br>
Ye heard the Tritsus!  Saved Sudâs, our king!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Ten kings who know no pious rite<br>
Did in vain their troops unite!<br>
In vain did they their weapons bring<br>
Against Sudâs, our mighty king!<br>
You listened to the Tritsus’ holy lay,<br>
For gods accord their help when mortals pray!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">With their long and braided hair<br>
Tritsu’s white-robed sons appear,<br>
And with pious gifts and song<br>
The holy sacrifice prolong!<br>
Ten hostile kings attacked our monarch brave!<br>
In vain! the gods were nigh, and they can save!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Indra slays the foe in fight,<br>
Varuna tends our pious rite!<br>
For your glory, for your praise,<br>
We will chant our pious lays!<br>
Ever gracious to the voice of prayer,<br>
Bounteous gods! to worshippers appear!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-78"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="78" data-folio="40" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n77_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 40">[p. 40]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Varuna! Indra! strong in might,<br>
Aryaman! and Mitra bright!<br>
Grant to us, ye sons of light,<br>
A home and all your favours bright.<br>
Be good to us, Aditi’s holy ray,<br>
And we will chant Savitri’s glorious lay!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, VII. 83.</p></div>
