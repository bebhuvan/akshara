<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-85"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="85" data-folio="47" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n84_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 47">[p. 47]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE GOLDEN CHILD.</em></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The following is one of the hymns of the Rig Veda which show that the mind of the ancient Hindu worshipper travelled “from Nature up to Nature’s God.”</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">1.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?<br>
To Him, the Golden Child, the One,<br>
Existent from creation’s dawn !<br>
To Him who built the earth and spread the sky,<br>
We bring our offerings, chant our prayers high !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?<br>
To Him who lighted life’s first ray,<br>
Whose will celestial hosts obey !<br>
Whose mighty shade is immortality !<br>
Whose slave is death ! Transcendent Deity !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom on earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-86"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="86" data-folio="48" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n85_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 48">[p. 48]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To Him, the glorious King, above,<br>
Of living things that see and move !<br>
The Lord of men and beasts, of birds in air,<br>
To Him we offer gifts and chanted prayer !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?<br>
To Him whose mighty hand hath made<br>
The earth’s broad fields, the ocean’s bed,<br>
The snowy mountains, the far-spreading sky,<br>
To Him we lift our prayers chanted high !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?<br>
To Him who fixed the earth and sky,<br>
And spread the glorious heavens on high,<br>
The highest heaven whose fingers fixed and bent,<br>
Whose hand has measured out the firmament !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer ?<br>
Who hung the sky at nature’s birth<br>
And spread this broad and spacious earth !<br>
The sounding sky and earth each shining hour,<br>
And radiant sun attest His mighty power !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-87"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="87" data-folio="49" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n86_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 49">[p. 49]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer?<br>
To Him,—the Life of gods!—the One!<br>
Who breathed forth at creation's dawn!<br>
When mighty waters filled chaotic gloom,<br>
And held the nascent <em>agni</em> in their womb.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-18-fn-001" href="#sec-18-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-18-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer?<br>
Who held the waters in the gloom,<br>
And breathed forth life into their womb,<br>
To Him, the God of gods, the mighty One!<br>
Who held the universe at creation's dawn!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">To whom in earth or sky or air<br>
Shall we offer gifts and prayer?<br>
To Him, the earth and sky who made,<br>
And the mighty waters laid,<br>
For He is Truth! the great Almighty Power!<br>
And He will save us in the evil hour!</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-88"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="88" data-folio="50" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n87_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 50">[p. 50]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Lord of Universe and King!<br>
Thy hand hath shaped each living thing!<br>
To Thee we lift our humble praise,<br>
For Thou canst save, and Thou canst bless!<br>
Fulfil our wishes, bless our sacrifice,<br>
And on Thy worshippers bestow Thy grace!</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Rig Veda</em>, X. 121.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-18-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-18-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">The Hindu scriptures, like those of other nations, hold that in the beginning water pervaded the universe.</span></li></ol></section>
