<span class="line"><span class="sc">Nachiketas</span>, righteous boy,—</span><br>
<span class="line">By his father sent below,—</span><br>
<span class="line">From the mighty king of Death,</span><br>
<span class="line">His great secret feign would know!</span><br>
<span class="line">“There is doubt,”—so spake the boy—</span><br>
<span class="line">“When an earthly mortal dies,</span><br>
<span class="line">Is that death his final end?—</span><br>
<span class="line">Or does he live in earth or skies?”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>
<span class="line">Unto him thus answered Yama,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Nachiketas! you speak well,</span><br>
<span class="line">Mortals often ask that question,</span><br>
<span class="line">Gods that question often feel!</span><br>
<span class="line">Nachiketas! ’tis my secret,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Secret unto all unknown,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for every other favour,</span><br>
<span class="line">Death’s great secret leave alone!</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-112"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="112" data-folio="74" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n111_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 74">[p. 74]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">3.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Ask for sons and happy grandsons—</span><br>
<span class="line">They a hundred years will live,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for gold and cars and horses,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Every blessing mortals crave!</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for broad and spacious acres,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for harvests rich and rare,</span><br>
<span class="line">Many autumns,—as thou willest,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Live without a pang or fear!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Nachiketas! other objects</span><br>
<span class="line">If thy anxious heart desire,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for them,—for wealth or glory,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Dread refusal nor my ire!</span><br>
<span class="line">Be a king of mighty kingdoms,</span><br>
<span class="line">Be a lord upon the earth,</span><br>
<span class="line">Happy in thy life’s fruition,</span><br>
<span class="line">Be the foremost in thy worth!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">5.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Every wish thy heart can fathom,</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for joys on earth so rare;</span><br>
<span class="line">With their cars and heavenly music</span><br>
<span class="line">Take these heavenly damsels fair!</span><br>
<span class="line">Be attended by these maidens,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Such as they men do not see,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Ask for these and other favours,</span><br>
<span class="line">Leave my secret unto me!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-113"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="113" data-folio="75" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n112_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 75">[p. 75]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">6.</span><br>
<span class="line">“These are,” Nachiketas answered,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Objects that will fade away,</span><br>
<span class="line">What will please us in the present,</span><br>
<span class="line">Fade to-morrow and decay!</span><br>
<span class="line">Keep thy dance and keep thy music,</span><br>
<span class="line">For great objects let me pray,</span><br>
<span class="line">Teach me thy great secret, Yama,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Secret of the after-day!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">7.</span><br>
<span class="line">Pressed by pious Nachiketas,</span><br>
<span class="line">Death his secret thus revealed,</span><br>
<span class="line">“To the man of contemplation</span><br>
<span class="line">Life and Death their secret yield.</span><br>
<span class="line">And he sees the ancient Soul,</span><br>
<span class="line">Hid in dark from mortal eyes,</span><br>
<span class="line">Sees the great and mighty Self,—</span><br>
<span class="line">God pervading earth and skies!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">8.</span><br>
<span class="line">“And the man who knows this secret,</span><br>
<span class="line">Comprehends and grasps it all,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Casteth off his mortal body,</span><br>
<span class="line">Mingles in the mighty Soul!</span><br>
<span class="line">This, O mortal! is my secret,</span><br>
<span class="line">Brahman is the mighty Breath!</span><br>
<span class="line">Brahman’s house is ever open,</span><br>
<span class="line">Life existeth after death!”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from Katha Upanishad</em>, I. 1, 2.</p></div>
