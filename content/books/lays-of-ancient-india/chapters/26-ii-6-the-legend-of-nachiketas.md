<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-111"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="111" data-folio="73" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n110_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 73">[p. 73]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE LEGEND OF NACHIKETAS.</em></h2><h4 class="sec-h3">1.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Nachiketas</span>, righteous boy,—<br>
By his father sent below,—<br>
From the mighty king of Death,<br>
His great secret feign would know !<br>
“There is doubt,”—so spake the boy—<br>
“When an earthly mortal dies,<br>
Is that death his final end ?—<br>
Or does he live in earth or skies ? ”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">2.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Unto him thus answered Yama,<br>
“Nachiketas ! you speak well,<br>
Mortals often ask that question,<br>
Gods that question often feel !<br>
Nachiketas ! ’tis my secret,—<br>
Secret unto all unknown,—<br>
Ask for every other favour,<br>
Death’s great secret leave alone !</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-112"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="112" data-folio="74" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n111_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 74">[p. 74]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Ask for sons and happy grandsons—<br>
They a hundred years will live,—<br>
Ask for gold and cars and horses,—<br>
Every blessing mortals crave !<br>
Ask for broad and spacious acres,<br>
Ask for harvests rich and rare,<br>
Many autumns,—as thou willest,—<br>
Live without a pang or fear !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Nachiketas ! other objects<br>
If thy anxious heart desire,<br>
Ask for them,—for wealth or glory,—<br>
Dread refusal nor my ire !<br>
Be a king of mighty kingdoms,<br>
Be a lord upon the earth,<br>
Happy in thy life’s fruition,<br>
Be the foremost in thy worth !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Every wish thy heart can fathom,<br>
Ask for joys on earth so rare ;<br>
With their cars and heavenly music<br>
Take these heavenly damsels fair !<br>
Be attended by these maidens,—<br>
Such as they men do not see,—<br>
Ask for these and other favours,<br>
Leave my secret unto me !”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-113"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="113" data-folio="75" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n112_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 75">[p. 75]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“These are,” Nachiketas answered,<br>
“Objects that will fade away,<br>
What will please us in the present,<br>
Fade to-morrow and decay!<br>
Keep thy dance and keep thy music,<br>
For great objects let me pray,<br>
Teach me thy great secret, Yama,—<br>
Secret of the after-day!”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Pressed by pious Nachiketas,<br>
Death his secret thus revealed,<br>
“To the man of contemplation<br>
Life and Death their secret yield.<br>
And he sees the ancient Soul,<br>
Hid in dark from mortal eyes,<br>
Sees the great and mighty Self,—<br>
God pervading earth and skies!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“And the man who knows this secret,<br>
Comprehends and grasps it all,—<br>
Casteth off his mortal body,<br>
Mingles in the mighty Soul!<br>
This, O mortal! is my secret,<br>
Brahman is the mighty Breath!<br>
Brahman’s house is ever open,<br>
Life existeth after death!”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from Katha Upanishad</em>, I. 1, 2.</p></div>
