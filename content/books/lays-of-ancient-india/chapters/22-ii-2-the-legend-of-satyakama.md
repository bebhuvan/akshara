<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-95"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="95" data-folio="57" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n94_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 57">[p. 57]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><em>THE LEGEND OF SATYAKÂMA.</em></h2><h3 class="sec-h2">1.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="sc">Satyakâma</span>,—truthful boy,—<br>
Poor Jabâlâ’s humble son,<br>
Felt a passion in his soul<br>
For the truth and truth alone !<br>
So he came unto his mother,<br>
Asked her of his father’s name,<br>
“ Mother ! I would be a student,<br>
Tell me of what race I am.”</div></div><h3 class="sec-h2">2.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Jabâlâ the humble woman<br>
Spoke in grief but spoke the truth,<br>
“ Sinfully I long have wandered,<br>
And conceived thee in my youth !<br>
And I know not who thy father,<br>
Know not of what race thou art,<br>
By the name of thy poor mother<br>
Call thyself, child of my heart !”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-96"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="96" data-folio="58" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n95_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 58">[p. 58]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">3.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Satyakâma,—truthful boy,—<br>
To Gautama went the youth,<br>
“I would be a student, Sir,<br>
For I seek to know the truth.”<br>
Gautama a welcome gave<br>
To the boy who to him came,<br>
“Tell me of what race thou art,<br>
Tell me what may be thy name.”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">4.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Satyakâma,—truthful boy,—<br>
Spoke in grief but spoke the truth,<br>
“Sinfully my mother wandered,<br>
And conceived me in her youth.<br>
And I know not who my father,<br>
Know not of what race I am,<br>
Jabâlâ is my poor mother,<br>
Jabâlâ must be my name.”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">5.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Enough, enough, my truthful boy,”—<br>
Spake the sage unto the youth,—<br>
“By your worth you are a Brâhman,<br>
For you dare to speak the truth!<br>
Go and fetch the sacred fuel,<br>
I will teach you, noble youth,<br>
In the learning of our fathers,<br>
For you have not swerved from truth!”</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-97"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="97" data-folio="59" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n96_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 59">[p. 59]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3">6.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">Satyakâma brought the fuel,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-22-fn-001" href="#sec-22-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-22-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup><br>
Was a student young and brave,<br>
And he kept his teacher’s cattle,<br>
Served his teacher as his slave.<br>
And unto the pathless meadows<br>
With the cattle went the youth,<br>
In his high and eager bosom<br>
Nature flashed the light of truth!</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">7.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">From the strong and noble bull<br>
Of the herd that he was tending,<br>
From the evening’s faggot fire<br>
Over which he pondered bending,<br>
From the gay and bright flamingo<br>
As it sailed across the sky,<br>
From the diver-bird so beauteous<br>
Which unto him was not shy,</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">8.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">From the anxious contemplation<br>
Which for ever filled his mind,<br>
When the evening fire was lighted<br>
And the cattle had been penned,</div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-98"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="98" data-folio="60" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n97_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 60">[p. 60]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">From each humble, lowly object,<br>
Came its meaning to the youth,<br>
From great Nature’s mighty wonders<br>
Flashed on him the light of truth !</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">9.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">And to Gautama he came,<br>
And a light was on his face !<br>
For a knowledge of the truth<br>
Flings its glamour and its grace !<br>
“Lo ! a light is on thy face,<br>
Dost thou know the mighty Brahman ?<br>
Only those who know the Brahman<br>
With such light and radiance shine ?”</div></div><h4 class="sec-h3">10.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">“Father ! I have sought to know,”—<br>
Sweetly answered thus the youth,—<br>
“Not from men I gathered knowledge,<br>
Nature whispered to me truth !<br>
The earth, the quarters of the sky,<br>
The lofty heavens, the ocean broad,<br>
The sun and moon, the mind and soul,—<br>
All, all is Brahman,—all is God !”</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Abridged from Chhândogya Upanishad</em>, IV. 4.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-22-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-22-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body">The fuel is required for the rite of <em>initiating</em> a boy in the Vedas and the sacred learning of old.</span></li></ol></section>
