<span class="line"><span class="sc">Satyakâma</span>,—truthful boy,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Poor Jabâlâ’s humble son,</span><br>
<span class="line">Felt a passion in his soul</span><br>
<span class="line">For the truth and truth alone!</span><br>
<span class="line">So he came unto his mother,</span><br>
<span class="line">Asked her of his father’s name,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Mother! I would be a student,</span><br>
<span class="line">Tell me of what race I am.”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">2.</span><br>
<span class="line">Jabâlâ the humble woman</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke in grief but spoke the truth,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Sinfully I long have wandered,</span><br>
<span class="line">And conceived thee in my youth!</span><br>
<span class="line">And I know not who thy father,</span><br>
<span class="line">Know not of what race thou art,</span><br>
<span class="line">By the name of thy poor mother</span><br>
<span class="line">Call thyself, child of my heart!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-96"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="96" data-folio="58" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n95_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 58">[p. 58]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">3.</span><br>
<span class="line">Satyakâma,—truthful boy,—</span><br>
<span class="line">To Gautama went the youth,</span><br>
<span class="line">“I would be a student, Sir,</span><br>
<span class="line">For I seek to know the truth.”</span><br>
<span class="line">Gautama a welcome gave</span><br>
<span class="line">To the boy who to him came,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Tell me of what race thou art,</span><br>
<span class="line">Tell me what may be thy name.”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">4.</span><br>
<span class="line">Satyakâma,—truthful boy,—</span><br>
<span class="line">Spoke in grief but spoke the truth,</span><br>
<span class="line">“Sinfully my mother wandered,</span><br>
<span class="line">And conceived me in her youth.</span><br>
<span class="line">And I know not who my father,</span><br>
<span class="line">Know not of what race I am,</span><br>
<span class="line">Jabâlâ is my poor mother,</span><br>
<span class="line">Jabâlâ must be my name.”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">5.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Enough, enough, my truthful boy,”—</span><br>
<span class="line">Spake the sage unto the youth,—</span><br>
<span class="line">“By your worth you are a Brâhman,</span><br>
<span class="line">For you dare to speak the truth!</span><br>
<span class="line">Go and fetch the sacred fuel,</span><br>
<span class="line">I will teach you, noble youth,</span><br>
<span class="line">In the learning of our fathers,</span><br>
<span class="line">For you have not swerved from truth!”</span></div></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-97"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="97" data-folio="59" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n96_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 59">[p. 59]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">6.</span><br>
<span class="line">Satyakâma brought the fuel,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-22-fn-001" href="#sec-22-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-22-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></span><br>
<span class="line">Was a student young and brave,</span><br>
<span class="line">And he kept his teacher’s cattle,</span><br>
<span class="line">Served his teacher as his slave.</span><br>
<span class="line">And unto the pathless meadows</span><br>
<span class="line">With the cattle went the youth,</span><br>
<span class="line">In his high and eager bosom</span><br>
<span class="line">Nature flashed the light of truth!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">7.</span><br>
<span class="line">From the strong and noble bull</span><br>
<span class="line">Of the herd that he was tending,</span><br>
<span class="line">From the evening’s faggot fire</span><br>
<span class="line">Over which he pondered bending,</span><br>
<span class="line">From the gay and bright flamingo</span><br>
<span class="line">As it sailed across the sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">From the diver-bird so beauteous</span><br>
<span class="line">Which unto him was not shy,</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">8.</span><br>
<span class="line">From the anxious contemplation</span><br>
<span class="line">Which for ever filled his mind,</span><br>
<span class="line">When the evening fire was lighted</span><br>
<span class="line">And the cattle had been penned,</span><br>
<span class="line"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-98" data-page="98" data-folio="60" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/laysofancientind00dutt/page/n97_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 60">[p. 60]</button>From each humble, lowly object,</span><br>
<span class="line">Came its meaning to the youth,</span><br>
<span class="line">From great Nature’s mighty wonders</span><br>
<span class="line">Flashed on him the light of truth!</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">9.</span><br>
<span class="line">And to Gautama he came,</span><br>
<span class="line">And a light was on his face!</span><br>
<span class="line">For a knowledge of the truth</span><br>
<span class="line">Flings its glamour and its grace!</span><br>
<span class="line">“Lo! a light is on thy face,</span><br>
<span class="line">Dost thou know the mighty Brahman?</span><br>
<span class="line">Only those who know the Brahman</span><br>
<span class="line">With such light and radiance shine?”</span></div></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza"><span class="stanza-no">10.</span><br>
<span class="line">“Father! I have sought to know,”—</span><br>
<span class="line">Sweetly answered thus the youth,—</span><br>
<span class="line">“Not from men I gathered knowledge,</span><br>
<span class="line">Nature whispered to me truth!</span><br>
<span class="line">The earth, the quarters of the sky,</span><br>
<span class="line">The lofty heavens, the ocean broad,</span><br>
<span class="line">The sun and moon, the mind and soul,—</span><br>
