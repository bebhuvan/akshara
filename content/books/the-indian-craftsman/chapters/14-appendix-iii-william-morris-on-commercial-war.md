The South Sea Islander must leave <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-129" data-page="129" data-folio="107" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/indiancraftsman00coomuoft/page/n128_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 107">[p. 107]</button> his canoe-carving, his sweet rest, and his graceful dances, and become a slave of a slave: trousers, shoddy, rum, missionary and fatal disease—he must swallow all this civilisation in a lump, and neither himself nor we can help him now till social order displaces the hideous tyranny of gambling that has ruined him.’’<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-14-fn-001" href="#sec-14-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-14-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-14-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-14-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">*.</a> <span class="fn-body"><em>William Morris</em>, “<em>Signs of Change</em>,” <em>p</em>. 10.</span></li></ol></section>
