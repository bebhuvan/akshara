<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-661"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="661" data-folio="647" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naor/page/n660_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 647">[p. 647]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">XII.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><strong>THE INDIAN TRIBUTE.</strong></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The following is the full text of a letter sent by Mr. Dadabhai Naoroji to the <em>Daily News</em> :—</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">22, Kennington Road, S. E., April 3, 1905.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Sir</span>,—In the <em>Daily News</em> of 31st ult. a correspondent, “A Reader,” asks for information about the yearly drain of £30,000,000 from India. Will you kindly allow me to give it? I have given this explanation two or three times before, as may be seen in my book; but I now bring the figures up to date.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Any drain from, or addition to, the wealth of a country in connexion with other countries takes place through the channel of commerce. I give an approximate calculation.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In order to have a fair average, I take figures for ten years; but I leave out the years 1899-1900 and 1900-1, as these two years were those of famine in India, and were, therefore, not of average normal condition.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I take United Kingdom for the same ten years, <em>viz.</em>, 1892 to 1899 and 1902 and 1903. (The latest figures available are till 1903. Parl. Ret. Cd. 2192—1904.)</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The total imports of the United Kingdom for these ten years (merchandise and treasure) are £4,988,919,359.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The total exports for the same period (merchandise and treasure) are £3,421,478,153.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This shows an excess of imports over exports, or, in other words, the profits on the exports as £1,567,441,206. That is to say, the United Kingdom received back the whole amount of its exports (£3,421,478,153), and also <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-662" data-page="662" data-folio="648" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naor/page/n661_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 648">[p. 648]</button> over and above that £1,567,441,206 more as an addition to its wealth by all its international transactions with foreign countries during the ten years.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Thus, the United Kingdom made a profit of 45·8 per cent. over its exports.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I would make, however, the following allowance :— The total profit of £1,567,441,206 includes, taking roughly, £300,000,000 in ten years of the political drain of India. Deducting this £300,000,000 from the above profit leaves the net profit of its transactions with other countries as £1,267,441,206 independently of the drain from India. This deduction reduces the percentage of the profit of the United Kingdom from 45·8 to 37 per cent. on its own exports.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I now take India. (Parl. Ret. Cd. 2299—1904.) The total exports (including Native States) of merchandise and treasure during the ten years are Rx. 1,180,665,000. To this must be added freight and insurance on exports to the United Kingdom, because they are paid in the United Kingdom, and not included in the invoices and official returns. This was the case when I was in business in the city. I do not know how the case is for exports to other countries, so I do not add this item. I take roughly for freight and insurance on exports to the United Kingdom from India for the ten years at 5 per cent. The amount of exports is Rx. 364,948, 240, and 5 per cent. on it will be Rx. 18,247,412. This addition will make the total of exports from India to be Rx. 1,180,665,000, plus Rx. 18,247,412, = Rx. 1,198,912,412.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The next item to be considered is the profit on the total exports. Though the profits of the United Kingdom, as stated above are 37 per cent., I take for India a profit of only 20 per cent. The total, therefore, of exports and <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-663" data-page="663" data-folio="649" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naor/page/n662_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 649">[p. 649]</button> profit will be for the ten years Rx. 1,198,912,412 plus profit 239,782,482, = Rx, 1,438,694,894.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This, then, is the amount equal to which India ought to have imported under normal circumstances like those of the United Kingdom.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">But India has not imported this amount, but only a much less amount of Rx. 923,205,000, leaving a drain or deprivation of Rx. 515,489,894 in the ten years.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Taking the present exchange of Rs. 15 to £1, this drain in ten years amounts to £343,659,920, or, say, average of £34,000,000 every year.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">If the exports and imports of the Native States are excluded, the drain from British India will be larger than £34,000,000 a year. Besides this, there is the burden of foreign debt inflicted on India without India's voice.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Now, one thing must be carefully borne in mind--that the people in India have not the slightest voice in the administration which is producing such disastrous results. The rule is absolute despotism.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Here, then, is a strange and sad contrast. The United Kingdom and India are governed by the same Government, with the result of bringing to the United Kingdom an addition to its wealth, as profits of its exports, in ten years, of £1,267,441,206, and, on the other hand, causing to India in the same ten years a deprivation and loss of £343,659,920.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Not only this. The loss to India must be measured by how much more India would have benefited had this enormous drain of the ten years and all drain of previous years been at India's own disposal and fructified in the Indians' pockets. It must be further remembered that what Europeans consume in India itself, to the deprivation of the Indians, is not included in this drain. Truly has Macaulay said: "Of all forms of tyranny I believe that the worst is that of a nation over a nation."</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The present evil system of the government of India is that kind of tyranny.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Yours faithfully,</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Dadabhai Naoroji</span>.</p></div>
