<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-17"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="17" data-folio="xi" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/lifelettersoftor00duttuoft/page/n16_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page xi">[p. xi]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">AUTHOR’S PREFACE</h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Biography</span> is a subject which until recently Indian writers have somewhat neglected. The whole trend of India’s history and philosophy has been against it. This is of itself sufficient to account for the fact that, although it is now forty-three years since Toru Dutt’s death, no biography of her has yet been written. But now that India is beginning to realize her own solidarity as an Empire, along with the birth of this consciousness she has begun to cherish the memory of those who have added to the literary wealth, not only of India, but also of the world.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I feel, then, that no further apology is needed from me for this attempt to do honour to an Indian maiden, in truth an ‘inheritor of unfulfilled renown’, in view not only of what was lost to the world through her early death, but also of the comparative oblivion into which her name has sunk.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">It was during boyhood, in my native village at Sidhipasa, that I first heard the name of Toru Dutt from a student in whose examination text-book her poem ‘Buttoo’ was included. After reading these verses a desire to know something of its author was immediately born in me—a desire that was for several <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-18" data-page="18" data-folio="xii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/lifelettersoftor00duttuoft/page/n17_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page xii">[p. xii]</button> years to remain unsatisfied. Then, on glancing through a Bengali book, which contained sketches of famous Indian women, I found one of Toru Dutt. It was, however, so incomplete that it merely served to whet my appetite for still further knowledge.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">A few years later, on scanning the titles of the books in my father’s library, I found a beautiful volume entitled <em>A Sheaf gleaned in French Fields</em> by Toru Dutt. Seizing it eagerly, I found in it a prefatory memoir written by her father. Even this was not full enough to allay my thirst for a thorough acquaintance with the biography of my heroine, and at this juncture the idea of writing myself what I could not find elsewhere began to take shape in my mind.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In December 1911, I began the task of collecting materials for the biography, and was fortunate in obtaining an introduction to some of Toru Dutt’s relatives in Calcutta. Through them I was placed in touch with Miss Martin, who had known Toru intimately in Cambridge. In reply to my request for reminiscences, she wrote, ‘I am much interested in hearing that you are wishing to bring out a memoir of my dear friend, Toru Dutt ; I have already begun my notes for it. It is very wonderful after these many years of silence, that people should be beginning to think of her again.’</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Miss Martin’s visit to India in the winter of 1913 gave me an invaluable opportunity of gaining the information I sought. Through her kindness I am able to publish Toru’s letters, which had been treasured with zealous <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-19" data-page="19" data-folio="xiii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/lifelettersoftor00duttuoft/page/n18_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page xiii">[p. xiii]</button> care for so many years. My indebtedness to Miss Martin for her unvarying kindness in giving me access to all the available material in her possession, in addition to her constant and stimulating encouragement, cannot be over-estimated. Suffice it to say, that without this help the biography would never have been written. The correspondence between Toru and Mlle Bader concerning her <em>La Femme dans l'Inde Antique</em> has been translated and added to the appendix.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I should like to thank my friends, Mr. and Mrs. Barun Chunder Dutt, and other members of the Dutt family who have supplied me with useful information. Grateful acknowledgements too are due to another friend, Principal E. J. Thompson, B.A., M.C., of the Wesleyan College, Bankura, Bengal, who has revised the MS. and written the supplementary review which appears in this book. Mr. P. C. Lyon, C.S.I., sometime Member for Education on the Bengal Executive Council, the late Bishop Lefroy of Calcutta, and my friend Mr. G. C. Ghose, President, Christian Convention League, Bengal, have also laid me under a debt by their kindness in giving advice, encouragement, and many valuable suggestions.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">I have also to thank Mr. Edmund Gosse for permission to quote from the Introductory Memoir to the <em>Ancient Ballads of Hindustan</em>, and the following publishers for permission to quote from the books mentioned after their names : Messrs. Longmans, Green &amp; Co. (<em>The Dutt Family Album</em>); Messrs. Kegan Paul &amp; Co. (<em>Ancient Ballads of Hindustan</em>); and <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-20" data-page="20" data-folio="xiv" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/lifelettersoftor00duttuoft/page/n19_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page xiv">[p. xiv]</button> Messrs. T. Fisher Unwin, Ltd. (Mr. R. W. Frazer’s <em>Literary History of India</em>).</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">‘Read no history : nothing but biography, for that is life without theory,’ said Disraeli, and I venture to commend this Memoir to my readers, in the hope that they may find in it an illustration of the truth of that maxim.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">H. DAS.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">London.</span> <em>January 20, 1920.</em></p></div>
