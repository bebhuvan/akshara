<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-210"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="210" data-folio="172" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naiduoft/page/n209_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 172">[p. 172]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>The following speech was delivered by Mrs. Sarojini Naidu on the occasion of the prize distribution at the Kanya Maha Vidyalaya, Jullundur, on the 31st March 1918.</em></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">My Punjabi Friends,</span>—You have all seen in your cities, it is a very common sight in India, the wandering singer with a stick on his shoulder with two bundles tied on its each end going from city to city singing his songs. I stand before you to-day as a wandering singer like that with all my possessions carried in my two bundles—one a little bundle of dreams and another a growing bundle of hopes. These are the only two things which I have in this world.</p></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">A WANDERING SINGER</span></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Exactly like a wandering singer I, too, am going from city to city and like him, too, I have always something to dream about every new city that I visit. My dreams have not always proved shattered illusions but they have come some time as realized hopes too. To-day we in India stand upon the verge of destiny, a glorious destiny (<em>Cheers</em>). This is no mere nightmare, no mere poet’s dream, but the dawn is already in sight, the glorious dawn that would light up the eastern sky and fill it with light (<em>Loud cheers</em>).</p></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">THE PROMISE OF THE DAWN</span></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Every province, every part of India, has its own peculiar characteristics, symbols you may call them the promise of what the dawn is to bring and my little dreams are clustered round these symbols and promise. When I think of the great Maharashtra, I dream not of the glory <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-211" data-page="211" data-folio="173" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naiduoft/page/n210_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 173">[p. 173]</button> of the Peshwas, not of the clashing swords, not of the warring hordes of Shivaji, but I see and think of the Fergusson College of Poona with its noble band of professors working, silently and selflessly, for the educational progress of the Maharashtra. In Madras, I see the little College at Masulipatam with its burning idealism trying to hold up all that is noblest and purest in our national life. In the United Provinces, it is not Benares, the Eternal City, the holy Kasi that I dream of, nor do I dream of the glories of Oudh, the splendour of its rulers that are no more, but I dream of the two streams which like the great Ganges and the Jumna are going to meet and mingle with each other, the two streams from the Aligarh Muslim and the Benares Hindu Universities (<em>Cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Her Dream of the Punjab</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">But what is the dream that I dream here in the Punjab? Not the great military traditions of the province, nor the battles it has fought and won have I dreamt of; my first vision of the Punjab has been this cloister, this sanctuary, this stronghold of the women of the North. The women of the Punjab who are to be the redemption of this land of the five rivers, greeted me first in the Punjab and I would never forget their kindness and their welcome (<em>Cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Her Maiden Speech</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Eleven years ago, the Principal of this School (<em>cheers</em>), a Saraswati in reality, a little, frail, timid Hindu widow clad in her simple white garb came on a pilgrimage to my city of Hyderabad. I remember to this day, I vividly and distinctly remember the day when she stood there amidst hundreds of Hindus and Muhammadans, appealing for her school. It was on this occasion eleven years ago, that <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-212" data-page="212" data-folio="174" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naiduoft/page/n211_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 174">[p. 174]</button> delivered my first speech, on the request of my friends, to say something. I never dreamt that day that I would come eleven years later to the same school on whose behalf I delivered my maiden speech (<em>Cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">THE MOST HOPEFUL SIGN</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The most hopeful sign that meets my eye in this institution is the stand that it has taken on national grounds, the roots that it has laid on the soils of the country, taking its inspiration from its past and yet living in the present. Had I said that the school was an ideal one in every respect, I would be paying not a tribute but sounding its death-knell. Imperfections it has, but it is perfect because it is true to the past and yet alive to the modern conditions of life (<em>Cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">THE PRESENT SYSTEM OF WOMEN’S EDUCATION</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">We often hear, not without a taunt, that the education of girls during the last three generations has been a failure. It could not but be so, it would have been strange if it had not been so. It could not be fruitful because it went away from our traditions and ideals. Our educationists are now awake to the fact that education should and can only be on national lines. We have produced exceptional women and brilliant women, too, not because of the present system of education but in spite of it.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">OUR STANDARD OF WOMEN’S EDUCATION</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">If we want to reconstruct our educational system, it must be along a course which would continue to preserve the best traditions of the East and West. Our standard of education of Indian women should be a normal average. Not that one of our women should be pointed out with admiration as a wonderful and a brilliant woman for her culture and attainments, but rather people <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-213" data-page="213" data-folio="175" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naiduoft/page/n212_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 175">[p. 175]</button> should point out with horror at an illiterate woman in India.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Only this morning I was reading in one of your daily papers of what Lord Haldane recently said in connection with the granting of voting rights to the women of England. He said that the day is not very distant when people in England would wonder at their refusal to grant the parliamentary rights to women just as they now wonder as to how people kept slaves in the past. I think that time would also soon come to India when we too would wonder as to how we could keep out women in ignorance.</p></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">CHARACTERISTICS OF INDIAN WOMANHOOD</span></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Remember that woman does not merely keep the hearth-fire of your homes burning, but she keeps also the beaconfire of national life aflame. It is she who keeps the soldier-heart in time of battle and the priest-heart at the time of peace (<em>Cheers</em>). The power of self-surrender and self-realization had been the typical characteristics of Indian womanhood. This dual capacity of the personal and impersonal in her relation to man had always marked the Indian women. In this institution, too, I find manifest that spirit of self-surrender, joyous self-surrender, and self-realization. These are the qualities that make the Indian women great and these are the qualities that I am glad to find in this Vidyalaya (<em>Loud Cheers</em>).</p></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">THE COMING WOMEN OF INDIA</span></h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">To-day, we who dream dreams of the coming women of India have our hopes centred round institutions like this (<em>cheers</em>), institutions like that of Professor Karve at Poona, not the institutions that only slavishly imitate men’s college but the institutions that would send forth to <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-214" data-page="214" data-folio="176" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/speecheswritings00naiduoft/page/n213_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 176">[p. 176]</button> the world women not merely brought up and fed in the dry pages of lifeless books but rather women trained in the beauties and necessities of life. These women would go forth not bearing the burden of dead knowledge but culture transmuted in the services of humanity (<em>Loud cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">COMRADESHIP OF MEN AND WOMEN</h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The historic significance of this crowd gathered here to-day lies not in its number for I have addressed crowds five times larger than this; but its significance lies in the presence of the very large number of women that are gathered here. Their presence here is the indication of the coming comradeship between men and women in India. The old partition between <em>Mardana</em> and <em>Zenana</em> is broken down for ever. It is in the comradeship of sexes that future India shall come out man and woman working hand in hand and supplementing each other (<em>Cheers</em>).</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">FAREWELL</h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Friends, to-morrow, again, I shall fare forth as a singing wanderer with my two bundles of hopes and dreams but never, never shall I forget this institution of yours which is destined to take its legitimate place in the history of the regeneration of India with the promise, the guarantee, almost the realization of the high ideal that it stands for (<em>Loud and prolonged cheers</em>).</p></div>
