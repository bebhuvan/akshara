<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-9"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="9" data-folio="iii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/imperialgazette02hunt/page/n8_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page iii">[p. iii]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">PREFACE</h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">The</span> present volume differs from the three other volumes of ‘The Indian Empire,’ in that for the most part it has been planned and written in England, and therefore does not bear the same official sanction of the Government of India.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">When dealing with so vast a subject as the history of India, it has been held necessary to divide it into chapters which relate not so much to separate periods of time as to the separate sources from which the materials are derived, and to entrust these chapters to different authors with special qualifications. Such important branches of historical investigation as epigraphy, numismatics, archaeology, and architecture are thus introduced into their proper place as preliminary to the chapters based upon written records, while the available evidence from both Sanskrit and vernacular literature has likewise been included. It must, however, be admitted that this method of treating the subject possesses certain inherent disadvantages. The matter of the several chapters cannot be marked off by rigid lines. For example, inscriptions comprise those on coins, and the origin of both building and sculpture is to be sought in prehistoric times. So again when the days of history proper have been reached. Periods that may conveniently be distinguished overlap one another in fact, while Northern and Southern India can hardly be brought within the same focus. It must also be borne in mind that large portions of the early history of India are still the field of conjecture and controversy, where scholars of equal eminence hold divergent views. Consequently, there may be found in the present volume some lack of logic in <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-10" data-page="10" data-folio="iv" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/imperialgazette02hunt/page/n9_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page iv">[p. iv]</button> arrangement, a certain amount of repetition, and possibly a few inconsistencies of statement. It has been thought better to admit such apparent defects than to attempt a strict uniformity, which would only produce results inadequate and misleading. In particular, the editor has not felt it his duty to demand that the contributors should all follow a conventional spelling of Indian names and words.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The names of the authors are appended to their several chapters, but it may be desirable to enumerate them here :—</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Chapter I, ‘ Epigraphy,’ has been written by Dr. J. Smith, late I.C.S., author of <em>The Early History of India</em> ; Chapter V, ‘ Architecture,’ by Dr. James Burgess, C.I.E., formerly Director-General of the Archaeological Survey of India ; Chapter VI, ‘ Sanskrit Literature,’ by Dr. A. A.
