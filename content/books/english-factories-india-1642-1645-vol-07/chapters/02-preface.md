Except for one letter on p. 183 (of slight importance), which comes from the <em>Egerton MSS.</em> at the British Museum, they are all drawn from the India Office archives. Neither the Public Record Office at home nor any of the Indian Record Offices has been able to contribute to the collection.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The period synchronizes roughly with the first phase of the Civil War in England, from the raising of the royal standard at Nottingham in August, 1642, to the battle of Naseby in June, 1645; and the consequent difficulty with which the East India Company's operations were carried on at home of course affected to some extent the proceedings of their servants in the East. Yet on the whole the embarrassment caused to the latter was smaller than might have been expected. Each year ships arrived from England, though with reduced cargoes, and each year one or more went back; and the most startling effect of the war on the Company's trade was the loss of the <em>John</em>, which in 1644 was betrayed to the royalists by her commander. In India itself the factors, though much hampered by debt and the lack of sufficient supplies from home, managed to keep going the trade with Mokha, Basrā, Gombroon, Achin, <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-10" data-page="10" data-folio="iv" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories07fost/page/n9_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page iv">[p. iv]</button> Bantam, and other places; while in addition they dispatched ships from Surat to Macao and Manilla—the latter a pioneer venture which met with less success than it deserved. On the Coromandel Coast and in Bengal English commerce was making little headway; but this was largely due to the disturbed state of the Vijayanagar kingdom, now tottering to its fall.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The frontispiece consists of a map of the Malabar and Coromandel Coasts at this period, intended to supplement the map of Northern India given in the first volume of the present series (1618–21).</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The index has been compiled by Mr. H. G. Bull, of the Record Department of the India Office.</p></div>
