<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-15"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="15" data-folio="v" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/religionveda00bloouoft/page/n14_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page v">[p. v]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">The</span> American Lectures on the History of Religions are delivered under the auspices of the American Committee for Lectures on the History of Religions. This Committee was organised in 1892, for the purpose of instituting “popular courses in the History of Religions, somewhat after the style of the Hibbert Lectures in England, to be delivered annually by the best scholars of Europe and this country, in various cities, such as Baltimore, Boston, Brooklyn, Chicago, New York, Philadelphia, and others.”</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The terms of association under which the Committee exist are as follows:</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><ul class="editorial-list"><li>1. The object of this Association shall be to provide courses of lectures on the history of religions, to be delivered in various cities.</li><li>2. The Association shall be composed of delegates from Institutions agreeing to co-operate, or from Local Boards organised where such co-operation is not possible.</li><li>3. These delegates—one from each Institution or <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-18" data-page="18" data-folio="vi" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/religionveda00bloouoft/page/n17_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page vi">[p. vi]</button> Local Board — shall constitute themselves a Council under the name of the “American Committee for Lectures on the History of Religions.”</li><li>4. The Council shall elect out of its number a Chairman, a Secretary, and a Treasurer.</li><li>5. All matters of local detail shall be left to the Institutions or Local Boards, under whose auspices the lectures are to be delivered.</li><li>6. A course of lectures on some religion, or phase of religion, from an historical point of view, or on a subject germane to the study of religions, shall be delivered annually, or at such intervals as may be found practicable, in the different cities represented by this Association.</li><li>7. The Council (<em>a</em>) shall be charged with the selection of the lecturers, (<em>b</em>) shall have charge of the funds, (<em>c</em>) shall assign the time for the lectures in each city, and perform such other functions as may be necessary.</li><li>8. Polemical subjects, as well as polemics in the treatment of subjects, shall be positively excluded.</li><li>9. The lecturer shall be chosen by the Council at least ten months before the date fixed for the course of lectures.</li><li>10. The lectures shall be delivered in the various cities between the months of September and June.</li><li>11. The copyright of the lectures shall be the property of the Association.</li></ul></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-19"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="19" data-folio="vii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/religionveda00bloouoft/page/n18_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page vii">[p. vii]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><ul class="editorial-list"><li>12. One-half of the lecturer’s compensation shall be paid at the completion of the entire course and the second half upon the publication of the lectures.</li><li>13. The compensation to the lecturer shall be fixed in each case by the Council.</li><li>14. The lecturer is not to deliver elsewhere any of the lectures for which he is engaged by the Committee, except with the sanction of the Committee.</li></ul></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The Committee as now constituted is as follows: Prof. Crawford H. F. Harper, University of Chicago, Chicago, Ill.; Prof. Paul Haupt, 2511 Madison Avenue, Baltimore, Md.; Prof. F. W. Hooper, Brooklyn Institute, Brooklyn, N. Y.; Prof. E. W. Hopkins, New Haven, Conn.; Prof. Edward Knox Mitchell, Hartford Theological Seminary, Hartford, Conn.; Prof. George F. Moore, Cambridge, Mass.; Rev. F. K. Sanders, Boston, Mass.; Pres. F. C. Southworth, Meadville Theological Seminary, Meadville, Pa.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The lecturers in the course of American Lectures <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-20" data-page="20" data-folio="viii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/religionveda00bloouoft/page/n19_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page viii">[p. viii]</button> on the History of Religions and the titles of their volumes are as follows:</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1894–1895—Prof. <strong>T. D. —The Development of Religion in Japan.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The present course of lectures, the seventh in the series, was delivered by Professor Maurice Bloomfield, Ph.D., LL.D., Professor of Sanskrit and Comparative Philology at the Johns Hopkins University, and one of the leading authorities on Vedic Literature. His latest work, a Concordance of the Vedic hymns and prayer formulæ, covering 1100 pages, the embodiment of a life’s study, published as Vol. 10 of the Harvard Oriental Series, will ensure Professor Bloomfield a permanent place in the history of Vedic studies. Besides this he has edited from the manuscripts the Vedic ritual book, known as the Kauçika-Sutra; published a translation of the Hymns of the Atharva-Veda, included in Professor Max Müller’s Sacred Books of <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-21" data-page="21" data-folio="ix" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/religionveda00bloouoft/page/n20_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page ix">[p. ix]</button> the East (Oxford, 1897); written a volume on the Literature and History of the Atharva-Veda, entitled: “The Atharva-Veda and the Gopatha-Brahmana” (Strassburg, 1889); and edited, in collaboration with Professor Richard Garbe of Tübingen, a chromophotographic reproduction of the unique birch-bark manuscript of the Kashmirian Atharva-Veda (3 vols., Baltimore, 1901). He has also contributed to the technical journals of this country and Europe numerous papers on linguistic, mythological, and ethnological topics in general, in addition to a large number of contributions on the interpretation, textual restoration, and religion of the Veda in particular.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The lectures in this course were delivered before the Johns Hopkins University, Baltimore; Union Theological Seminary, New York; Brooklyn Institute of Arts and Sciences, Brooklyn; Drexel Institute, Philadelphia; Meadville Theological Seminary, Meadville; University of Chicago, Chicago; and Hartford Theological Seminary, Hartford.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">John P.
