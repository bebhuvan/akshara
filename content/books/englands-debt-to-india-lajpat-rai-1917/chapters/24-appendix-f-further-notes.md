<span id="page-397" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><h2 class="sec-h1">APPENDIX F</h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">FURTHER NOTES</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Gold Value of Rupee.</em> Until 1871 the gold value of the rupee except in one year always exceeded 1s. 11d. In 1872-3 it fell to a little over 1s. 10¾d. and thenceforward downward until in 1894-5 it reached 1s. 1d. The difference it made to India may be judged from the fact that the sterling value of the bills paid <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-398" data-page="398" data-folio="358" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englandsdebttoin00lajpuoft/page/n397_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 358">[p. 358]</button> in England, in 1894–5, was £15,770,533. The rupee equivalent actually paid by the Government of India was 28·9 crores (or 280·9 millions) of rupees while at the rate prevailing in 1872–3 it would have amounted to only 16·6 crores. In this way India suffered a high financial loss. The sterling debt contracted at the time when the value of the rupee was about 2s. was afterwards converted into a rupee debt when the value of the rupee had fallen to 1s. 4d.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Says Mr. A. J. Wilson (“An Empire in Pawn”), p. 26: “The Indian people pay altogether more now than ever they did. More of the net proceeds of their labour goes every year to pay the foreign debt charges <em>under one head or another</em>, because the aggregate of these charges increases.”</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">“The official mind has created a cloud-world of its own and looks at all Indian affairs from a point of view so far above everything native, so conventional and entirely bureaucratic, that it is easily able to demonstrate to us a <em>priori</em> that Indian populations are happy and flourishing, though millions of them be dead of starvation or to gush about loyalty with a mutiny and massacre hanging on their heads.”</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">(P. 28.) “The Stracheys and men of that official set present only the outside of the sepulchre to view. There is an official India where all is well, an India serenely indifferent to the toiling India, and there is an India composed of nearly 200 millions of toiling and suffering people.”</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">(P. 28.) “The truth of the matter is, that the natives of India are in no sense their own masters in the conduct of their trade any more than in the conduct of their government. Our system of land revenue alone would bring, and does bring them into a state of slavery and abject dependence, almost whether we like it or not.”</p></div>
