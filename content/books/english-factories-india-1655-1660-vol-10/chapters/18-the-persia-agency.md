<span id="page-180" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">The</span> non-survival of any letter written from Persia during this year and the general silence of the Surat Council on the subject prevent us from learning what the three factors—Andrews, Daniel, and Bell—who were there at the opening of 1658, were <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-181" data-page="181" data-folio="169" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n180_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 169">[p. 169]</button> doing during its course. On the other hand we have full information concerning the views of the Company in the spring of 1658 on the policy to be pursued in that country, as set forth in the instructions given to William Garway,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-18-fn-001" href="#sec-18-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-18-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> who, as we have seen (p. 142), had been selected for the post of Agent in Persia. Garway went out to Surat with President Wyche, and thence proceeded in the <em>Society</em> to Gombroon (Oct. 1658), accompanied by his second, Nicholas Buckeridge; probably also by George Morton, whom the Company had designated for employment in Persia. They reached their destination on 29 November. Garway had brought with him from London detailed instructions from the Company, and to these Wyche and his Council had added others. The former (6 April, 1658) contained, besides general admonitions, the following special instructions:</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote><p>When it shall please God you shall arrive in Persia, endeavour to continue and encrease a faire correspondencie betweene the English and Persians; and to that purpose, in a convenient time, to make addresses to the King, intimating unto him the Companies expectation to receive the moyetie of the customes in Gombroone, according to the severall phirmaunds given unto them by his predecessors, a coppie whereof is here inclosed.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-18-fn-002" href="#sec-18-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-18-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup></p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote><p>Copies should be obtained of the privileges granted by the king to the Dutch; and these, with any other particulars likely to be useful, should be dispatched home, both overland and via Surat, for the consideration of the Company.</p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote><p>In the meane time that you use all dilligence and carefullnes in the having an inspection at the custome howse in Gombroone, in the collecting and sattisfying of yourselves what the amount of that custome may annually bee; which wee are enformed by advice from Surratt in the yeare 1651 the custome only of the goods from that port of Surratt amounted to 89,000<em>l.</em> in the whole, whereof one moyetie is 44,500<em>l.</em>, besides the custome of goods from <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-182" data-page="182" data-folio="170" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n181_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 170">[p. 170]</button> other places; and in the yeare Mr. Spiller was in Persia the officers for the King received for custome for that one yeare to the amount of 118,000<em>l.</em> Therefore wee desire you to treate, and fairely and peaceably to demaund the full moyetie of the said customes for our accompt. However, refuse not to accept of such parte thereof as they shall tender unto you; yet capitulating with them for the encreasing thereof as much as may be, alleadging such arguments and reasons unto them as you shall conceive may be most probable to incline them to enlarge our proportion of the said customes. And this to continue untill from the Company you shall receive further directions and instructions.</p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The Committees, however, did not rely entirely upon the eloquence of Garway and his companions, for they also tried their own persuasive powers in a letter (14 April) which they delivered to the factors for presentation to the Persian king. This was addressed to ‘the high and mightie monarch, Shaw Abbas, Emperour of Persia, Media, Armenia, and other great and populous countries and dominions’; and it commenced by humbly reminding His Majesty of the agreement made by his royal predecessor with the English at the time of the capture of Ormus.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Now may it please Your Majestie to take knowledge that, notwithstanding by our assistance the conquest was obteyned, with the expence of much blood and treasure, to the great advantage of the crowne of Persia, yet have wee not received yearelie the one tenth part of what hath arisen by the said customes, and which ought in honnor and justice to have been duely paid unto us. Wee are most willing to beleive that this hath been acted, not only without Your Majesties knowledge, but also contrarie to your royall will and pleasure; and therefore earnestly desire that Your Majestie will now be pleased to impose such strict commands and injunctions upon your ministers and officers, who are or shall be entrusted with the manadgment of this affaire, that for the future a more due and just observance may be had to the performance of the said articles, and that wee, by our agents, may yearlie receive the full moyetie of the said customes, according to the true intent and meaning of the said agreement. As wee have a just and undeniable interest and proprietie to the said moyetie of customes, soe is it verie consistent with equitie and reason that wee should demaund and expect the same, and doubt not but that Your Majestie will be pleased to enjoyne and command its performance; which will be acknowledged for a singuler act of your justice, and eternize your name in the world amongst the nomber of the most famous and glorious princes.</p></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-183"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="183" data-folio="171" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n182_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 171">[p. 171]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The instructions given by the President and Council (16 Oct.) were naturally of a more practical character. These enjoined upon the factors the speedy appraisement and taking over of the goods belonging to the United Joint Stock; promised an early supply of Indian commodities; and gave directions for the sale of their English broadcloth.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Wee are informed that the Companies broker, Jechand, hath been imprisoned, and that the Shawbunder hath taken from him 1500 tomand, for his unjust practises to countenaunce straungers goods in Englishmens names; which wee desire you will verie seriouslie enquire into. And although you ought to require justice, if it hath been wrongfully extorted from him (being the Companies servant), yet if you find that hee is guiltie of so great an offence, wee conceive it but just that hee should pay the Companie the same summ, and require you to force it from him. Hee hath, wee heare, a verie ill name; and it is reported that hee will not sufferr anie merchant to come into the Companies howse but whome hee pleases for to buy goods, and those shall be his owne creatures; so that the Companie cannot have the benefitt of the marketts that other men have. This wee desire you will search into; and if you find him guiltie of this or anie unjust dealing, wee give you full power to turne him out of the Companies service, and imploy another that shall be more faithfull.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Reference was then made to an account obtained from the Surat custom-house of the value of goods exported to Persia during the past two years; and similar information was promised yearly, ‘that you maie the better demand from them the Companies right’. The letter intended for the king was made over to Garway; but he was told that it would be necessary at the same time to offer presents to His Majesty and to the Itimād-uddaula. He was instructed to ascertain (and advise by the <em>Society</em>) what English goods would be most suitable for that purpose; these would then be forwarded by the next ship, and would ‘arrive time enough for you to carry up along with you’. The <em>Society</em> was to be sent back to Surat as quickly as possible, with any freight she could procure, calling on her way at Lahrībandar, in Sind.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">If Mr. Matthew Andrews be not come awaie from Gombrone before you arrive, pray acquaint him the Companie hath graunted his desire for to leave Persia, and hath appointed him to be here Generall Purser of the Marine. Wee desire, therfore, hee will not faile to retourne hether by this shipp to take that charge upon him.</p></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-184"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="184" data-folio="172" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n183_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 172">[p. 172]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Garway was then asked to supply Surat with five or six horses. The factory had only one, ‘and that scarse worth the riding’; and it was hoped that the profit made by selling the rest would pay the cost of the one retained. Care was to be taken to see that the freight goods on board the <em>Society</em> were not landed until the freight money and customs had been paid.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">By the two years accompt, which with some difficultie wee have procured from the officers of this custome howse, it doth appeare that in anno 1656 here was cleared for Persia to the amount of 332,000<em>l.</em> sterling worth of goods, and the last year 112,500<em>l.</em>; which together is 444,500<em>l.</em> At 10 per cent. custome (as is paid in Gombrone) [this] amounts to 44,450<em>l.</em> The Companies due, being the halfe, ought to bee 22,225<em>l.</em>, besides what is brought from, and sent to, other parts. Comparing that with what hath been received from those ministers, you will see how the Companie hath been abused; which wee hope your care will for the future prevent.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">By the autumn of 1658 the Committees of the Company were beginning to waver in their resolution to rely upon peaceful methods in dealing with the Persians. This was due to the receipt of the letters written by Andrews in September and December, 1657. Referring to them in a postscript to a letter to Surat, dated 13 September, 1658, the Company said:</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><blockquote><p>Wee take notice that, notwithstanding he [Andrews] hath used his uttmost endeavours, yet wee are in hazard to loose our priviledges in Persia and our proportion of customes there. Yet wee are not out of hope that, when that King shall heare of our resetling of our Presidencie in Surratt, and sending a person of worth and quallitie as our Agent into Persia, that he will be perswaded by faire meanes to establish our former priviledges; which if he shall refuse to doe, wee have some thoughts of maintaining our just rights, though with some hazards. And therefore, unlesse you shall find a faire complyance with him, wee desire you to be very cautious that you engage not our estates in sending it up to Spahan, but rather to take the present markett at Gombroone, from whence wee can more easilie withdraw the same, if wee see occasion.</p></blockquote></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This letter was sent in duplicate, overland and by the <em>Samaritan</em>; but when either copy reached Surat is not recorded.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In a subsequent communication (23 March, 1659) to Surat, the Company acknowledged the receipt of a letter (now missing) written by Andrews on 2 July, 1658, in which (they stated) he <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-185" data-page="185" data-folio="173" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n184_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 173">[p. 173]</button> Gives us a relation how the Companies affaires then stood in Persia, and what a meane esteeme they had of our nation, not sufferring our people to sitt in the customehowse<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-18-fn-003" href="#sec-18-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-18-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> but when they were pleased to permitt the same; and that notwithstanding the Kings share of customes for that yeare amounted to about 8,000 tomaunds, besides the Shawbanders fees etc., yet noe more then 500 tomaunds would be afforded for our proportion, and that purchased with great presents and much trouble. Soe that wee are very sencible of those great abuses which wee lye under, though wee hope that, when it shall appeare unto the King that wee are againe conjoyned, and our desires (by our letter sent unto him) shall be made knowne unto him, wee shall find a more faire and better complyance, by his commaund unto his ministers, then formerly wee have done; but if not, wee shall then further consider, and give you order how to proceede in this businesse.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">That Garway and his associates wrote overland to the Company on 10 and 31 December, 1658, appears from the latter’s reply, which will be noticed in due course. The letters themselves are no longer extant. We know also that Andrews returned in the <em>Society</em> to Surat, arriving about the middle of January, 1659. Of his former colleagues, Bell was probably left by the same vessel at Lahrībandar, to take up his post in Sind; while, as the name of Anthony Daniel henceforth disappears from the records, it may be opined that the climate of Gombroon had proved as fatal to him as it had to so many other Europeans.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-18-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-18-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">Garway belonged to a family which had been prominent in the East India Company from its commencement.
