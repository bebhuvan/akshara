<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-423"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="423" data-folio="411" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n422_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 411">[p. 411]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">APPENDIX</span></h2><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">SOME BENGAL FARMĀNS</span>, 1633–60</h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Soon</span> after the appearance of the 1651–4 volume, my attention was drawn by the late Mr. J. S. Cotton to an entry in Dr. Rieu's <em>Catalogue of the Persian MSS. at the British Museum</em> (vol. i. p. 407), from which it appeared that in that collection there was a volume containing copies in Persian of <em>farmāns</em>, &amp;c., relating to English trade in India, ranging in date from <span class="sc">a.d.</span> 1633 to 1712. These copies, written on Indian paper, apparently in the late eighteenth century, came from the collection of Horace Hayman Wilson, who had presumably acquired them during his residence in India (1808–32).</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">An examination of the volume (<em>Addl. MSS.</em>, no. 24039), made with the assistance of Mr. E. Edwards and Mr. A. G. Ellis, showed that it included several documents of considerable importance for the present series. Rotograph copies of these were accordingly prepared and dispatched to Calcutta, where Mr. A. F. Scholfield, the officer in charge of the Imperial Record Office, was good enough to arrange for their translation by Maulavi Muhammad Isrāīl Khān, who is specially skilled in reading the <em>shikasta</em> script in which they are written. From his versions (which have been placed in the India Office Library, together with a rotograph copy of the whole volume) the following abstracts have been made.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 1</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>parwāna</em> under the seal of the Nawāb,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-31-fn-001" href="#sec-31-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-31-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> dated 25 Ardībihist in the sixth year of the reign.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Upon the suit of the English captain, Mr. Cartwright, permission is granted for English vessels to trade in the ports of Orissa, without payment of any dues whatsoever. The English merchants are to be allowed to sell their goods and to carry the remainder wherever they please. If they buy goods in the province, no one is to hinder <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-424" data-page="424" data-folio="412" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n423_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 412">[p. 412]</button> them from exporting the same freely. They are to be permitted to purchase or repair vessels, and for such work payment is to be accepted at the usual rates. A wooden house may be erected at any place which Cartwright may choose, but no compound is to be attached to it. Order is given to all officials to treat the English well and not to molest them for duties. Any dispute arising among them is to be settled by themselves without interference. No obstacle is to be placed in the way of their returning to England when they please. Cartwright is enjoined to bring into the province curiosities and articles of European manufacture from Wazīrābād [<em>not identified</em>] and Masulipatam; and for such services he will be honoured and amply rewarded.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This is evidently the grant obtained by Ralph Cartwright in 1633 (see <em>The English Factories, 1630–3</em>, p. xxxi, and C. R. Wilson's <em>Early Annals of the English in Bengal</em>, vol. i. p. 8). The date agrees closely with that given by William Bruton, who says that the grant was drawn on May 3 and sealed two days later. His account of its contents corresponds in the main with the present document, except that in the latter there is no trace of the 'free leave of coining moneys', which, according to Bruton, was added by the Nawāb at the last moment. Such a concession is in fact improbable, and it can only be concluded that Bruton was mistaken on the point.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">There seems to be some doubt as to which of the two Nawābs granted the <em>parwāna</em>. Bruton does not give any name, and there is no indication in the contemporary English records. However, in a list of <em>farmāns</em>, &amp;c., entered in the Bengal Consultations for 19 February, 1704, Dr. C. R. Wilson found the present document described as having been granted by Āgha Muhammad Zamān, and he therefore concluded that this individual was Nawāb of Orissa from 1631–2 to 1634–5 as well as from 1640–1 to 1645–6 (<em>op. cit.</em>, p. 8 <em>n.</em>). Dr. Wilson admitted that the contemporary Indian historians were against him, but he thought that they were in error. It must, however, be pointed out that the entry in the 1704 Consultations may have been an imperfect one, and that the <em>Pādshāh Nāmah</em>, which gives the list of Nawābs as Bākir Khān (to 1631–2), Mutakid Khān (to 1639–40), Nawāz Khān (to 1640–1), Muhammad Zamān (to 1645–6), Mutakid Khān, may <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-425" data-page="425" data-folio="413" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n424_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 413">[p. 413]</button> be correct after all, particularly as Muhammad Zamān appears to have been serving in Bengal from 1630–1 to 1634–5. The mention of Mutakid Khān’s name first in the <em>parwāna</em> seems to suggest that it was really granted by him<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-31-fn-002" href="#sec-31-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-31-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> and confirmed by Muhammad Zamān at a later date.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio 2</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>farmān</em> from the Emperor Shāh Jahān, dated 24 Jumāda II in the eleventh year, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1047 [i.e. 3 Nov., 1637, O.S.].</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">It has been represented to the Emperor that the English merchants frequenting the ports of Surat and Broach pay there the customary duties, and that they hold a <em>farmān</em> from His Majesty to the effect that no one shall make any other demands in respect of their goods in any place. Now the <em>mutasaddis</em> [accountants] of Mumtāzābād,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-31-fn-003" href="#sec-31-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-31-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup> which is by Akbarābād [i.e. Agra], demand the same payments from them on their goods brought from and taken to Pūrab,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-31-fn-004" href="#sec-31-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-31-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">3</a></sup> &amp;c., as are paid by other merchants. Order is therefore given to those and other officials not to molest the English for duties or other payments on their goods, but to allow them to pass freely from Mumtāzābād Ghāt and other ferries on the Jumna.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Attached is a copy of a memorandum, dated 2 Jumāda I (10 Sept., 1637, O.S.), by the Wazīr Makramat Khān and the news-writer Ināyatullah, recording the Emperor’s orders, together with endorsements by various officials in the course of the preparation of the <em>farmān</em>.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The existence of this grant was known from the list of 19 February, 1704, in the Bengal Consultations already mentioned, but no copy has hitherto been traced. It was procured by Henry Bornford during his residence at court in 1637–9 (see <em>The English Factories</em>, 1637–41, p. xx).</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio 3</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>nishān</em> from Sultān Shuja [Governor of Bengal], dated in the month of Dai in the twenty-second year, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1059. <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-426" data-page="426" data-folio="414" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n425_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 414">[p. 414]</button> Makes known to all officials, and to the guards of the imperial highways between His Highness’s residence and Agra and the port of Surat, that Captain Gabriel Boughton, the English physician, is going to those parts to purchase goods for His Highness. Enjoins them to be very attentive to him, to provide him with escort, and not to delay him or molest him for duties.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 4</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a similar <em>nishān</em>, addressed to the officials on the routes to the ports of Pegu, Tenasserim, Kharapattam, and Negapatam.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In the introduction (p. xxvi) to the 1650–4 volume, reference was made to Boughton’s sojourn at the court of Sultān Shuja, and the favour with which he was regarded by that prince, owing to his success in curing a lady of the <em>haram</em>. The traditional account, written in February, 1685, which is there mentioned, says : ‘This Prince . . . offers Mr. Boughton, if he would trade, he should be free from paying of custom and all other duties, and gave Mr. Boughton two neshauns to that end.’ Evidently we have here the two documents referred to, and we now know for certain that, as surmised in my article in the <em>Indian Antiquary</em> for September, 1911, they were personal to Boughton and not general to the English nation, as has been so often affirmed. It is true that the traditional account goes on to say that in 1650 Brookhaven’s vessel ‘upon the account of Mr. Boughton’s neshauns was free of all duties’; but this, if true, may have been due to the factors making out that their goods belonged to Boughton.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The date given appears to correspond with January, 1649, since <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1059 did not begin till then, while Dai in the twenty-second year cannot be placed later than that month.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 5</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>farmān</em> from the Emperor Shāh Jahān, dated 23 Shabān in the twenty-fourth year, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1060 [i.e. 11 Aug., 1650, O.S.].<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-31-fn-005" href="#sec-31-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-31-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Order is given to the various officials, including those having charge of the roads between Agra and Bengal and between Agra <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-427" data-page="427" data-folio="415" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n426_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 415">[p. 415]</button> and Surat, either by way of Burhānpur or via Ahmadābād, that the English, having paid the usual customs at Surat, Broach, or Lahrī [bandar], are not to be troubled with any further demands. This injunction is to be considered as perpetual. Should any robbery be committed, the <em>jagīrdar</em> of the place is to use his best endeavours to recover the goods, paying every attention to the English.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This is the ‘phirmand prohibiting the takeing of radarres in the provinces and parts of Porab, Brampor, and Ahmada[bad]’ mentioned on p. 320 of the 1646–50 volume as having been obtained by Richard Davidge at Delhi in 1650.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 6</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>nishān</em> from Sultān Shuja, dated 6 Ramazān in the twenty-fifth year, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1061 [i.e. 13 August, 1651, O.S.].</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">It has been represented to His Highness that the goods of the English Company are by an imperial <em>farmān</em> exempt from duties, but that nevertheless the <em>mutasaddis</em> of Balasore and other ports of Orissa molest the merchants on that account, hindering them from buying and selling, and giving them trouble on the roads His Highness now orders that none of the officials shall demand any duties from them, either at the ports or on the roads, nor obstruct them in any way.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The grant of this <em>nishān</em> is recorded in the passage quoted on p. xxvii of the 1650–4 volume. As there stated, no copy has hitherto been known.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 7</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>nishān</em> from Sultān Shuja, dated 21 Jumāda II, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1066 (i.e. 6 April, 1656, O.S.), corresponding with 29 Farwardīn in the twenty-eighth year.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This document is dealt with on p. 111 of the present volume. There has been some controversy over the date (see Wilson’s <em>Early Annals of the English in Bengal</em>, p. 28 <em>n.</em>), owing to the difficulty of reconciling the twenty-eighth regnal year (which began in March, 1654) with the Hijra date. There can be no doubt, however, that the latter is right.</p></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-428"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="428" data-folio="416" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories10fost/page/n427_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 416">[p. 416]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Folio</em> 8</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Copy of a <em>parwāna</em> from Muazzam Khān [i.e. Mīr Jumla], dated 7 Jumāda II in the first year of the reign [of Aurangzeb].</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Jonathan Trevisa, the English Agent, has represented that the goods of the English Company are by imperial <em>farmān</em> free from all duties. It is therefore ordered that, in obedience to that command, all officials in Bengal and Orissa shall refrain from demanding anything from the English on this account.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">On the margin is a note by the Dīwān, Rāy Bhagwati Dās, that the order of the deceased Nawāb must be carried out in the same way as in the past.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">From p. 391 it is evident that the date is 7 Jumāda II, <span class="sc">a.h.</span> 1070, i.e. 9 February, 1660, O.S. Evidently the regnal year is in this case dated from Aurangzeb's formal accession in Ramazān 1069.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The endorsement by the Dīwān appears to have been written after the death of Mīr Jumla in March, 1663.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The remaining grants are of dates considerably later than those described above.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-31-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-31-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">The names of Mutakid Khān and Āgha Muhammad Zamān are written above.</span></li><li id="sec-31-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-31-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">In his <em>Studies in Mughal India</em>, 1919 (p. 199), Professor Jadunath Sarkar states that Mutakid Khān was Governor of Orissa from 1632 to 1641.</span></li><li id="sec-31-fn-003" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-31-fn-003" aria-label="Return to text">2.</a> <span class="fn-body">This name has not been found.
