They total 185, of which 175 belong to the India Office collections, while the Public Record Office contributes three and the British Museum seven. No Indian Record Office possesses any document relating to these years.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In the light of subsequent developments, the most noteworthy event of the period may be said to be the building of a fort at Madraspatam, and the transfer of the head-quarters of the Coast Agency from Masulipatam to the new settlement. To some extent the interest of the present volume in this respect has been anticipated by the publication of my <em>Founding of Fort St. George</em> (1902); but here the picture has at least the advantage of its proper setting by the information given regarding concurrent events at the other stations of the Company's factors. Nor were those events by any means devoid of interest in themselves. A new trade was opened up from Surat by the dispatch of shipping to Basrā, at the head of the Persian Gulf; while at the same time increased attention was paid to the development of commerce with Sind on the one hand and with the Malabar seaboard on the other. Of more general interest are the gradual strengthening of the Anglo-Portuguese <em>entente</em> and the rivalry of Courteen's factors with the servants of the older body.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">As explained on page viii, the frontispiece depicts a group <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-10" data-page="10" data-folio="iv" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories06fost/page/n9_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page iv">[p. iv]</button> of old Dutch and English monuments still to be seen at Agra, and among them the tombstone of John Drake, whose death from a wound received in a skirmish with some villagers is narrated in the present volume.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In the preparation of the footnotes much assistance has been received from Professor Blumhardt, Mr. J. J. Cotton, I.C.S., Mr. Edgar Thurston, C.I.E., and other friends; while Mr. H. G. Bull, of the India Office, has again helped largely in the correction of proofs and the compilation of the index.</p></div>
