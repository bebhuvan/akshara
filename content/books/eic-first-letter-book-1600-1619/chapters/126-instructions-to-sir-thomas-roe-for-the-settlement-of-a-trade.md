<span id="page-547" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>Coppin.</em></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Per lre de priuato Sigillo.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">A declaration of the opinions of vs the Gouern<sup>r</sup> and Company of M<sup>r</sup>ch<sup>te</sup> of Londond trading into the East Indies, what we thinke fitt to set downe as Enstruccõns for the right Hon<sup>ble</sup> S<sup>r</sup> Tho: Roe,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-126-fn-001" href="#sec-126-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-126-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> his Ma<sup>ties</sup> Embassador residing w<sup>th</sup> the great Mogoll. concerning the setling of a Trade in Persia.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">first THAT yo<sup>r</sup> Lo<sup>pp</sup> receiue due informacõn from <em>Armenians Moores</em> and such oth<sup>r</sup> M<sup>r</sup>ch<sup>te</sup>, as trade out of <em>Persia</em> into the <em>Mogores</em> dominions, of the distance of place, wher the Silke is principallye to be had, vnto the Port, that may be most comodious for our Shippes to repaire vnto.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">2. ALSO to take good knowledge of a suffic<sup>t</sup> Port, both for fitnes (in regard of repaire, and entrance therunto) as also for the saufetye of o<sup>r</sup> people and goode.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">3. LIKEWISE of the quantitye of Silke that is yearlye bought by m<sup>r</sup>ch<sup>te</sup> and exported out of <em>Persia</em>. As also of the currant price, that it beareth in those parte ordinarily, And what the charge therof may be, by cariage from that place vnto the Port wher wee ar to lade it To the end that the principall cost and charge, may plainlye appeare vnto vs.</p></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-548"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="548" data-folio="456" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n547_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 456">[p. 456]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">4. WHICH pointe being dulye examined and informa~on taken accordinglye then to consid<sup>r</sup> of some fitting p̄son or persons (two or three at the most) whome yo<sup>u</sup> may send to treate w<sup>th</sup> the <em>King of Persia</em>, on our Kingē behalf, for the establishment of such a Trade w<sup>th</sup> vs as will aunswer w<sup>th</sup> o<sup>r</sup> meanes and vente of our Com̄odityes . well enstructed by yo<sup>r</sup> Lo<sup>pp</sup> in the Articles following, and such oth<sup>r</sup> thinge as to yo<sup>u</sup> shall seme most convenient, And therin to lymit the charge, according to yo<sup>r</sup> Lo<sup>ps</sup> discre~on, for that wee haue fownd the expence of Richard Steele,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-126-fn-002" href="#sec-126-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-126-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> to be verie extraordinarye and much distastfull to the Company</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">5. AND the better to enhable them to treate w<sup>th</sup> the <em>Kinge of Persia</em> or his officers, wee haue sent yo<sup>u</sup> a Coppie of the Capitula~ons, agreed vppon betwixt the <em>Kings Ma<sup>tie</sup></em> and the <em>Grand Sig<sup>r</sup></em>. for our free Trade into <em>Turkey</em>, wherby (w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>r</sup> Lo<sup>pps</sup> good helpe) such Articles may be framed ther to be sent w<sup>th</sup> them, as may be fit to be pn̄ted to the <em>King of Persia</em> for the establishm<sup>t</sup> of what wee desire: least being referred to their setting downe somwhat may be mistaken to o<sup>r</sup> disadvantage And for the better enhabling of yo<sup>r</sup> Lo: therin, wee haue set downe such other notes, as wee thinke fit to be incerted.</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">To say.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1. THAT there may be a certaine rate agreed vppon for Customes or Tolles (yf any be ther payable) and that w<sup>th</sup> as much fauo<sup>r</sup> as may be.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">2. THAT ther may be assigned vs a saufe and secure Port, wher o<sup>r</sup> shippes may ride, w<sup>th</sup>out pill of the Seas, and daunger of the Enemyes, And wher our Mart for the selling of ours, and buying of their com̄odityes may be setled, w<sup>th</sup>out ou<sup>r</sup> farre cariage into the Countrye.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">3 THAT wee may haue some good assurance, That for their Silke they will accept at the least th’one half of <em>English</em> com̄odityes at reasonable rates, especiallye Cloath at 20<sup>li</sup> str p̄ Cloath or 80 Ryalls of 8.
