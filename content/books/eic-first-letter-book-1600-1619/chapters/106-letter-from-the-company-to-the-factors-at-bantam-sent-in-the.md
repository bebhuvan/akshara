19<sup>th</sup> of March. 1609.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><div class="sidenote-stack"><aside class="shoulder-sidenote">Iohn Hearne Robert Browne Henry Sydall Bantam</aside></div><p class="prose-p">BY the Dragon w<sup>ch</sup> God be praysed arryved at Plymouth the middle of Septembƀ last we receiued lres twoe from yo<sup>u</sup> Iohn Hearne one from Roƀt Browne one from Iohn Sarys &amp; one from Henry Sydall: By Iohn Hearnes łre the third of Septembƀ we haue aduise what pepp, Alloes, white Benyamin and silke was sent for o<sup>r</sup> accompt all w<sup>ch</sup> we haue receiued by Godę favo<sup>r</sup> althoughe the Ship was very leakye and came weaklie home, in soe yll case as that we durst scarce adventure her (after she was lightened aboue 100 Tonns []) to come for London notw<sup>th</sup>standinge we finde avery greate want of the pepp yo<sup>u</sup> write was put abourd, to the full want as yt appeth to vs to be, Aboue twentie Tuns each Tun cont 2000 waight :<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-106-fn-001" href="#sec-106-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-106-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> w<sup>ch</sup> seemeth the more strange vnto vs in that the pepp yo<sup>u</sup> laded at Bantam had lyen soe longe and dryed soe much, that of 7489 sackę, there was lost 238 there, all w<sup>ch</sup> Considered together maketh vs thinke that o<sup>r</sup> busines was not well husbanded neither w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>u</sup> nor in the Shipp comeinge home: Therefore we pray yo<sup>u</sup> that hereafter nothinge be bought nor sould privatelie but that yo<sup>r</sup> Iournall or booke Couchant may contynually lay open for eu<sup>r</sup>y one that is o<sup>r</sup> ffacto<sup>r</sup> to looke vpon, for wee hould yt noe way fitt, that he that is o<sup>r</sup> cheefe ffacto<sup>r</sup>, should carrie all the accomptę close to him selfe in such sorte that those whoe are w<sup>th</sup> him can giue noe reason howe o<sup>r</sup> Comodities are lessened, guiftę given, nor howe the wantę doe happen as some of yo<sup>r</sup> selues haue written vnto vs in theis yo<sup>r</sup> łres, that yo<sup>u</sup> can yeald vs noe reason w<sup>ch</sup> way the foresaid 238 baggę of pepp should be lost :</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">We are sorry that there hath not beene a Contynuall booke kept there that we might thereby haue discerned in good sorte howe o<sup>r</sup> busines hath beene carried. ffor we are giuen to vnderstand that m<sup>r</sup> Scott brought away his owne accomptę as alsoe m<sup>r</sup> Towerson did the lyke and lefte noe Coppies of them w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>u</sup>. It had beene fittinge for such as are the Companies servantę to haue kept a booke for the Company, there to haue remaned, and haue brought home a Coppy thereof for their owne vse, or at least so much thereof as might concerne them. ffor an amendm<sup>t</sup> <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-405" data-page="405" data-folio="313" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n404_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 313">[p. 313]</button> whereof we haue sent yo<sup>u</sup> a lardge booke to be a Contynuall Couchant soe longe as yt shall last, willinge yo<sup>u</sup> the principall ffactor<sup>r</sup> and those that shall succeede yo<sup>u</sup>, that from hencforth yo<sup>u</sup> suffer those that are factors<sup>rs</sup> w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>u</sup> to be priuie to all thinge bought and sould or otherwyse to be disposed of As alsoe that yo<sup>r</sup> booke and accompte may be publique and comon to all the rest of o<sup>r</sup> ffactors<sup>rs</sup>, w<sup>ch</sup> shall be good for yo<sup>r</sup> selfe to avoyed scandall or suspi<span class="sc">c</span>õn : And will be alsoe agood informa<span class="sc">c</span>õn vnto the rest of the ffactors<sup>rs</sup> vnder yo<sup>u</sup>, and bringe contentm<sup>t</sup> vnto vs, to free vs from suspi<span class="sc">c</span>õn of any practizes when yo<sup>u</sup> shall carry yo<sup>r</sup> busines publiquelie.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Wee write vnto yo<sup>u</sup> twoe yeares past by the <em>Assencõn</em> and <em>Vnion</em> as alsoe by the <em>Expedicõn</em>, Captaine, David Middleton, the Coppies of w<sup>ch</sup> łres we doe herew<sup>th</sup>all send yo<sup>u</sup>.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-106-fn-002" href="#sec-106-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-106-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Albeyt we haue heretofore suffered yo<sup>u</sup> to haue private trade, yett the Company hath now vpon speciall considera<span class="sc">c</span>õn resolued, Not to pmitt yt any longer, for that we finde that those whoe haue had the manadgeinge of o<sup>r</sup> busines, haue had more care to inrich them selues, then to seeke o<sup>r</sup> proffitt, for yf they had not soe done they might haue ymployed o<sup>r</sup> stocke (w<sup>ch</sup> they haue had in their hande since S<sup>r</sup> Iames Lancaster came from thence) soe as the benefitt thereof might haue borne the chardges of o<sup>r</sup> howse at Bantam at the least.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">We wrote vuto yo<sup>u</sup> by Captaine David Middleton that yo<sup>u</sup> should noe more pay yo<sup>r</sup> selues yo<sup>r</sup> wages by Royalls of 8 at iiij<sup>s</sup> the peece but att such a moderate rate, as might be indifferent:<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-106-fn-003" href="#sec-106-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-106-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup> yett now vpon better considera<span class="sc">c</span>õn the Company haue concluded, and giue yo<sup>u</sup> direct order to pay noe more wages at all there, then may be needfull for the comelie apprellinge of any of o<sup>r</sup> ffactors<sup>rs</sup> w<sup>ch</sup> we deeme to be x<sup>li</sup> or 20 m<sup>r</sup>ke at the most p̄ Annū and the rest to be paid here in England, either to be adventured in the generall stocke or to such of yo<sup>r</sup> freinde, as yo<sup>u</sup> shall assigne the Company to pay yt vnto. And w<sup>th</sup>all such wages as yo<sup>u</sup> shall pay to lett the Ryall of 8 for fyve shillinge.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Alsoe we signifie vnto yo<sup>u</sup> all, that althoughe any of yo<sup>u</sup> shall <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-406" data-page="406" data-folio="314" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n405_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 314">[p. 314]</button> succeede such as were before yo<sup>u</sup> in place yett there shall be noe succession of increase of wages allowed vnto yo<sup>u</sup> except yt be by spetiall order &amp; direc̃cõn from the Governo<sup>r</sup> and Company. Wee haue appointed S<sup>r</sup> Henry Middleton to be o<sup>r</sup> Generall, vnto whome we wish yo<sup>u</sup> to shewe yo<sup>r</sup> selues obedient, and to doe as he shall ymploy yo<sup>u</sup> whether yt be by tarieinge there, or goeinge to any other place whether he shall see occasion to carry yo<sup>u</sup>.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Althoughe we had giuen order heretofore that noe man should bringe home any goode in o<sup>r</sup> Shipps of the nature and kinde that we o<sup>r</sup> selues doe lade, yett the greatest nomber in the Dragon brought home store of pepp, w<sup>ch</sup> causeth vs to thinke that the same was stolne from vs and occasioned the want we found of o<sup>r</sup> pepp in the said Shipp. ffor prevencõn whereof hereafter we haue reiterated the same Comandement, and wish eu<sup>r</sup>y one of yo<sup>u</sup> that shall either be at the cleiringe of any of o<sup>r</sup> Shipps away from thence, or come home in any of them, that yo<sup>u</sup> see and make diligent inquirie amongst the Company of the said Shipp publiquelie, whether any of them haue any goode of such kinde yea or noe. yf they haue and can make due proofe that they bought yt there, then yo<sup>u</sup> are to pay them for yt &amp; shoote yt into the hould: yf they haue none signifie soe much vnto vs, that they denied to haue any there.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">We haue alsoe taken order, that whosoeu<sup>r</sup> shall lade any goode in o<sup>r</sup> Shipps and shall not register the same w<sup>th</sup> the Pursar shall haue yt taken from him. And we requier eu<sup>r</sup>y one of yo<sup>u</sup> to doe yo<sup>r</sup> best that the same may be dulie pformed.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">We will doe o<sup>r</sup> best endevo<sup>rs</sup> to prevent that none such as S<sup>r</sup> Edward Michelborne was, should disturbe yo<sup>r</sup> quiett whoe dyed a yeare since hopinge that tyme will weare out the remembrance of his deede.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">fforasmuch as we haue found greate inconvenyence by lettinge of yo<sup>r</sup> money there at 2 or 3 for one to be paid heare, whereby diu<sup>r</sup>s of the Mariners and their wyues and Children after their deathes haue beene ympou<sup>r</sup>ished we doe therefore thinke yt fitt, that noe such Exchandge be suffered or pmitted for the avoidinge of scandall both from yo<sup>u</sup> and vs.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">It appeth by the Accompte sent home that yo<sup>u</sup> haue drawne yo<sup>r</sup> wages after iiij<sup>s</sup> the Ryall of 8 from the first of October 1605 <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-407" data-page="407" data-folio="315" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n406_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 315">[p. 315]</button> to the 31<sup>th</sup> of October 1608, w<sup>ch</sup> being accompted after iiij<sup>s</sup> vj<sup>d</sup> the Ryall as yt costeth vs beside the adventure we finde you haue allowed yo<sup>r</sup> selues 470 Ryalls of 8 more then is yo<sup>r</sup> due w<sup>ch</sup> we expect to haue restored by yo<sup>u</sup> that haue receiued the same, as alsoe that from the 31<sup>th</sup> of Octoƀ 1608 yo<sup>u</sup> receiue the Ryall of 8 at 5<sup>s</sup> and not otherwyse.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Thomas Harwood the Gunner of the Dragon giueth vs in accompt 20 barrells of powlder lefte w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>u</sup> Iohn Hearne, whereof as of all thinge ells lefte vnder yo<sup>r</sup> chardge we expect a good accompt.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">There is one W<sup>m</sup>: Lanwell lefte at Bantam whoe standeth indebted in the Pursars booke of the Dragon iij<sup>li</sup>—iiij<sup>s</sup>—x<sup>d</sup> w<sup>ch</sup> yo<sup>u</sup> are to defaulke out of his wages there
