And whereas the said m<sup>r</sup>chaunte haue chosen you the said Willm Kealinge to be principall Gou<sup>r</sup>nor or <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-204" data-page="204" data-folio="112" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n203_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 112">[p. 112]</button> Generall of all the m<sup>r</sup>chaunte Marriner<em>s</em> and other o<sup>r</sup> subiecte w<sup>ch</sup> are or shall be shipped in any of the said shipps. And you the said Willm Hawkins to be leiuetenante generall of all the m<sup>r</sup>chaunte Marriner<em>s</em> &amp; other o<sup>r</sup> subiecte, w<sup>ch</sup> are or shall be shipped in any of the said shipps. We graciouslie favoringe their intended voyadge &amp; approueing &amp; alloweing of their choyse of you to the same Gou<sup>r</sup>nm<sup>te</sup>, being desirous to furnish you w<sup>th</sup> all fitt &amp; Convenient power and aucthoritie to rule &amp; gou<sup>r</sup>ne all &amp; eu<sup>r</sup>y o<sup>r</sup> subiecte ymployed in this voyadge, by a due obedience to be by them yealded vnto you in the obserueing and executeing of all such good orders &amp; Constituçõns as you shall thinke Conuenient, to ordaine &amp; appointe for the furtheraunce of the said voyadge to the Hono<sup>r</sup> of vs &amp; o<sup>r</sup> kingdomes &amp; for the advauncem<sup>t</sup> of the said trade doe hereby straightlie chardge &amp; Comaund all &amp; eu<sup>r</sup>y pson &amp; psons ymployed vsed or shipped in this voyadge in any of the said shipps to giue all due obedience &amp; respect vnto you duringe the said voyadge, &amp; to beare them selues one towarde an other, in all good order &amp; quietnes for avoydinge any occasion that might breed Muteny quarrells or discension amongst them to the hindrance of the good successe, w<sup>ch</sup> is to be hoped for by gode puidence of the said intended voyadge, and in default of such dutie &amp; obedience to be pformed towarde you, &amp; for the Correcçõn &amp; quenchinge of all such Muteny quarrells or dissention that shall or may be moued by the disorder euill dispotiçõn, or pversnes of any of the said psons. Wee doe hereby authorize you the said Willm Keelinge during the said voyadge, or during soe longe tyme as you shall lyue in the said voyadge to Chastice correct &amp; ponnish all offenders &amp; transgresso<sup>rs</sup> in that behalfe according to the qualitie of their Offences w<sup>th</sup> such punishm<sup>te</sup> as are comonlie vsed in all Armyes by Sea, when they are not capitall. And for Capitall Offences as for wilfull murther w<sup>ch</sup> is hatefull in the sight of God, or mutenie w<sup>ch</sup> is one offence that may tend to the ou<sup>r</sup>throw of the said voyadge the same being trulie and iustlie proued agaynst any of the pson or psons aforesaid. We doe hereby giue vnto you the said Willm Kealeing during all the tyme of the said voyadg or during soe longe tyme as you shall liue in the same, full power &amp; aucthoritie to vse &amp; putt <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-205" data-page="205" data-folio="113" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n204_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 113">[p. 113]</button> in Execution o<sup>r</sup> lawe called Marshall lawe in that behalfe. And in case of yo<sup>r</sup> decease (w<sup>ch</sup> god forbidd) we doe then likewyse hereby aucthorize you the said Will̃m Hawkins to execute the power &amp; aucthoritie by this o<sup>r</sup> Comission graunted to the said Will̃m Keeleing in as ample manner as is herein before expressed, &amp; in such sorte as the said Will̃m Kealeing did or might doe by vertue hereof. And theis o<sup>r</sup> l̃res shall be vnto you, and either of you sufficient warrant &amp; discharge, for the doing &amp; executeing of all and singuler the p<sup>r</sup>misses. And for as much as att this present tyme we are in ametie w<sup>th</sup> all Christian Princes, &amp; are vnwilling that any our subiectę should giue occasion of breach or hindraunce of that league or ametie w<sup>ch</sup> we hold w<sup>th</sup> any o<sup>r</sup> Confederatę ffreindę or Allyes. And because we are not ignorante of the emulac̃on &amp; envie that doth accompany the discou<sup>r</sup>y of Countries &amp; trade &amp; of the quarrells &amp; Contenc̃ons w<sup>ch</sup> doe many tymes fall out betweene the subiectę of diuers Princes that meete the one w<sup>th</sup> the other in forreine &amp; farr remote Countries, in the Course &amp; p̃secuteing of their discou<sup>r</sup>ies, and being desirous that o<sup>r</sup> subiectę should forbeare to moue or beginn any quarrell or contenc̃on vpon the subiectę of o<sup>r</sup> Confederatę ffreindę or Alleys</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">either in their p̃ceeding, or retourne vpon or from any of their voyagę. Wee therefore doe hereby straightly chardge &amp; Comaund you the said Will̃m Keeling &amp; you the said Will̃m Hawkins &amp; all other vnder yo<sup>r</sup> Gou<sup>r</sup>nm<sup>te</sup>, that neither in yo<sup>r</sup> voyadge outward or homeward or in any Countrie, Iland, Porte or place where you shall abide, or come duringe the tyme of yo<sup>r</sup> being abroade out of o<sup>r</sup> kingdomes or dominions where you meete w<sup>th</sup> any the Subiectę of the kinge of Spaine, or of any other o<sup>r</sup> Confederatę ffreindę or Alleys, or any other Nac̃on or people, their shipps, vessells goodę or m<sup>r</sup>chandize, you doe not attempt or goe aboute to sett vpon, take or surprise their p̃sons shipps vessells goodę or m<sup>r</sup>chandizes, or offer any iniurie, or discourtesie vnto them vnlesse you shall first by them, therevnto iustlie be puoked, or driuen in the iust defence of yo<sup>r</sup> owne p̃sons, yo<sup>r</sup> shipps, vessells, goodę or m<sup>r</sup>chandizes, or for recompence or recou<sup>r</sup>y of any the p̃sons goodę or m<sup>r</sup>chandizes of any o<sup>r</sup> subiectę that are alredie in or neere the East Indies as you will <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-206" data-page="206" data-folio="114" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n205_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 114">[p. 114]</button> aunsweare the Contrarie att yo<sup>r</sup> vttermost prills. State Papers</em>, 1513-1616, p. 150.)</span></li><li id="sec-41-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-41-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">2.</a> <span class="fn-body">William Hawkins, the lieutenant-general of this voyage, on reaching Surat proceeded as directed (<em>see</em> p. 124) to the court of the Great Mogul, where he arrived in April, 1609. He was received with great favour, and succeeded, in spite of Portuguese intrigues, in obtaining a license for the establishment of an English factory at Surat. The emperor gave him a handsome allowance, and married him to the daughter of an Armenian Christian. Later, however, his favour declined, and Hawkins applied for permission to depart. He reached Surat in Feb., 1612, and there found Sir Henry Middleton with the ships of the <em>Sixth Voyage</em>. He accompanied Sir Henry to the Red Sea, and afterwards to Bantam, whence he took ship for England in the <em>Thomas</em>, but died on the passage home (1613). His widow afterwards married Gabriel Towerson (<em>see</em> p. 33). An attempt has been made to connect this William Hawkins with the nephew of Sir John Hawkins who was lieutenant-general of Fenton's abortive voyage in 1582-3, but it is extremely doubtful whether the identity can be established.</span></li><li id="sec-41-fn-003" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-41-fn-003" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">1607.</span></li></ol></section>
