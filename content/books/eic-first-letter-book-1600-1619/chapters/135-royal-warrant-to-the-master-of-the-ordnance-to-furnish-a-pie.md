<span id="page-591" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><h4 class="sec-h3">Iames Rex<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-135-fn-001" href="#sec-135-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-135-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup></h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Right</span> trustie, and right welbeloued Counsaylor wee greet yow well; These are to will and require yow forthwith to deliuer to our trustie, and welbeeloued S<sup>r</sup>. Thomas Smith Knight Gouernor of the East Indya Companye of Merchante, or to any whome hee shall apoynt, one Brass Demye Culueringe of ffiue and twentie hundred weight or thereaboute; to be presented in our name to the Kinge of Macassar in the East Indyes, from whome wee haue receyued manye presente of farr greater value heretofore. And for your soe doeinge, this shall bee your warrant. Geuen vnder our Signet at Whitehall this 13<sup>th</sup>. of Ianuary 1616.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">To our right trustie and right welbeeloued Counsayler the Lo: Carew<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-135-fn-002" href="#sec-135-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-135-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup> M<sup>r</sup> of our Ordinance</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-135-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-135-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">This letter explains itself. There is no reference to the matter in the Court Minutes.</span></li><li id="sec-135-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-135-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">2.</a> <span class="fn-body">George Carew, Baron Carew of Clopton, and afterwards Earl of Totnes (<em>b.</em> 1555, <em>d.</em> 1629), was a fine example of the many sidedness which characterized the men of his day. He had made a voyage with Sir Humphrey Gilbert, and had held a captaincy in the royal navy; as Lieutenant-General of the Ordnance, he had taken part in Essex's expeditions to Cadiz and to the Azores; he had been for a short period ambassador in France; he had rendered most important services in Ireland, both in a military capacity and in administrative posts; as a courtier, he had the good fortune to enjoy the favour of three successive occupants of the throne; and he is still remembered with gratitude by students for his extensive collection of documents bearing on Irish history, a subject in which he took the liveliest interest. He was appointed Master of the Ordnance on the 26th June, 1608, and held the post until the 5th May, 1617 (<em>see Dict. of National Biography</em>, s. n.).</span></li></ol></section>
