<span id="page-544" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">JAMES<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-125-fn-001" href="#sec-125-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-125-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> by the grace of God king of England, Scotland, ffrance and Ireland defend<sup>r</sup> of the faith &amp;c. TO our trustye and welbeloued Subiect Cap<sup>ne</sup> Dauid Middleton. WHERAS dyuers of o<sup>r</sup> louing Subiecte, by the name of the Gouern<sup>r</sup> and Companye of M<sup>r</sup>chante of Londō, trading into the East Indyes at ther owne aduentures coste and charges for the hono<sup>r</sup> of this o<sup>r</sup> Realme of England and for the encrease and aduauncem<sup>t</sup> of trade and merchandize w<sup>th</sup>in the same, haue set forth sundrye voiage to the said East Indyes, and haue alreadye discouered and begunne to settle a trade into those p̃te, And intending the contynuinge therof, aswell by frequenting those place alreade setled, as by furth<sup>r</sup> discoueryes of oth<sup>r</sup> p̃te w<sup>ch</sup> may be fit for interchanging of the Comōdityes of o<sup>r</sup> kingdomes w<sup>th</sup> the Comōdityes of those place, such as may be of speciall vse and benefitt to vs and o<sup>r</sup> subiecte. Hauing to this purpose now p<sup>r</sup>pared and set forth thre good Shippes, called the Samaritane, the Thomas and the Tomazin, to the said East Indyes, and the place adioyning therunto, And wheras o<sup>r</sup> said subiecte the Aduenturer<sup>s</sup> of those voiage, haue chosen y<sup>u</sup> the said Cap<sup>ne</sup> Dauid Middletō, to be the principall Com̃aund<sup>r</sup> or Generall of all the M<sup>r</sup>ch<sup>te</sup> Maryners and other o<sup>r</sup> Subiecte w<sup>ch</sup> are or shalbe shipped in the said thre shippes, Wee graciouslye fauouring their intended voiage, and approuing and allowing of their choise of y<sup>u</sup> to the same place of com̃aund, <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-545" data-page="545" data-folio="453" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n544_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 453">[p. 453]</button> being wylling to furnishe y<sup>u</sup> w<sup>th</sup> all fitt and conuenyent power, and aucthoritye, to rule &amp; gouerne all and eu<sup>r</sup>ye o<sup>r</sup> Subiecte employed in this voiage, by a due obedyence to be by them yeilded vnto y<sup>u</sup> in the obseruing and executing of all such good orders and constitucõns as y<sup>u</sup> shall thinke conuenient to ordeyne and make, for the furtherance of the said voiage according to such pticłer direccõns and enstruccõns as y<sup>u</sup> shall receiue from the said Gouern<sup>r</sup> and Companie of M<sup>r</sup>chante the Aduenturers in this voiage. W<sup>ch</sup> direccõns and enstruccõns our intentiō is, And so wee wyll and require y<sup>u</sup> that y<sup>u</sup> punctuallye obserue in all thinge. WEE do therfore straictlye charge and com̄aund all and euerye pson or psons employed in this voiage, and shipped in those said three shippes, to giue all due obedyence and respect vnto y<sup>u</sup>, during the said voiage, And to beare themselues one towarde anoth<sup>r</sup> in good ord<sup>r</sup> and quietnes, for the auoyding any occasiō, that might breede mutinye quarrells, or dissentions amongst thē, to the hinderance of the good successe w<sup>ch</sup> is to be hoped for by Gode prouidence of the said voiage. And in default of such duitye and obedience to be pfourmed towarde y<sup>u</sup> for the correcting &amp; quenching of all such mutinyes quarrells or dissencõns that may arise by the euill and disorderlye disposicõns of any psons whatsoeu<sup>r</sup>, WEE do hereby aucthorize y<sup>u</sup> the said Cap<sup>ne</sup> Dauid Middletō, during the said voiage, or during so long time as y<sup>u</sup> shall liue in the said voiage, To chastice, correct and punishe all offend<sup>rs</sup> and transgresso<sup>rs</sup> in that behalf, according to the qualitye of their offence, w<sup>th</sup> such punishm<sup>te</sup> as are com̄onlye vsed in all Armyes at Sea, when they are not capitall, And for capitall offence, as wilfull murthe<sup>r</sup>, w<sup>ch</sup> is hatefull in the sight of God, or Mutinye w<sup>ch</sup> is an offence that may tend to the ou<sup>r</sup>throwe of the said voiage, the same being dulye and iustlye proued against any of the pson or psonnes aforesaid. WEE do hereby giue vnto y<sup>u</sup> the said Cap<sup>ne</sup> Dauid Middletō during all the tyme of the said voiage, or during so long as y<sup>u</sup> shall liue in the said voiage, full power and aucthoritye, to vse and put in execucõn our lawe called Martiall lawe in that behalf: But that therin y<sup>u</sup> do exp<sup>r</sup>slye vse the aduise and counsell of the two principall Maisters, and of the two principall m<sup>r</sup>chante appointed for the time being, or of any two of them. <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-546" data-page="546" data-folio="454" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n545_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 454">[p. 454]</button> And theis our lres shalbe vnto y<sup>u</sup> sufficient warrant and discharge, for the doing and executing of all and singler the p<sup>r</sup>misses. AND forasmuch as at this p<sup>r</sup>sent tyme, wee are in amitye w<sup>th</sup> all Christian Prince, And are vnwylling that any o<sup>r</sup> Subiecte should giue occasiō of breach or hinderance therof towarde any o<sup>r</sup> Confederates  friende or Allyes; And because wee are not ignorant of the emulatiō and enuye, w<sup>ch</sup> doth accompanie the discouerye of Countryes and Trades, and of the quarrells and contenc̃ōns, w<sup>ch</sup> do many times fall out, betwene the Subiecte of dyu<sup>r</sup>s Prince, when they meete the one w<sup>th</sup> the oth<sup>r</sup> in forreyne and farre remote Countryes, in prosecuting the course of their discou<sup>r</sup>yes; And being desirous that our Subiecte should forbeare to moue or beginne any quarrell or contenc̃ōn vppō the subiecte of our Confederates, friende or Allyes, eith<sup>r</sup> in their going forth, or retourning frō any of their voiag[e] WEE therfore do herby straictlye charge and comaund y<sup>u</sup> the said Cap<sup>ne</sup> Dauid Middletō, and all ot[hers] vnd<sup>r</sup> yo<sup>r</sup> gouernm<sup>t</sup>, that neith<sup>r</sup> in yo<sup>r</sup> voiage outwarde or homewarde, or in any Countrye, Island Port or place, wher y<sup>u</sup> shall abide or come, during the time of yo<sup>r</sup> being abroad out of o<sup>r</sup> kingdomes or dominions, wher y<sup>u</sup> meete w<sup>th</sup> any the Subiecte of the king of <em>Spaine</em>, or of any oth<sup>r</sup> our Confederates friende or Allyes, or of any oth<sup>r</sup> Natiō or people, ther shippes vessells goode, or m<sup>r</sup>chandizes, y<sup>u</sup> do not attempt, or go about to set vppō take or surprize, their psons, shippes, vessells, goode, or M<sup>r</sup>chandizes, Or offer any iniurye or discourtesie vnto thē, (except y<sup>u</sup> shalbe by thē first therunto iustlye prouoked or driuē, eith<sup>r</sup> in the iust defence of yo<sup>r</sup> owne persons, Shippes, vessells good or M<sup>r</sup>chandizes by any their disturbance or hinderaunce whatsoeu<sup>r</sup>, in yo<sup>r</sup> quiet course of Trade, Or for recompence and recou<sup>r</sup>ye of the persons, Shippes goode or m<sup>r</sup>chandizes of any our Subiecte, that are alreadie in or neare the <em>East Indies</em>, or for any oth<sup>r</sup> iust cause of yo<sup>r</sup> defence[])], In w<sup>ch</sup> cases so excepted, if y<sup>u</sup> attempt surprize and take the psons Shippes and goode of any Princes or State, by whose Subiecte y<sup>u</sup> shall sustayne any wrong or losse in mann<sup>r</sup> as aforesaid, y<sup>u</sup> shall not for any such act or acte grounded vppon the occasions aboue menc̃ōned, be in daung<sup>r</sup> or subiect to the pill and penaltyes of our lawes. Alwayes p<sup>r</sup>supposing, and so requiring <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-547" data-page="547" data-folio="455" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n546_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 455">[p. 455]</button> y<sup>u</sup> to vse all possible endeuo<sup>rs</sup>, by all meanes w<sup>t</sup>soeu<sup>r</sup>, to suffer no spoile to be made of any goode or M<sup>r</sup>chandizes, Nor imbezilling of any thinge whatsoeu<sup>r</sup>, that shalbe recou<sup>r</sup>ed by y<sup>u</sup>, But to see them sauflye brought home w<sup>th</sup> their Billes of lading &amp; Chart<sup>r</sup> pties; to the intent that ther may be restitucõn made by vs, so soone as y<sup>u</sup> shall receiue satisfaccõn from that State or people by whome y<sup>u</sup> are dampnifyed of what Nacion soeu<sup>r</sup>. In wytnes wherof wee haue caused theis o<sup>r</sup> lres to be made Pattente. Witnes o<sup>r</sup> self at Westm<sup>r</sup> the one and twentith day of Aprill, in the Twelfth yeare of o<sup>r</sup> raigne of <em>England France and Ireland</em>, and of <em>Scotland</em> the seuen and fortith.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-125-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-125-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">A commission to Captain David Middleton (see note, page 115) for his voyage in 1614.</span></li></ol></section>
