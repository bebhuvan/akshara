The <em>Hector</em>, a "ship of London, trading to Surat, where she arrived in August last, intercepted by "four Portugal frigates 3rd October following and taken prize, and the men prisoners "for using commerce within their conquest without the King of Spain's licence; "value of the goods, 2,000<em>l</em>; 19 prisoners carried to Goa, three brought to Lisbon, "the rest at Goa. Fras. Bucke and Anthony Marlowe, [see note 3, page 120] Walter "Romney died on the ship's arrival, prisoners in the Trunk in Lisbon, in great "necessity, without apparel, money or other means." <em>Cal. S. [Lee was mistaken in saying that the <em>Hector</em> itself was taken; he should have said two English boats engaged in carrying goods from Surat down the river to the <em>Hector</em> at Suhali.] "Was alsoe red twoe lres the one from Anthonie Marlowe thother from ffrancis "Bucke the Companies servauntes dated at Lisborne the 15<sup>th</sup> of Iulie last, Alsoe w<sup>th</sup> "a discourse of their goinge to Surate &amp; of their takeinge &amp; mishapps therein: &amp; of "their ymprisonment at Lisborne &amp;c: And their request to the Companie for their "inlardgm<sup>t</sup> ........ Upon Consideracon whereof ...... it was thought fitt That "w<sup>th</sup> all expedicon the Lord Threr be made acquainted w<sup>th</sup> theis their lres, w<sup>th</sup> "request of his honorable favo<sup>r</sup> to his ma<sup>tie</sup>: for releefe theirin, for diuers respectes "mentioned, <em>As by the peticon in the Register at lardge appereth</em>. And for the effectinge "of this busines m<sup>r</sup> Gou<sup>r</sup>no<sup>r</sup>, m<sup>r</sup> Deputie, m<sup>r</sup> Harrison, m<sup>r</sup> Iohnson, &amp; m<sup>r</sup> Hamersley "were intreated w<sup>th</sup> all Convenient speede to repaier vnto the Lord Threr at "Salisburie, or where his Lo<sup>p</sup>: may be found, to acquainte his Lo<sup>p</sup>: therew<sup>th</sup>, &amp; w<sup>th</sup> "what ells they shall thinke fitt for the good &amp; speedie dispatch hereof." (<em>Court Minutes</em>, 8th Aug., 1609.) "Att this Courte m<sup>r</sup> Gou<sup>r</sup>no<sup>r</sup> declared how kindlie he &amp; those appointed to goe "w<sup>th</sup> him to the Courte were intreated by the Lord Threr whoe promyseth to haue "a spetiall care of their sute vnto his Ma<sup>tie</sup>: &amp; at his Lo<sup>ps</sup>: retourne to London w<sup>ch</sup> "would be w<sup>th</sup>in Tenn daies, he would giue them further satisfaccon touchinge their "sute." (<em>Ibid.</em>, 14th Aug., 1609.) A further letter on this subject will be found on page 306. It would appear that the prisoners were set at liberty at the end of 1610 or the beginning of 1611, through the good offices of the English ambassador in Spain.</span></li></ol></section>
