<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-526"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="526" data-folio="434" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n525_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 434">[p. 434]</button></div><h4 class="sec-h3"><em>Laus deo.</em> 20 <em>Decēb</em>: 1611. <em>In Amsterdam.</em></h4><div class="prose-unit"><div class="sidenote-stack"><aside class="shoulder-sidenote">M<sup>r</sup> Pasfyld to M<sup>r</sup> Ro Middleton about the same buysines.</aside></div><p class="prose-p">LOUING friend M<sup>r</sup> Middletō &amp;c.  About 2 daies since, I had some speech w<sup>th</sup> Burgom<sup>r</sup> Pavij about the <em>E. India</em> matt<sup>rs</sup>; and he wylled me to certifye yo<sup>u</sup>, that they were not vnmyndfull of that matt<sup>r</sup>, y<sup>u</sup> wrote to thē about, though as yet by reason of oth<sup>r</sup> buysines, nothing effected. Yet ther were certaine appointed, (wherof he was one) to moue the States about it, because they could do nothing therin w<sup>th</sup>out ther aduise and consent: and said moreou<sup>r</sup> that they were to go w<sup>th</sup>in 8 or 10 daies to the <em>Hagh</em> about it: and that they had giuen exp<sup>r</sup>sse charge to the shippes, y<sup>t</sup> are gone out, y<sup>t</sup> they should carye themselues, in all friendlye manner y<sup>t</sup> was possible, towarde the <em>English</em> in the <em>Indies</em>, w<sup>th</sup>out offring them the least iniurye that might be;  And y<sup>t</sup> he doubted not, but ere long, ther should so good order be taken, that both Nations should contynue good friende.  Thus much he wylled me to certifye y<sup>u</sup>.  And so not hauing else at instant to enlardge &amp;c.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Yo<sup>r</sup> louing friend <em>Samuell Pasfyld.</em><sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-001" href="#sec-119-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup></p></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="sidenote-stack"><aside class="shoulder-sidenote">Translacon of y<sup>e</sup> Grand Sign<sup>rs</sup> Passe graunted to y<sup>e</sup> East India Merch<sup>ts</sup>.</aside></div><p class="prose-p">YEE<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-002" href="#sec-119-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup> that are my most laudable, fortunate, welthye and great Vizroies and <em>Beglerbyes</em>,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-003" href="#sec-119-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">3</a></sup> y<sup>t</sup> are on the way frō my most happie and Imperiall Throne, (both by sea and land) vntill the confynes and lymitte of the <em>East</em> <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-527" data-page="527" data-folio="435" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n526_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 435">[p. 435]</button> <em>Indyes</em>; Owners of some part of dignitye, and those vnto whome belongeth to giue ayd, helpe and succo<sup>r</sup> in gode cause and <em>Musulmanicall</em> religion, vppō their Empero<sup>rs</sup> beck: the wealth and greatnes of whome, let it contynue for euer. Likewise vnto y<sup>u</sup> my most laudable and valiant <em>Sangrack-beys</em>,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-004" href="#sec-119-fn-004" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-004" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup> (that are vnder the abouenam'd <em>Beglerbeys</em>) Owners, and hope, of future great<sup>r</sup> dignityes; and those vnto whome belongeth duityfull ayd and helpe in gode cause and religion, vppō their Empero<sup>rs</sup> aduise, the hono<sup>r</sup> and dignitye of whome, be eu<sup>r</sup> contynuing. And vnto yee my most laudable, wise and prudent Iustice of peace, and Iudge,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-005" href="#sec-119-fn-005" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-005" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup> (that are in the p<sup>r</sup>scincte of the said <em>Sangrack-beyes</em>) whose iudgmente, iustice, &amp; worde do flowe, as from a myne<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-007" href="#sec-119-fn-007" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-007" role="doc-noteref">3</a></sup> of all wisdome and prudence; the worthines and greatnes of whose dignitye and functiō, let it contynue for euer. Also to y<sup>u</sup> my laudable, great and most worthie Cap<sup>nes</sup>, and <em>Reyses</em><sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-006" href="#sec-119-fn-006" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-006" role="doc-noteref">4</a></sup> of all o<sup>r</sup> Nauyes, and shippinge, that swym̄e on the face of the sea. Vnto yee my laudable Cap<sup>nes</sup> of the Castles, Cityes and Townes; And vnto y<sup>u</sup> worthie and laudable <em>Customers</em>, dwelling vppō the <em>Sea</em>,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-008" href="#sec-119-fn-008" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-008" role="doc-noteref">5</a></sup> <em>Ryuers</em>, <em>Bridges</em>, and all oth<sup>r</sup> pte of o<sup>r</sup> Domynions ther adiacent, Vppō the sight of this my most high and Imperiall comaundm<sup>t</sup>, in conformitye of yo<sup>r</sup> most bounden duityes, yee shall arise and do obeysaunce therunto.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">And herby yee shall vnderstand that the <em>English Ambassad<sup>r</sup></em>, that here resydeth, in o<sup>r</sup> happye and most high <em>Port</em>, hath giuē vs to vnderstand, by his supplicatiō, That forasmuch, as some of his <em>Ma<sub>tie</sub></em> the <em>King of England</em> his subiecte, haue w<sup>th</sup> great charge and labo<sup>r</sup>, found out a Trade in the <em>East Indyes</em>; and hearing of the fame, and wealth<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-009" href="#sec-119-fn-009" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-009" role="doc-noteref">6</a></sup> of some parte of our Countrye, in their passage to the <em>Indyes</em>, are desirous to visite those place, for y<sup>e</sup> enlarging of the said Trade; and<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-010" href="#sec-119-fn-010" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-010" role="doc-noteref">7</a></sup> that such men in so good and profitable<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-011" href="#sec-119-fn-011" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-011" role="doc-noteref">8</a></sup> an enterprize, hauing deserued<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-119-fn-012" href="#sec-119-fn-012" data-fn-target="sec-119-fn-012" role="doc-noteref">9</a></sup> all fauo<sup>r</sup> ayd and <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-528" data-page="528" data-folio="436" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/registerofletter00eastuoft/page/n527_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 436">[p. 436]</button> helpe, so farre as may be lawfullye and conuenientlye yeilded, hath requested vs in the name of his <em>Ma<sup>tie</sup></em> the <em>King of England</em>, to vouchsaufe them o<sup>r</sup> saufe conduct &amp; com̃endac̃ons. In conformitye of whose request, as also in regard wee and o<sup>r</sup> p<sup>r</sup>decesso<sup>rs</sup>, are and haue bene for the space of many yeares, in strict league and amitye, w<sup>th</sup> the mēconed <em>King of England</em> and the Subiecte of that <em>Kingdome</em>, that haue long had, and now haue free traffique and trade in merchandizing in o<sup>r</sup> dominions and Countryes, thorough the <em>Mediterranean</em> seas. Wee do com̃aund and exp<sup>r</sup>slye charge y<sup>u</sup> all, and eu<sup>r</sup>ye one of y<sup>u</sup> our abouenamed subiecte &amp; officers, that y<sup>u</sup> will not onlye kindlye and louinglye entertayne and receyue the said merchantte subiecte of <em>England</em>, com̃yng or passing through, or by any of o<sup>r</sup> Dominions, esp̄iallye intending to trade to the Dominions of <em>Yemen</em>, <em>Aden</em>, and <em>Mochon</em>, and the pte adioyning therunto ; assisting and releyuing them w<sup>th</sup> all thinge nedefull for themselues, their men and their Shippes, but also freelye to pmitte them, by land or by Sea, to go or sayle, to go forward or retourne, euen as their occasion shall require, to remaine in any of o<sup>r</sup> Dominions, Countryes or Cityes, graunting them such libertye of Trafficke and priuiledge, as shalbe reasonable, w<sup>th</sup>out giuing, or suffring any let or hinderaunce, iniurye or molestac̃on to be offered or done vnto thē; yea, y<sup>u</sup> shall yeild vnto thē, such office of beneuolence and humanitye, as shalbe meete and conuenyent to be yeilded vnto honest men, and straung<sup>rs</sup>, vndertaking so long and laboryous a io<sup>r</sup>nye. And if it be so, that wee shall vnderstand that contrarye to the Capitulacõn the Amitye and the League w<sup>ch</sup> is betwene vs and the <em>King of England</em>, y<sup>u</sup> do offer them the least wrong, and any way molest and trouble the said m<sup>r</sup>chante in their traffique, or ought els, Knowe yee for certaine that y<sup>u</sup> shall not onlye incurre our high displeasure, but y<sup>u</sup> shalbe punished for example vnto oth<sup>rs</sup>: And therfore in conformitye of this my esp̄iall Imperiall com̃aundm<sup>t</sup>, y<sup>u</sup> shall gouerne yo<sup>r</sup> selues, and gyue creditte to this my Imperiall insigne.
