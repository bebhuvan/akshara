Of his early life little is known. He is said to have been a native of Basingstoke, and to have been at one time in Portugal as a soldier, and afterwards as a merchant. In 1591 he took part in Captain Raymond’s ill-fated expedition to the East Indies, of which he was one of the few survivors. He was next engaged in an enterprize to the coast of Brazil, where an attack was made on the Portuguese settlement of Pernambuco. He took an active part in the formation of the East India Company, and was one of the original directors, or ‘‘committees’’ as they were then called. Of the voyage now undertaken, as also of his two previous voyages, a full account is given in Mr. C. R. Markham’s <em>Voyages of Sir James Lancaster</em> (Hakluyt Society, 1877). On his return, Lancaster was knighted by King James; and thenceforward he appears as an active and trusted member of the Company, whose advice was constantly sought and listened to with the utmost deference. He died in June, 1618.</span></li><li id="sec-05-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-05-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">1601.</span></li></ol></section>
