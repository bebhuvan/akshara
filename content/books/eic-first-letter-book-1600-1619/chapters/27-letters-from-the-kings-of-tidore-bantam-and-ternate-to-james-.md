And I after the death of my ffather liued in the same hope till I was ffather to 11 Children, since w<sup>ch</sup> tyme we haue beene informed that English men were men of bad dispotition, &amp; came not as peaceable m<sup>r</sup>chaunte but as theeues. and robbers and to depose vs of o<sup>r</sup> Countries, w<sup>ch</sup> by the comeing of the bearer hereof yo<sup>r</sup> ma<sup>ties</sup> servant Capt: Henrie Middleton we haue found to the Contrary whereof we greatelie reioyce. And after many yeares of expectacõn of some English fforces by the pmyse of Capt: Drake heere arriued certeine shipps w<sup>ch</sup> we well hoped had beene English men, but they pued to be Hollenders, &amp; then being out of all hope of sucker of the english nation we were enforced to wryte to the Prince of Holland to craue aide and succo<sup>r</sup> of him agaynst o<sup>r</sup> auncient enymies the Portingalles. Wherefore we craue pardon of yo<sup>r</sup> ma<sup>tie</sup>. And whereas the Hollenders Admirall doth soliscite vs not to hold any ffreindshipp w<sup>th</sup> the englishe nor to giue eare nor Creditt to yo<sup>r</sup> ma<sup>te</sup> łres : yett neu<sup>r</sup>theless for all their sute yf yt please you to send hither agayne they shall be most freindlie wellcome, &amp; in token of ffreindshipp w<sup>ch</sup> we desire w<sup>th</sup> yo<sup>r</sup> ma<sup>tie</sup>. Wee haue sent you a small remembraunce of a Bahare of Cloues o<sup>r</sup> Countrie being poore &amp; yealding noe other Comoditie w<sup>ch</sup> we pray yo<sup>u</sup> to accept in good parte.
