Some idea of the exports to Europe during this period can be formed from the following abstracts of some invoices preserved in Holland, photographic transcripts of which have been supplied to me by the Public Record Office at The Hague. The values are given in guilders (⅖ rupee); I have eliminated fractional entries, and have rounded the quantities in converting to English pounds and yards.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1. Invoice of the <em>Black Bear</em> (<em>Swarte Beer</em>) from Masulipatam for Holland, most probably in 1615–16. (<em>Kol. Archief, portef.</em> O.)</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The invoice itself is not dated on the first page, but each of the inner pages is headed “<em>Anno 1616: Masulipattan</em>”; I take these headings to have been added in Amsterdam, and the year to be that of arrival. We know from the English records that another ship, the <em>White Bear</em>, was expected to sail for Holland in 1616 (<em>Letters Received</em>, iv. 34), and it is probable that the <em>Black The bulk of the cargo consisted of Malabar pepper (200,000 lb.), saltpetre (180,000 lb.), indigo (60,000 lb.), and yarn (55,000 lb.), together with a re-export of 35,000 lb. of pepper from Achin. Invoice of the <em>Schoonhoven</em>, from Masulipatam, October 1624. (<em>Kol. Arch.</em>, <em>portef.</em> DD<sup>i</sup>.)</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The invoice is dated August 1624, but a supplement was added in October. This vessel carried no pepper, but there were ordinary quantities of indigo, yarn, and saltpetre. The cotton goods consisted of about 100,000 yards of Guinea cloth, nearly 30,000 yards of moorees, about 3000 yards of percalles, and the same quantity of salempores (an ordinary calico).</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Invoices are also available of the direct shipments from Surat <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-343" data-page="343" data-folio="321" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/bwb_KV-449-199/page/n342_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 321">[p. 321]</button> to Holland in 1624 and 1625, almost, if not quite, the first Dutch efforts in this branch of trade.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1624. Early in this year two vessels, <em>Heusden</em> and <em>Vrede</em>, sailed for Holland; duplicate invoices of each have been preserved (<em>Kol. Arch. portef.</em> AA. DD<sup>i</sup>. EE), and dates are given in one copy of each. The cargoes consisted principally of indigo and saltpetre, with small lots of yarn, lac, borax, and other goods. The pieces of cotton goods entered are Guinea cloth (1520), mentasses (9240), baftas of sorts (2928), semianoes (200), and cassas (muslin) (120). The Guinea cloth was as usual in long pieces, each of 50 ells, or say 37 yards. I have found no description of mentasses, but from the detailed entries they must have been short, because 280 pieces went to the bale (against 120 baftas), while the price (about Rs. 8 per 20 pieces) shows that they were of very low quality. I suspect that they were bought for Africa and not for Europe. The baftas were of all colours and dimensions: some bales are marked as samples, others as “for Holland.” It appears safe to infer that the bulk of the cotton goods was intended for the African trade, while various trial consignments were included to test other markets.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1625. Invoices exist for two vessels, <em>Weesp</em> and <em>Dordrecht</em>. They are not dated, but a note in the <em>Dagh Register</em> of June 28, 1625, makes it clear that they relate to the voyage made by these vessels in April of that year, after they had taken part in the naval action off Gombroon (<em>English Factories</em>, iii. 47, 76). On both vessels the most valuable item of the cargo was Persian silk, the first-fruits of Dutch enterprise in that direction; indigo and saltpetre took most of the space. As regards cotton goods, the <em>Weesp</em> carried only a single bale of samples; the <em>Dordrecht</em> had 6000 pieces of mentasses, 1000 of Guinea cloth, 200 semianoes, and 800 cassas (described as “of Bengal”). If my conjecture regarding mentasses is correct, this shipment, like that of the previous year, may be described as intended mainly for Africa, with some samples for Europe.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-15-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-15-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">See Appendix D for these measures.</span></li></ol></section>
