<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-316"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="316" data-folio="294" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/indiawhatcanitte00mluoft/page/n315_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 294">[p. 294]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">NOTE K, p. 242.</h2><h3 class="sec-h2">ON <em>SRÂDDHAS</em>.</h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">In the Nirnaya-sindhu the <em>Srâddhas</em> are classified under twelve heads<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-20-fn-001" href="#sec-20-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-20-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">1</a></sup>:—</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><ul class="editorial-list"><li>1. Nitya-<em>srâddha</em>; perpetual, obligatory, daily offerings to ancestors, without the Vaisvadeva offerings<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-20-fn-002" href="#sec-20-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-20-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">2</a></sup>. A man who is unable to offer anything else may perform this <em>srâddha</em> with water.</li><li>2. Naimittika-<em>srâddha</em>; occasional, as, for instance, the ekoddish<em>t</em>a, i.e. the <em>srâddha</em> intended for a person lately deceased, and not yet incorporated with the Pit<em>r</em>is. This, too, is without the Vaisvadeva offering, and the number of Brâhma<em>n</em>as invited should be unequal.</li><li>3. Kâmya-<em>srâddha</em>; voluntary, or rather, offered for a special object.</li><li>4. Vriddhi-<em>srâddha</em>; offered on occasions of rejoicing or prosperity, such as the birth of a son, &amp;c.</li><li>5. Sapi<em>nd</em>ana-<em>srâddha</em>; performed when the recently departed is incorporated among the Pit<em>r</em>is. For this <em>srâddha</em> four pâtras or vessels are required, full of sesame and scented water for argha, and the vessel of the recently deceased person is poured into the vessels of the Pit<em>r</em>is, with the two verses ‘ye samânâ<em>h</em>.’ It is in one sense an ekoddish<em>t</em>a, and for the rest to be performed like the nitya-<em>srâddha</em>. It can be offered for a woman also<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-20-fn-003" href="#sec-20-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-20-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">3</a></sup>.</li></ul></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-317"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="317" data-folio="295" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/indiawhatcanitte00mluoft/page/n316_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 295">[p. 295]</button></div><div class="prose-unit"><ul class="editorial-list"><li>6. Pârva<em>n</em>a-<em>s</em>râddha; performed on a parvan day, i.e. new moon, the eighth day, the fourteenth day, and full moon.</li><li>7. Gosh<em>th</em>î-<em>s</em>râddha; performed in a gosh<em>th</em>î (house of assembly), for the benefit of a number of learned men.</li><li>8. <em>S</em>uddhi-<em>s</em>râddha; performed for the expiation of some sin, and including the feeding of Brâhma<em>n</em>as. It forms part of a prâya<em>sk</em>itta, or expiatory rite.</li><li>9. Karmâṅga-<em>s</em>râddha; forming part of some other ceremony, such as the Sa<em>ms</em>kâras or sacraments at birth, &amp;c.</li><li>10. Daiva-<em>s</em>râddha; offered for the sake of the Devas.</li><li>11. Yâtrâ-<em>s</em>râddha; performed by a person going on a journey, for his safe return.</li><li>12. Push<em>t</em>i-<em>s</em>râddha; performed for the sake of health and wealth; also called aupa<em>k</em>âyika.</li></ul></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The four principal <em>S</em>râddhas are the Pârva<em>n</em>a, Ekoddish<em>t</em>a, V<em>r</em>iddhi, and Sapi<em>nd</em>ana-<em>s</em>râddhas.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>S</em>râddhas may be performed in one’s own house, or in some secluded and pure place. There are besides certain localities which are considered particularly favourable to the performance of the ancestral rites, and these naturally vary during different periods of Indian history. In the Mahâbhârata the following are mentioned as particularly sacred: Kurukshetra, Gayâ, Gaṅgâ, Sarasvatî, Prabhâsa, Pushkara. In the Âditya-purâ<em>n</em>a Gayâkshetra is described as five krosas, Gayâ<em>s</em>iras as one krosa, west of the great river as far as the mountain G<em>r</em>idhre<em>s</em>vara, north of Brahmayûpa, as far as Dakshi<em>n</em>amânasa (?). Other localities are mentioned also as particularly unfavourable for the performance of <em>S</em>râddhas, and a careful study of these places, both favourable and unfavourable to the performance of <em>S</em>râddhas, would be very instructive as to the geographical horizon of successive generations.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The number of <em>S</em>râddhas to be performed each year by those who can afford it varies considerably, but ninety-six seems to be a generally received number. M. Bourguin, in his translation of the Dharmasindhu (Journal of the Royal <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-318" data-page="318" data-folio="296" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/indiawhatcanitte00mluoft/page/n317_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 296">[p. 296]</button> Asiatic Society of Bombay, 1881, p. 22), enumerates them as follows:—‘Twelve Amâ or new-moon rites; four Yuga and fourteen Manu rites (i.e. on the anniversary days of the beginnings of the fourteen Manvantaras and the four Yugas); twelve Krânti, corresponding to the twelve passages of the sun into the zodiacal mansions; twelve Dh<em>r</em>iti, performed on the day of the month the sun and the moon are on the same side of either solstice, but of opposite direction; twelve Pâta, performed on the day of the month the sun and the moon are on opposite sides of either solstice and their declination is the same; fifteen Mahâlaya, great funeral rites and sacrifices performed at the end of the Hindu lunar year in the month of Bhâdrapada (which is the last month of the year of the era of Vikramâditya, but not of <em>S</em>âlivâhana, showing that Vikramâditya’s era was once followed by all Hindus (?), as now even those who follow <em>S</em>âlivâhana’s era still perform those rites according to Vikramâditya’s calendar in the month of Bhâdrapada); five Ash<em>t</em>akas, performed on the eighth day of five months of the year; five Anvash<em>t</em>akas, performed on the ninth day of five months of the year; and five Pûrvedyu<em>h</em>, performed on the seventh day of five months of the year.’ This is summed up in the following verse:</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><div class="verse-stanza">अमामनुयुगक्रांतिधृतिपातमहालयाः ।<br>
अन्वष्टक्यं तु पूर्वेद्युः षष्णवत्यः प्रकीर्तिताः ॥</div></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">It should be remarked, however, as Colebrooke pointed out, that different authorities do not concur exactly in the number, or in the particular days, when the <em>S</em>râddhas should be solemnized.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp; Notes</h2><ol class="apparatus-list"><li id="sec-20-fn-001" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-20-fn-001" aria-label="Return to text">1.</a> <span class="fn-body">See Colebrooke, Life and Essays, vol. ii. p. 196; Wilson, Vish<em>n</em>u-purâ<em>n</em>a, p. 314.</span></li><li id="sec-20-fn-002" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-20-fn-002" aria-label="Return to text">2.</a> <span class="fn-body">Vish<em>n</em>u-purâ<em>n</em>a, p. 326.</span></li><li id="sec-20-fn-003" class="apparatus-item" role="doc-endnote"><a class="fn-backlink" href="#ref-sec-20-fn-003" aria-label="Return to text">3.</a> <span class="fn-body">See Yâ<em>gñ</em>avalkya I. 252–253.</span></li></ol></section>
