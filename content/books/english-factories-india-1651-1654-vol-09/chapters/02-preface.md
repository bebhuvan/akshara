<div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-9"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="9" data-folio="iii" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories09fost/page/n8_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page iii">[p. iii]</button></div><h2 class="sec-h1">PREFACE</h2><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><span class="sc">Apart</span> from the general history of the East India Company's settlements, the outstanding feature of the present instalment is the war between England and Holland in 1652-54, and its consequences in the East. Thanks to their naval power, the Dutch were able to sweep our shipping from the seas in those regions, thus causing the East India Company much material loss, and diminishing greatly the prestige of the English both in India and Persia. Other points of interest are the renewed struggle between the Portuguese and the Dutch; the mission of Lord Bellomont to Shāh Abbās of Persia on behalf of the exiled King Charles; and the efforts of Shāh Jahān's troops in 1652 and 1653 to recapture Kandahār from the Persians.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Including those dealt with in the notes, the documents here summarized number about two hundred and fifty. Of these all but two (viz. one from the Public Record Office and one from the British Museum) belong to the India Office archives, mainly to the <em>Original Correspondence</em> series. No Indian Record Office possesses any documents of this period.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The portrait of the Nawāb Mīr Jumla given as a frontispiece is one of the collection of Indian paintings prepared by order of Niccolao Manucci for the illustration of his <em>Storia do Mogor</em>. This collection, now in the Bibliothèque <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-10" data-page="10" data-folio="iv" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/englishfactories09fost/page/n9_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page iv">[p. iv]</button> Nationale, Paris (Cabinet des Estampes, O.D. no. 45, réserve), was utilized by the late Mr. William Irvine in his edition of Manucci's work (Indian Text Series, 1907–8), and amongst the rest Mīr Jumla's portrait was reproduced (vol. ii, p. 468). As, however, the Nawāb figures so prominently in the present volume and its predecessor, it has seemed appropriate to give a likeness of him here; and the editor's acknowledgements are due to M. Henri Omont, the head of the MSS. Department of the Bibliothèque Nationale, for his courteous assent to this utilization of the original.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The editor has also to thank Sir Charles Lyall, K.C.S.I., C.I.E., and Mr. Henry Dodwell, the Curator of the Madras Record Office, for assistance in regard to Indian names and terms occurring in the text and notes.</p></div>
