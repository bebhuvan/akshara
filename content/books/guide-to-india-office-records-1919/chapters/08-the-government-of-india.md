<span id="page-84" class="sec-page-anchor" aria-hidden="true"></span><h2 class="sec-h1"><span class="sc">government of india</span>.</h2><h4 class="sec-h3"><span class="sc">consultations</span>.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-001" href="#sec-08-fn-001" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-001" role="doc-noteref">*</a></sup></h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">As already noted under Bengal (p. 57), the India Act of 1833 (3 &amp; 4 Wm. IV. c. 85) vested the supreme control of the administration in a Governor-General of India and his Council, who were to be for the present also the Governor and Council of Bengal. The change was made on 16 June 1834, at which date Lord William Bentinck was staying at Utakamand, in the Nilgiris, while Sir Charles Metcalfe was carrying on the administration at Calcutta as Deputy-Governor. Separate "India" Proceedings were at once started in ten departments, viz., General,<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-002" href="#sec-08-fn-002" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-002" role="doc-noteref">†</a></sup> Judicial, Revenue, Separate Revenue, Secret, Political, Foreign, Ecclesiastical, Financial, and Military.<sup class="fn-ref"><a id="ref-sec-08-fn-003" href="#sec-08-fn-003" data-fn-target="sec-08-fn-003" role="doc-noteref">‡</a></sup> The joint secretariat arrangements, as regards most of the departments, have been described already at p. 58, to which may be added that <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-85" data-page="85" data-folio="65" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/guidetoindiaoffi00greauoft/page/n84_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 65">[p. 65]</button> the Government of India had its own Secretary for the Military Department.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The only record of the early legislative activities of the Government of India is to be found in the Civil Judicial Proceedings for 1834-5. From 6 July 1835, however, a separate series of Legislative Proceedings begins, which continues to 19 May 1854, and then comes to an end until 1861. The Judicial Proceedings are continuous from 1834 to 1858.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">A separate India Marine Department was formed in May 1838 under the orders from home, alluded to on p. 58. The corresponding Proceedings run from that date to the close of 1858.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The establishment in May 1843 of distinct secretariats for the Government of India and for Bengal has been recorded on p. 58. The former Government had already (January 1843) appointed a distinct secretary for its Financial Department, and it now resolved to amalgamate the Political and Foreign Departments under another secretary, and to group the remaining branches, viz., Revenue, Separate Revenue, General, Marine, Judicial, Legislative, and Ecclesiastical under a third secretary, in what was to be termed the Home Department. No change was made in the arrangements regarding the Military Department.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Strictly speaking, the proceedings in the subordinate branches of the Home Department should from this point be termed “Home (Revenue),” “Home (Ecclesiastical),” and so forth, but the distinction has not been made in the Press List. The Secret Proceedings continued to form a series apart from the Political and Foreign.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The assumption in the same year by the Governor-General of the direct administration of “The Saugor and Nerbudda Territories” (<em>see</em> p. 37) led to the institution of distinct series of “Judicial,” “Revenue,” and “Separate Revenue” Proceedings for business relating to those parts (including also Bundelkhand). Of these, the “Judicial” extend from 1843 to 1852 (the 1847 volume is missing); the “Revenue,” likewise from 1843 to 1852; and the “Separate Revenue,” from the same date to 1849. Particulars of these series are given in the <em>List of India Proceedings,</em> 1834–58. As already noted, in 1853 “The Saugor and Nerbudda Territories” were placed once more under the administration of the Government of the North-Western Provinces, while, in the following year, Bundelkhand became part of the new Central India Agency.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">India Public Works Proceedings were started in Feb. 1855 and ran on to the end of 1858. Questions relating to railways are dealt with in the same series.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">CORRESPONDENCE.</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">For the despatches exchanged between the Government of India and the Company, reference should be made to the sections <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-86" data-page="86" data-folio="66" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/guidetoindiaoffi00greauoft/page/n85_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 66">[p. 66]</button> commencing on p. 11. Correspondence in India itself must generally be sought in the Proceedings, though two volumes of correspondence between the Supreme Government and the Government of the Punjab will be found in the <em>Home Miscellaneous</em> series (<em>see</em> p. 30). There is, however, a special series of <em>Military Estates Correspondence</em>, in 29 vols., extending from 1853 to 1858, with indexes (Range 49, vols. 2–30). This contains copies of letters received or sent by the Military Department of the Government of India relating to the estates of deceased officers and men belonging to the Company's Bengal army.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2">ACCOUNTS.</h3><h4 class="sec-h3">GENERAL JOURNALS AND LEDGERS.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>See under</em> "Bengal" (p. 60).</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">DURBAR JOURNALS AND LEDGERS.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>See under</em> "Bengal" (p. 61).</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">TREASURY CASH ACCOUNTS.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p"><em>See under</em> "Bengal" (p. 61).</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">CIVIL PAYMASTER'S CASH ACCOUNT.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1856-57 (1 vol.).</p></div><h4 class="sec-h3">MILITARY JOURNALS AND LEDGERS.</h4><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">1852--59 (28 vols.).</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">For particulars, <em>see the Press List of Bengal Records</em>, p. 105.</p></div><section class="apparatus-zone" aria-label="Footnotes"><h2 class="apparatus-heading">Critical Apparatus &amp;
