called the book of the Śatarchins, that is of a hundred or a large indefinite number of authors of verses.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Of the hymns forty-four are specially addressed to Indra, forty-three to Agni, fifteen to the Aśvins, eleven to the Maruts, nine to the Viśvedevas, four each to Ushas and the Ṛibhus, three to Heaven and Earth, etc. Other hymns are addressed to gods conjointly, as Indra and Agni, Mitra and Varuṇa. Two hymns are addressed to the Horse, one is in praise of Food.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala II</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains only forty-three hymns. It is commonly called the Book of Gṛitsamada, as nearly all the hymns are ascribed to that Rishi.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">Fourteen of the hymns are addressed to Indra, two of them in the form of the Kapinjala, a kind of partridge, and nine to Agni.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala III</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains sixty-two hymns, ascribed to the rishi Viśvāmitra, or to members of his family. It is <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-280" data-page="280" data-folio="268" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/rigvedavedicreli00clayiala/page/n279_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 268">[p. 268]</button> said that he was born a Kshatriya, but by virtue of his intense austerities he raised himself to the Brahman caste.</p></div><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The rishis who wrote the hymns were not always friendly with one another. ‘Especially prominent,’ says Weber, ‘is the enmity between the families of Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra, which runs through all Vedic antiquity, continues to play an important part in the epic, and is kept up to the latest times; so that, for example, a commentator of the Veda who claims to be descended from Vasiṣṭha, leaves passages unexpounded in which the latter is stated to have had a curse imprecated upon him.’</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala IV</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains fifty-eight hymns. The first forty-one are ascribed to the rishi Vāmadeva, son of Gotama; so also are the last fourteen. Twelve are addressed specially to Indra, and eleven to Agni.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala V</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains eighty-seven hymns. Of these twenty-one are addressed to Agni, eleven to Mitra and Varuṇa, nine each to Indra, the Maruts and Viśvedevas, and six to the Aśvins.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala VI</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">The rishi of this book is Bharadvāja, to whom, with few exceptions, all the hymns are attributed. It contains seventy-five hymns. To Indra twenty-one hymns are <button type="button" class="folio-trigger folio-turn" id="page-281" data-page="281" data-folio="269" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/rigvedavedicreli00clayiala/page/n280_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect original scan for page 269">[p. 269]</button> addressed; to Agni, thirteen; to Pūshan, five; to the Viśvedevas, four.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala VII</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">All the hymns of this book are ascribed to the rishi Vasishṭha, with whom his sons are associated as the seers of parts of two hymns. There are 104 hymns; of which fourteen are addressed to Indra, thirteen to Agni, eight to the Aśvins, seven each to Ushas and the Viśvedevas, four to Varuṇa, and one to frogs. The prevailing metre is Trishtub.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala VIII</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book is by a variety of authors. It contains ninety-two hymns, with eleven called Vālakhilya or supplementary hymns. Of the hymns thirty-six are addressed to Indra, eleven to Agni, five to the Viśvedevas, and three to the Maruts.</p></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">Mandala IX</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains 114 hymns. Except one to the Āprīs and two in which Soma is invoked conjointly, all the hymns are addressed to Soma. Even in the hymn to the Āprīs the attributes of Agni are transferred to Soma. He is addressed as Pavamāna, representing the juice as it flows through the wool which was used to strain it and thus purify it. The hymns were intended to be sung while this process is going on. There are very many repetitions in the book.</p></div><div class="page-rail-marker" id="page-282"><button type="button" class="folio-trigger" data-page="282" data-folio="270" data-scan="https://archive.org/download/rigvedavedicreli00clayiala/page/n281_w1400.jpg" aria-label="Inspect scan for page 270">[p. 270]</button></div><h3 class="sec-h2"><span class="sc">MANDALA X</span></h3><div class="prose-unit"><p class="prose-p">This book contains 191 hymns. To Indra thirty-four are addressed, to Agni twenty-five, to the Viśvedevas twenty-two. There are hymns on creation and several to be used as charms to cure sickness, to remove rivals, to prevent miscarriage, etc. The book includes some of the latest hymns in the Rig-veda. Several of the hymns are ascribed to gods, as if the real authors wished by this device to conceal their late origin.</p></div>
