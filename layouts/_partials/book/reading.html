What a machine reading can get wrong

  • Letters and words. A word misread, a line ending’s hyphen kept or a word wrongly joined; accents and diacritics dropped; punctuation missing; figures confused, a 1 for an l, a 0 for an O.
  • Other languages and scripts. Words in Indian scripts, Urdu, Persian, Arabic, Greek or Latin are read less surely than English; an old romanisation of Sanskrit may be quietly turned into modern IAST, or an old spelling modernised.
  • The shape of the page. A paragraph broken or wrongly joined where a page turns; a running head or page number left in the text; a heading split, or set at the wrong level; pages in two columns read in the wrong order; stray marks and stamps in the front matter.
  • Notes, tables and pictures. A footnote parted from its mark; a table’s columns out of line or a figure misread; an illustration, map or plate with no marker where it stood.
  • Now and then, words that are not there. A model can supply a plausible word the page does not have. This has grown rare, but it happens.
