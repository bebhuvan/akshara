How a book is made

The method

From the camera’s photographs to a page that can be trusted. The hardest part of an old book is often its figures, so here is one of its tables, photographed and set.

From Romesh Chunder Dutt, The Economic History of India in the Victorian Age, 1904. Every table is given with its book as CSV, as printed and in tidy form.

  1. The photographs

    The PDFs on the Internet Archive are compressed, and compression can turn one digit into another. Akshara goes back to the camera’s own photographs of each page and rebuilds a clean copy before a word is read.

  2. The reading

    A vision model reads each page as it was printed: old spellings, accents and italics kept, footnotes followed across pages, shoulder notes kept beside the paragraph they belong to.

  3. The figures

    Every table in rupees, annas and pies, or in pounds, shillings and pence, is added up again by program, and each printed total is read a second time on its own.

  4. The proof

    Nothing read by machine is passed off as proofread. Each edition says how far it has been checked, and any reader can open a page beside its scan and set it right.
