India in the Victorian Age

Chapter I

Auckland and Ellenborough

Lord William Bentinck left India in 1835. His seven years’ rule was an era of Peace, Retrenchment, and Reform. He secured tranquillity in the East India Company’s dominions, and lived at peace with the Indian Powers. He reduced the public debt, decreased the annual expenditure, and showed a surplus. He commenced that revised settlement of land revenue in Northern India which gave relief to landlords and cultivators. He admitted the educated people of India to the higher appointments in the revenue and judicial departments. He abolished the practice of Sati and suppressed the crime of Thugs. He promoted English education in India, and endeavoured to carry out the maxim that the administration of India was primarily for the interests of the people. His successor, Sir Charles Metcalfe, trained in the traditions of his school, worked in the same lines, and followed the same principles. He gave liberty to the Press of India, and earned for himself a high reputation as an able and benevolent administrator.

How a book is made

Four steps, from the camera’s photographs to a page that can be trusted. The hardest part of an old book is often its figures, so here is one of its tables, photographed and set.

From page 384 of Romesh Chunder Dutt, The Economic History of India in the Victorian Age, 1904. The table is given with the book as CSV, as printed and in tidy form.

  1. 1

    The photographs

    The PDFs on the Internet Archive are compressed, and compression can turn one digit into another. Akshara goes back to the camera’s own photographs of each page and rebuilds a clean copy before a word is read.

  2. 2

    The reading

    A vision model reads each page as it was printed: old spellings, accents and italics kept, footnotes followed across pages, shoulder notes kept beside the paragraph they belong to.

  3. 3

    The figures

    Every table in rupees, annas and pies, or in pounds, shillings and pence, is added up again by program, and each printed total is read a second time on its own. The tables are given as data too.

  4. 4

    The proof

    Nothing read by machine is passed off as proofread. Each edition says how far it has been checked; where the machine was unsure the page is flagged, and any reader can open it beside its scan and set it right.

Proofread a page

The last step needs people. Open a book, choose one page, read it beside its scan, and correct what the machine misread. It takes about five minutes. When a second reader agrees, the correction goes into every edition of that book.
